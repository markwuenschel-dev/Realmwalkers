# Style Examples — Dominion Realm

> **Purpose:** Applied demonstrations of the voice, prose contract, dialogue rules, and system message formatting. Each example shows both a violation and a correct version where relevant. Use these as calibration when writing or editing.

> **Note:** All examples are illustrative drafts only — not final prose, not canon-locked scenes. Characters, situations, and details here are placeholders for demonstrating technique.

---

## 1. Interiority as Engine (Prose Contract Rule 1)

**Violation — skipping interiority to reach the action:**

> Marcus fought the creature for several minutes. It was fast and he had to work to keep up. Eventually he got an opening and took it. The creature fell. He walked away and found shelter before dark.

**Correct — interiority is the scene:**

> The thing moved like water finding a crack and he kept almost having it, kept almost reading the pattern, and then it would shift and he'd be wrong again and the cost of wrong here was getting smaller by increments he could feel.
>
> His shoulder. His left knee, now. Something in his side that wasn't pain yet but would be.
>
> *Stop trying to predict it. Watch what it's already doing.*
>
> He watched. Waited. Let it come.
>
> The opening arrived and he took it and it was over and he stood there afterward with his chest going and the wrongness of what he'd just done settling into him the way cold settles — not all at once but finding the places you'd forgotten to protect.

## 2. Emotion Through Behavior (Prose Contract Rule 2)

**Violation — naming the emotion directly:**

> Marcus felt grief when he saw the ruins of the settlement. It reminded him of home and of the people he might never see again.

**Correct — behavior and sensation carry the weight:**

> He stopped at the edge of what had been a market square. The stalls were still there, mostly — canvas rotted down to frames, a ceramic bowl someone had left on a table and nobody had come back for.
>
> He stood there longer than he meant to.
>
> Illyri said nothing, which was unusual enough that he noticed.
>
> He picked up the bowl. Set it back down. Kept moving.

---

## 3. Load-Bearing Details (Prose Contract Rule 3)

**Violation — decorative description:**

> Eriadne was a city of stone and old wood, its streets winding and narrow, its buildings pressing close together as if for warmth. The sky above was a deep grey and the cobblestones were slick with recent rain. Banners hung from iron brackets. People moved through the market district, bundled in heavy coats.

**Correct — every detail earns its place:**

> The city smelled like people and old stone and something underneath both that he couldn't name — something that sat at the back of the throat and made his eyes want to track things that weren't there.
>
> He catalogued: three exits from the market square, two he could reach before anyone stopped him. The vendor on the left was watching him in the way that meant either suspicion or recognition and he didn't know yet which was worse. A child was sitting on the steps of a building that had been something official once, eating something, watching him with the frank appraisal of someone who hadn't learned yet to pretend they weren't looking.
>
> He nodded at the kid. The kid didn't nod back.

## 4. Violence With Weight (Prose Contract Rule 4)

**Violation — violence that's clean and free:**

> Marcus cut down two of them before the third got close. He handled that one too. Three bodies on the ground. He wiped his blade and kept moving.

**Correct — the cost is present:**

> The first one was fast. The second one was faster and he almost didn't manage it and the almost had a sound — a specific sound, close and wrong — that he knew he would hear later in whatever passed for sleep here.
>
> The third one he didn't think about. That was the part he didn't want to examine, that in the end he'd just stopped thinking and the body had handled it, and somewhere in the gap between deciding and done he'd been absent in a way that frightened him more than any of them had.
>
> He stood there. His hands were steady. He wasn't sure what that meant about him.

## 5. The Realm Through Marcus's Perception (Prose Contract Rule 5)

**Violation — authorial exposition:**

> The ley lines beneath Eriadne had been established thousands of years ago by a civilization that no longer existed. They channeled the world's magical substrate toward a central convergence point beneath the city, which was why the ruins here were still partially active.

**Correct — experienced through Marcus:**

> Something was different about the ruins here.
>
> He'd been in three other sets of ruins since he'd arrived in this place and those had felt like ruins — dead weight, old stone, atmosphere and nothing else. This felt like he was standing on something that was still running. Like a building with the lights off but the hum of electricity still there if you put your hand on the wall.
>
> His eyes did something at the edges, the limbal warmth he hadn't learned to control yet, and he blinked it back.
>
> *Interesting,* Illyri said.
>
> "That's not helpful."
>
> *No,* she agreed. *It's interesting.*

## 6. UI Integration — Correct and Incorrect (System Message Rules)

**Violation — UI as pause button:**

> Marcus faced the creature. It raised its forelimbs.
>
> *[SYSTEM: New creature detected. Classification: Unknown. Threat level: High.]*
>
> He backed away slowly.

**Correct — UI as interruption inside the scene:**

> The thing raised its forelimbs and he was already reading it, already finding the weight distribution, when the interface fired across his vision like someone had switched a light on in a dark room.
>
> ```
> [ SYSTEM ]
> Classification: No match found.
> Biological structure: Partially assimilated.
> Threat model: Unavailable.
> ```
>
> No threat model. That was new. That meant the implant had nothing to compare it to, which meant whatever this thing had been before, it wasn't anymore.
>
> He revised his assumptions and hoped he had enough time to.

## 7. Illyri's Voice

**Wrong — too human, too explanatory:**

> "The ley lines here are very ancient," Illyri told him. "They were built by a civilization that existed long before the current inhabitants of this region. The convergence beneath Eriadne is particularly significant because it connects to multiple planar anchors."

**Correct — her specific register:**

> *The ground here remembers being somewhere else,* Illyri said.
>
> "That doesn't mean anything."
>
> *It means something. I'm not sure it means something you can use.*
>
> He waited to see if there was more. There wasn't.

## 8. Dialogue With Subtext

**Scene:** Marcus and Serra after surviving something together. Neither has addressed what happened. Both know what the other is thinking.

**Violation — characters say the thing:**

> "I missed you," Marcus admitted.
>
> "I missed you too," Serra said. "I thought about that night a lot. Before all of this."
>
> "Me too."

**Correct — the conversation is about something else:**

> "Your shoulder." She nodded at it.
>
> "It's fine."
>
> "It's not." She said it the way she said most things — flat, not unkind, already past the argument.
>
> He let her look at it. She checked the joint with efficient hands and he looked at the wall and thought about how she always knew when something wasn't fine and he'd always found that either useful or unbearable depending on the day.
>
> "You need to wrap it."
>
> "I know."
>
> She handed him the cloth from her pack. Their fingers overlapped briefly. Neither of them said anything about that either.
>
> "How long are you staying," he asked. Not a question the way he said it.
>
> She was quiet for a moment. "A few days."
>
> Which meant she'd already decided it wasn't longer.

## 9. Character Voice Differentiation in the Same Scene

**Same information, three different characters delivering it:**

**Marcus:**
> "The passage goes left and then — I think left, the interface gave me something earlier but it was partial, it might have been left — and there's a drop somewhere, Illyri mentioned a drop, I'm not sure what kind of drop we're talking about —"

**Serra:**
> "Left passage. Drop at the end. We go in pairs."

**Seb:**
> "Left, from what we have. There's a drop — could be a problem, could be an asset depending on what's below. I'd want to know before we commit." A pause. "But we should commit soon."

## 10. Scene Ending — Landing Without Summarizing

**Violation — summarizing:**

> As Marcus walked away from the ruins, he realized that everything had changed. He wasn't the same person he'd been when he arrived, and the Realm had made sure of that. Whatever came next, he would face it differently.

**Correct — landing:**

> He walked until the ruins were behind him and then a little further, until the feeling that they were watching him had faded enough to ignore.
>
> The sky here was the wrong color. He'd stopped noticing that somewhere in the last few days.
>
> He wasn't sure what it meant that he'd stopped noticing.

## 11. Choreography When the Action Carries the Meaning (Prose Contract Rule 1 — choreography mode; `prose_clarity_rules.md` R1–R4)

Most scenes lead with interiority and compress the physical action (see #1, #4). This is the opposite case: a tactical exchange where the choreography *is* the characterization, so the middle of the action cannot be deleted. The failure here is not too little interiority — it's referring to the action instead of showing it.

**Violation — the middle deleted, the result asserted:**

> She didn't bounce off the wall. She planted on it — used the half-second of his commit to put herself somewhere his block now made worse. He'd been on the other side of that exact decision before. He missed the next input.

The reader is handed four verdicts on actions they never saw: *planted on it* (how?), *his block made worse* (why?), *the other side of that decision* (what decision?), *missed the input* (which one, and what did it cost?). Every sentence is a conclusion with no event under it.

**Correct — every step staged, meaning after:**

> She came for Brent in a straight line — no feint, the read Seb had called. Marcus was already moving. Bastion was the slow form, the heavy one: it made him big and hard to move, good for standing in one place and refusing to leave it. He stepped into the gap between her and Brent and braced to eat the hit.
>
> Her burst broke against him and stopped there. None of it reached Brent. For half a second the plan was working.
>
> Then she didn't pull back. A blocked assassin resets — finds a softer angle. She drove in closer instead, right against the wall he'd made, and used the one flaw a wall has: it faces one way and turns slow. She slipped around his side, behind him — and now his own body stood between him and Brent. The block he'd raised to protect his support had become the thing she was hiding behind to kill it.
>
> Hold Bastion and she walks past him to Brent. Switch forms to chase her and the half-second the switch costs is the half-second Brent dies in. Both doors were bad. She'd won the exchange before he picked which way to lose it.
>
> He'd seen that refusal once before — the commit past the point a careful player bails, like the commitment was the whole plan. Once. Somewhere that had nothing to do with a game.
>
> He missed the input. One beat, the form-switch he'd already chosen just not happening, because the part of him that pressed the buttons had gone still to look at her.

What changed: the form's weakness is stated before it's exploited (faces one way, turns slow → *then* she goes around it); her counter is shown, not asserted (drives in, slips behind); the consequence is drawn (his own body now screens her kill); both bad options are spelled out so the trap is visible; the recognition anchors to one concrete observable (a refusal seen once) rather than a referred event. The interiority is still there — it just rides *after* the choreography, because here the choreography is the meaning.
