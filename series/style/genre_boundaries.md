# Genre Boundaries — Dominion Realm

> **Purpose:** Defines the tonal and genre lane this story operates in, and what crossing the boundaries looks like. Read before drafting any scene. Cross-reference `forbidden_drift.md` for specific patterns to avoid.

---

## What This Story Is

**Interface fantasy disguised as LitRPG.**

The Realm is real. The metaphysical laws are real. The UI is Marcus's implant translating those laws into data his gamer mind can process — not proof that reality is a game. The numbers, stats, notifications, and skill panels are interpretation, not truth.

This means the story operates in the intersection of:

- **LitRPG / progression fantasy** — a character grows in measurable ways, those measurements matter, the system has rules and costs
- **Portal fantasy** — real people transported into a real other world; the contrast between where they came from and where they are is load-bearing
- **Interface fantasy** — magic is a substrate accessed through different interfaces; the interface shapes what you can do and what you cannot; Marcus's interface is ocular and the implant translates it
- **Character-driven progression** — powers externalize psychology; growth is earned through reasoning and cost, not granted through power spikes

The emotional register is **sincere, grounded, and occasionally darkly funny**. The story takes its characters seriously without taking itself so seriously that humor is forbidden.

## The Four Walls

These are the genre walls. The story can press against them. It should not pass through them.

### Wall 1 — This is not a game

The Realm is not a server. Characters do not respawn without consequence. Death has identity cost. Pain is real. The people Marcus meets are not NPCs with behavior scripts. Eriadne is not a quest hub.

**Pressing against the wall:** Marcus catching himself thinking in game terms and having to correct. The UI rendering something in RPG logic that doesn't quite fit the metaphysical reality underneath. Ayla finding his interface-mediated understanding of the Realm subtly incomplete.

**Breaking through the wall:** Characters treating deaths as resets. Combat that feels like clearing content. The plot rewarding Marcus for playing optimally instead of for making human choices. The Realm having no stakes beyond progression.

### Wall 2 — This is not grimdark

Violence has weight and consequence. Bad things happen and they matter. The story does not pretend otherwise. But suffering is not the point. The prose does not aestheticize pain, wallow in hopelessness, or signal seriousness through body count.

**Pressing against the wall:** Real costs. Permanent consequences. Characters making hard calls that damage them. The aftermath of violence rendered honestly.

**Breaking through the wall:** Torture porn. Suffering described with relish. Characters who are defined only by their damage. Hopelessness without counterweight. The prose enjoying the dark for its own sake.

### Wall 3 — This is not parody or comedy

Marcus is funny. His humor is a survival mechanism and a social tool and a genuine part of who he is. The story can be funny. It is not a comedy. The humor exists inside the story's stakes, not above them.

**Pressing against the wall:** Marcus's playful chaos defusing real tension. Ayla's dry contempt producing genuine laughs. The absurdity of the Realm rendered with appropriate irony.

**Breaking through the wall:** Constant quipping that deflates stakes. Pop culture references used as substitutes for actual wit. Characters who exist to be comic relief and nothing else. Humor that signals the author doesn't take the story seriously.

### Wall 4 — This is not romance-primary

The Marcus/Serra dynamic is the emotional center of Book 1. It matters. It is not a romance novel. The relationship develops alongside everything else and is often the subtext rather than the text. It does not consume the story's other concerns.

**Pressing against the wall:** Charged scenes between Marcus and Serra where neither addresses the thing. Reunion scenes with emotional weight. The unaddressed Earth moment informing every interaction.

**Breaking through the wall:** Chapters that exist to service the romance arc at the expense of Marcus's individual arc. Serra's agency subordinated to the relationship. Resolution arriving too early or too cleanly.

## What the Story Sounds Like

| Quality | Present | Absent |
|---|---|---|
| Analytical | Marcus thinks in systems, tests assumptions, finds leverage points | Generic chosen-one instinct |
| Sincere | Characters want real things, fear real things, lose real things | Ironic detachment |
| Grounded | The Realm has physical reality; bodies matter; logistics matter | Hand-waving of consequence |
| Darkly funny | Humor inside the stakes, not above them | Constant comedy or zero comedy |
| Character-driven | Powers grow from psychology; choices reflect the person | Generic power fantasy |
| Mythic (lightly) | The Realm has depth, age, and strangeness | Either pure modernity or pure epic fantasy |

## Scene-Level Genre Checks

Before finishing any scene, ask:

1. Does this scene treat the Realm as real?
2. Does any character death or injury feel earned and consequential?
3. Is humor, if present, specific to the moment rather than generic quipping?
4. Is the progression beat (if present) earned through reasoning or cost?
5. Does Marcus feel like himself — analytical, morally serious, occasionally playful — rather than a generic protagonist?
6. Is Serra (if present) driving her own agenda rather than existing in relation to Marcus?

If any answer is no, the scene has crossed a genre wall and needs revision.
