# Prose Clarity Rules — Dominion Realm

> **Status:** Style canon. Binding on the drafting agent (Coder) and enforced by the reviewing agent (Critic).
> **Home:** `series/style/prose_clarity_rules.md`
> **Why this exists:** The recurring failure in drafts is *compression that deletes the middle of an action* — the prose hands the reader the conclusion of something that was never shown, and asks them to reconstruct frames that aren't on the page. They can't. Every rule below is a specific form of that failure, written so it can be caught mechanically.

---

## The One Test (hard gate — Critic runs this on every scene)

Two steps, in order.

**Step 1 — Mode.** Is this beat *choreography-load-bearing* (the physical events are what the scene is doing) or *interiority-load-bearing* (the interior cost is the point and choreography is rightly compressed — `prose_contract.md` R1)? Most scenes are mixed; judge per beat, not per scene.

**Step 2 — The gate.** For every *choreography-load-bearing* beat:

**Could a reader who knows nothing reconstruct the exchange beat-by-beat from the prose alone — what moved, where, and what resulted — without inferring a step that was never shown?**

This is a **hard gate.** Any load-bearing beat that is summarized, off-page, or unrenderable fails — **APPROVED is impossible until it is rendered, regardless of prose quality.**

A **load-bearing beat** is one the outcome turns on, or one whose removal makes a later beat unreconstructable. Trivial motion, and beats compressed *in service of interiority*, are out of scope — the gate forces rendering where the physical event carries meaning, never choreography for its own sake (that is its own drift; see the Note under `forbidden_drift.md` #11).

R1–R8 are the general failure modes. R9–R14 apply whenever Step 1 returns *choreography-load-bearing*.

---

## R1 — Show the event before its meaning

State what physically happens, in order, before any interpretation, metaphor, or significance. Meaning lands *after* the concrete event, never instead of it.

- **Broken:** "She planted on the wall and his block made it worse." *(The reader is handed the verdict on an action whose middle was deleted.)*
- **Fixed:** She drives in close instead of retreating, slips around his unguarded side, so his own body now stands between him and the ally he raised the wall to protect — *then* the meaning: the block became her screen.
- **Critic check:** For each action sentence, can the reader see what moved and where before being told what it means? If a sentence is a *verdict on an action not shown*, flag.

## R2 — Never refer to an event instead of staging it

Do not point at a thing that happens in space ("the other side of that decision," "she planted on the wall") as if the reader watched it. If it matters, stage it. If it's offstage, replace the reference with an observable the reader *has* seen.

- **Broken:** "He'd been on the other side of that exact decision before." *(Points at an event the reader was never shown.)*
- **Fixed:** Anchor to the thing the reader just watched — "the way she committed past the point a careful player bails." A refusal he saw once, concrete, nameable.
- **Critic check:** Does the phrase point at a specific event the reader hasn't been shown? Replace with a shown observable, or stage the event.

## R3 — A metaphor may color a shown action; it may never replace an unseen one

A metaphor adds meaning to something the reader can already picture. It cannot substitute for the literal thing when the literal thing hasn't appeared.

- **Broken:** A character calls the offer "a gun" before any coercion is concrete. The metaphor is doing the work the scene hasn't done → noise.
- **Fixed:** Show the literal thing first — the logout control is dead, "compatible whether or not anyone signs" — and *then* "it's a gun" lands, because the reader has seen the gun.
- **Critic check:** Is the literal thing the metaphor describes already on the page? If not, the metaphor is premature — cut it or move it after the literal beat.

## R4 — Define a mechanism before the beat that exploits it

If a moment turns on how something works — a power, a rule, a limitation — state that mechanism in plain terms *before* the moment it pays off. Never introduce and exploit a rule in the same breath.

- **Broken:** A form's weakness exists only implicitly, visible only because it gets defeated.
- **Fixed:** "Bastion faced one way and turned slow" is stated plainly; *then* she goes around it. The reader sees the trap close because they were told what the trap was.
- **Critic check:** Does this beat hinge on a rule/limit/power detail? Was that detail stated earlier in plain language? If introduced-and-exploited together, flag.

## R5 — Establish the normal before you break it

The impact of a rule being removed requires the rule to have been shown *working* first. Horror, betrayal, and reversal all depend on a baseline the reader watched operate.

- **Broken:** The game's safety rules stop protecting them — but the rules were never shown protecting them, so the removal carries no weight.
- **Fixed:** Logout, respawn, capped pain, safe zones are shown functioning as ordinary facts; *then* they go dead. The removal is the horror precisely because the reader saw the floor before it dropped.
- **Critic check:** Does any impact depend on a rule being violated or withdrawn? Was that rule shown operating earlier in the scene or a prior one?

## R6 — Anchor abstract significance to one plain observable

When a moment carries weight the reader can't yet explain — a person matters, a detail is ominous — anchor it to a single concrete, picturable thing. Do not carry significance on a cloud of evocative abstraction.

- **Broken:** "a density at the center flickering between resolutions" — atmosphere with nothing the reader can actually see.
- **Fixed:** One dropped input from a character who never drops inputs. The reader can hold that, and its weight comes from contrast, not adjectives.
- **Critic check:** Is the weight of this moment carried by abstraction/adjectives, or by one concrete observable? If abstraction, give it a single anchor.

## R7 — POV knowledge discipline (no leaked vocabulary or certainty)

The POV character may use only the words, concepts, and certainties they actually possess at this point in the story. Future-knowledge leaks break both immersion and canon.

- **Broken:** "the Realm" used on Earth, before the character has any such concept.
- **Fixed:** Marcus thinks in the terms he owns now — models, systems, the game. The later vocabulary arrives when he earns it.
- **Critic check:** Does the POV use a term, concept, or certainty they could not hold at this story moment? Flag as a leak. (Also a continuity check — cross-ref `canon_index.md` naming canon.)

## R8 — Proleptic restraint

Flash-forwards ("he would think about this later, in another world") spoil tension and, used repeatedly, make the narrator wink. At most one per scene, and only if earned.

- **Broken:** Three or more "later, when he had…" intrusions in one chapter; each one confirms he survives and dilutes the present stakes.
- **Fixed:** One, placed where the irony is strongest. The rest cut.
- **Critic check:** Count flash-forwards. More than one? Cut to the strongest, or justify explicitly in the divergence note.

## Choreography-load-bearing mode — additional checks (R9–R14)

These fire only when The One Test, Step 1, returns choreography-load-bearing. Each maps to a real miss the Critic approved.

## R9 (CC1) — Entrance before action
Every actor is rendered on-page — arriving, present, doing one specific physical thing — before they act on another character. An actor may not first appear mid-effect.
- **Broken:** "she's in" → the next time she exists on the page she's already a flicker mid-fight at the casters' edge. The most-anticipated arrival happens between paragraphs.
- **Fixed:** Serra loads in, takes her spawn, does one unmistakable thing that is *her* before a blow lands. The reader sees the benchmark, not just hears about it.
- **Critic check:** list every actor who affects the exchange. Each shown present and acting on-page before their first effect? An actor introduced *via* their effect = flag (R2 on a person).

## R10 (CC2) — No summarized load-bearing exchange
Render the beats; never hand the reader the exchange as a verdict.
- **Broken:** "trading hits with her," "the graze," "surviving exchanges he had no business surviving" — the duel delivered as outcome, middle deleted.
- **Fixed:** each trade is a shown action with a shown result.
- **Critic check:** any sentence that *reports* a sub-exchange instead of staging it? Flag. (This is the One Test made walkable.)

## R11 (CC3) — Commit and consequence both shown
Show the commitment — the specific line or overextension — and what it exposes or achieves. Never the commitment as assertion.
- **Broken:** "commits past the point a careful player bails" — told as reputation four times, shown as motion zero times.
- **Fixed:** the line she takes, what overextends, what's exposed when it does, how she recovers it (a dash, a reposition, a tempo cost).
- **Critic check:** for each commitment, are both the commit *and* its consequence on the page as physical events?

## R12 (CC4) — No free state-change
Every form, stance, or position shift is (a) **shown** — what it looks like — and (b) **costs** something — a beat, a window of vulnerability, a tax.
- **Broken:** Sentinel→Predator→Arbiter→Oracle rotate with no rendered look and one abstract "half-second loss." The class's defining mechanic, free and invisible.
- **Fixed:** the avatar's change is visible (silhouette, stance, speed); the switch opens a rendered window — for that window neither form's kit is live, abilities grayed mid-rotation, cooldowns still spinning, no shield up. Cost shown in-game, felt not named.
- **Critic check:** list every state-change. Each shown? Each costed? Invisible or free = flag. (If rotating is free, `mc.md`'s "cognitive load prohibitive for almost everyone" is unearned.)
- *[Rule: inside VR the player does not feel the capture chair — a form/state-change cost renders only as in-game mechanics (cooldown, transition window, ability lockout, exposure), never as sensation of the rig. The chair is felt only when immersion itself breaks.]*

## R13 (CC5) — Capability established before it decides *(R4, in combat)*
The move that wins an exchange — a form, a read, a limit it exploits — is shown before the beat it turns.
- **Broken:** a winning rotation or read appears for the first time at the moment it wins.
- **Fixed:** her opener rhythm is established ("the rhythm he could have hummed") *before* he breaks it by going off-tempo. (The scene does this one right — keep it.)
- **Critic check:** does the deciding move rely on a capability or limit shown earlier? Introduced-and-decisive in one beat = flag.

## R14 (CC6) — Abstraction anchored to the move *(R6, in combat)*
Any verdict word — "overperformed," "reset," "dominated," "outplayed," "no business surviving" — attaches to the specific physical action that earns it.
- **Broken:** "He shouldn't have survived that exchange. He survived it." — a verdict with no rendered mechanic under it. (Also the Pole-Overshoot tell: you reach for the verdict *because* the mechanic isn't on the page.)
- **Fixed:** show *how* the off-tempo rotation makes her miss; the verdict becomes unnecessary — the reader watches him be that good.
- **Critic check:** each evaluative claim about the fight — anchored to a shown action? Unanchored verdict = flag, and confirm it isn't substituting for deleted choreography.

## How the agents use this

**Coder (drafting):** Read this file before drafting any scene. After a draft, self-run The One Test (top of file) and R1–R8 before handing to the Critic. Drafting in scene-and-sequel beats helps: render the *physical sequence* of an action first, add interpretation second.

**Critic (reviewing):** Run The One Test on every scene as a hard gate. Then walk R1–R8 in order. Any flag → the scene is **CHANGES REQUESTED**, not APPROVED, with the specific failing sentence quoted and the rule number cited. A draft that fails The One Test cannot be marked APPROVED regardless of other merits.

**Wiring:** Add `prose_clarity_rules.md` to the Coder's required-reads and the Critic's review checklist (alongside `system_message_rules.md`). List it under the **style** tier in `canon_index.md`'s source-of-truth map. It governs *prose execution*, not canon facts — so it ranks below the canon docs on questions of *what is true*, but it is binding on *how any scene is written*.

**Critic — combat procedure (mandatory in choreography-load-bearing mode):** produce a **numbered beat-by-beat reconstruction** of the exchange from the prose alone, and explicitly list any load-bearing beat you **cannot** reconstruct (summarized, off-page, unrendered). Any unreconstructable load-bearing beat → CHANGES REQUESTED, beat cited. You may not APPROVE a choreography-load-bearing scene without attaching the reconstruction. This forces tracing the fight instead of vibe-passing it — the single step that catches skipped entrances, summarized exchanges, and free form-switches.

**Coder:** before handing off a choreography-load-bearing scene, run that same reconstruction on your own draft and fix any beat you can't trace.
