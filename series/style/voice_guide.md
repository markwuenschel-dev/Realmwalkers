# Voice Guide — Dominion Realm

> **Purpose:** Defines the narrative voice, POV structure, and tonal rules. Grounded in the author's actual writing from the original draft. Cross-reference `prose_contract.md` for specific rules, `dialogue_rules.md` for spoken voice.

## Where This Voice Comes From

The clearest version of this story's voice exists in three sequences from the original draft:
- The paladin vs. blood mage scene (first real Realm encounter)
- The mindscape sequence (archdemon possession attempt, bargaining, death)
- The Illyristranthe introduction (resurrection, the astral presence, first conversation)

These sections share specific qualities that define the voice. Everything in this document is derived from them.

## What the Voice Actually Does

### It earns humor through restraint

The strongest humor in the original draft arrives quietly. "No idiot, an inanimate machine is not spontaneously talking to you." The chin-raise while being choked. The smug offer of a deal to a demon that was torturing him for what felt like hours.

None of these are announced. They land because the writing doesn't signal them in advance.

**The rule:** humor arrives sideways. The funny thing is observational, specific to the moment, and never separated from the situation by distance. The narrator is not winking at the reader. He is finding the absurdity while still inside it.

**What to avoid:** jokes that interrupt the scene to be jokes. Pop culture references stacked without payoff. Quippiness as a substitute for actual wit. The original Chapter 1 has this problem — Tony Stark, Neo, Dorothy, Gandalf in rapid succession. None of them land because none of them earn anything. The mindscape has no references and is genuinely funny.

### It trusts specific detail over general description

The archdemon torture sequence works because it names specific places. Gums. Eardrums. Bones snapping in several places. The detail that all of it feels like paper cuts — mundane pain made relentless — is unexpected and exactly right.

Compare to the early Earth chapters, which describe the Astria facility in general terms: "breathtaking landscaping," "clean and well-organized." Nothing sticks. No detail does work.

**The rule:** one specific detail beats three general ones. The detail should be the one Marcus would actually notice — because of what it reminds him of, because it's wrong, because his eyes catch on it. Not because it fills space.

### It lets logic do the work

The mindscape climax works because Marcus solves it through reasoning, not sudden power. He notices he controls the space. He reasons that he can hold the archdemon there. He trades a bluff into a bargain. The smug chin-raise is earned because we watched him think his way to it.

This is the protagonist's core competency: systems intelligence. He finds the leverage point. His victories should feel earned through reasoning, not granted through power spikes.

**The rule:** when Marcus wins, show the thought process that got him there. The solution should feel inevitable in hindsight and surprising in the moment.

### It handles the strange matter-of-factly

Illyristranthe's introduction. A knot of old light — edges that don't resolve, not quite warm — drifts a few feet from his head. The prose doesn't linger on how weird this is. It just describes it and moves. "It didn't have eyes or even a face." Then: "What do you want?"

The Realm's strangeness lands harder when the narrative doesn't perform amazement at it.

**The rule:** describe strange things the way you'd describe ordinary things. No exclamation. No dwelling. The reader's imagination fills in the wonder; the prose provides the specific image and gets out of the way.

### Illyristranthe / Illyri has a specific voice that must be preserved

Her voice in the original is already sharp and distinct. Key qualities:
- Dry contempt that isn't cruel — "No idiot" not "you fool"
- Interrupts when something matters to her: "Never say your true name"
- Ancient but not formal — she uses contractions, she's blunt, she doesn't speechify
- Her pauses carry meaning — "There was silence. Several seconds passed."
- When she's amused she doesn't signal it. "I am a celestial being." Said simply. Slightly deadpan.
- She says less than she knows. Always.

Do not let Illyri become an exposition machine. The moment she starts explaining the world in paragraphs, she stops being Illyri.

## Author Influences (Background Reference)

These are lenses, not prescriptions. The actual voice comes from the writing above.

**Stephen King** — Marcus's interiority. Thoughts that spiral and catch on things. Dread that accumulates through specifics, not announcements. Humor as survival.

**Margaret Atwood** — Combat and violence. Precise, clinical, consequential. The body is observed. Nothing is gratuitous.

**Neil Gaiman** — The Realm's strangeness. Matter-of-fact about the impossible. Old things feel old. Names carry weight.

## Tonal Register by Zone

| Zone / Context | Feel |
|---|---|
| Marcus's interiority | Spiraling, specific, afraid and finding the angle |
| Combat / violence | Precise, clinical, body-level detail, aftermath matters |
| Eriadne's streets | Grounded and strange; people feel real, the city feels old |
| Ancient ruins / ley lines | Matter-of-fact wonder; describe it and move |
| Serra's POV | Faster, tighter, less spiral — she's already decided |
| Xyloryn encounters | Clinical horror; the body is data; dread accumulates |
| N'hal presence | Reality slightly wrong; the language itself should feel unstable |
| Illyri's voice | Dry, old, complete thoughts, pauses with meaning |
| Earth flashbacks | Specific and ordinary; the contrast with the Realm is the point |

## POV Structure

### Primary POV — Marcus
The default lens. When in doubt, stay here.

- The narrator does not know what Marcus does not know
- The narrator does not correct Marcus's wrong reads — the reader feels the wrongness through texture
- His emotional state shapes what he notices and how he describes it
- When he goes into operator mode (dissociation under pressure), the prose flattens deliberately — less texture, more task

### Secondary POV — Other Characters
Occasional shifts permitted. Rules:

- POV shifts at chapter or section breaks only, never mid-scene
- Serra's POV: tighter, faster, less interior spiral — she processes privately and speaks the output
- Seb's POV: starts feeling like Marcus's, gradually replaces warmth with justification
- Every secondary POV scene must show something the primary lens cannot — not just what's happening elsewhere

### What the Narrator Never Does
- Explains feeling when behavior can carry it
- Corrects Marcus's misreads from outside his perspective
- Rushes past consequences
- Makes violence feel clean or rewarding

## Rhythm and Sentence Structure

**High interiority / low stakes:** Longer, looser. Thoughts that interrupt themselves and don't quite finish.

**Rising pressure:** Sentences shorten. Subordinate clauses drop. The rhythm tightens before the action does.

**Combat / crisis:** Short. Declarative. Fragments. The body doesn't have time for syntax.

**Aftermath:** Long sentences that go somewhere unexpected then stop short. The mind trying to reassemble.

**Illyri:** Unusual rhythm. Complete thoughts that don't follow conversational cadence. Pauses that mean something.

---

## What This Voice Avoids

- Stacked pop culture references without payoff
- Announcing humor in advance of it landing
- General description where a specific detail would do work
- Characters delivering exposition they'd already know
- Victories through power spikes rather than earned reasoning
- Illyri explaining too much
- Making the strange feel strange by dwelling on its strangeness
