# Mechanics & Tiers — Dominion Realm

> **Status:** Canon · working draft
> **Owns:** the tier ladders — Spell Strength, Item Quality, Item Rarity, Gemstone Quality, Spell Skill Mastery, Soul Level. Class rarity → `core_rules.md` / `classes.md`. Domains → `classes.md` now and `power_expression.md` when expanded. Calendar → `realm_calendar.md`. **Resource formulas, regen/depletion, the Soul→Reserve multiplier, XP curve, threat readout, and stat templates → `resource_system.md`.**

## Read-this-first notes

- **These ladders are independent.** "Divine," "Mythic," "Legendary," "Exceptional," "Rare," "Common" recur across several ladders — a Divine *soul* ≠ a Divine *item* ≠ a Divine *spell-skill tier*. Don't conflate them.
- **Soul Level is load-bearing for the story.** It governs resistance to being altered, erased, bound, possessed, or overwritten — the engine under **true-names / namebinding** (Serra's arc; **any soul can be bound in principle — Soul Level sets the difficulty and cost, not a yes/no.** Low and average souls are cheap and routine — what the faction preys on; high souls are prohibitively costly; the top tiers (Brilliant → Absolute) approach but never reach true immunity), **death & resurrection** (Marcus), **the broker holding Seb's brother's soul**, and **vow enforcement** (Xazzidiuk/Zazriel). Cross-ref from `core_rules.md` (Naming) and `relationship_map.md`.
- **Spell Skill Mastery composes with Skill Affinity, not against it.** Skill Affinity = the *chance* a skill levels (Marcus's Unbound Affinity inverts this); Spell Skill Mastery = what the skill's *level* is worth (the tier bonus). Domains are separate and live in `classes.md` / `power_expression.md`. See `core_rules.md` → Skills and Affinities.
- **The Eyes of Meszkhal is a _Unique_ item** (Item Rarity, below). Its copycat / +100%-skill-XP is the *item's* power, billed in mana; the Neurochromatic interface is separate and bills in the body.

# **Spell Strength:**

| Spell Strength | Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Feeble** | Barely manifests. The spell functions, but its effect is weak, unstable, or easily resisted. Often seen in beginners, damaged casters, or severely underpowered castings. |
| **Weak** | A functional but unimpressive casting. Suitable for practice and minor tasks but rarely decisive in serious situations. |
| **Minor** | A reliable low-level manifestation. Useful in everyday applications and capable of affecting untrained or vulnerable targets. |
| **Normal** | The expected baseline. Most competent practitioners produce Normal-strength spells consistently. This serves as the benchmark for comparison. |
| **Enhanced** | Noticeably stronger, more efficient, or more refined than a standard casting. Demonstrates above-average control or power. |
| **Potent** | A powerful casting capable of significantly influencing combat, crafting, healing, or other magical applications. Difficult for novices to replicate. |
| **Strong** | A highly dangerous or impressive manifestation. Possesses enough force, resilience, or effectiveness to threaten trained opponents or overcome substantial resistance. |
| **Mighty** | An exceptional casting that clearly exceeds what most practitioners can achieve. Often remembered by witnesses and respected by peers. |
| **Grand** | A master-level manifestation. Represents a degree of magical expression rarely encountered outside accomplished specialists and veteran practitioners. |
| **Supreme** | Near-perfect execution of the spell. The caster extracts almost the full potential of the magical technique, producing effects that few can equal. |
| **Sovereign** | The highest known expression of a spell. The casting displays overwhelming authority, control, efficiency, and permanence, embodying the spell's ideal form. Sovereign manifestations are the subject of legends, research, and historical records. |

### **Design Philosophy**

A **Spell Strength** rating does not necessarily mean a spell becomes larger or more destructive.

Instead, higher strength reflects a more complete expression of the spell's potential. Depending on the spell, this may increase:

* Power  
* Efficiency  
* Precision  
* Duration  
* Range  
* Stability  
* Resistance to dispelling  
* Magical permanence  
* Degree of control

For example:

* A **Sovereign Fireball** may burn hotter, travel farther, and leave lingering magical flames.  
* A **Sovereign Healing Spell** may restore wounds with little scarring and resist later complications.  
* A **Sovereign Barrier** may endure far beyond its expected duration and resist magical disruption.  
* A **Sovereign Illusion** may become nearly indistinguishable from reality.

Thus, Spell Strength measures not merely how much power is poured into a spell, but how completely and effectively the spell's underlying concept is realized.

# **Item Quality**

Item Quality measures **how well an item was created**, regardless of what it is. A sword, suit of armor, potion, book, ring, tool, or wagon can all possess a Quality rating.

Quality reflects the skill of the creator, the care taken during construction, the precision of execution, and the overall refinement of the finished product. It does **not** measure rarity, magical power, material quality, or historical significance.

A higher-quality item performs closer to its theoretical ideal, suffers fewer flaws, lasts longer, and generally provides greater reliability and effectiveness.

| Quality | Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Shoddy** | Poorly made and riddled with flaws. Often unreliable, fragile, or inefficient. Such items are frequently produced by inexperienced craftsmen, rushed work, or damaged production methods. |
| **Poor** | Functional but clearly substandard. Noticeable defects reduce performance, durability, or reliability. Common among cheap mass-produced goods. |
| **Below Average** | Adequate for basic use but exhibits visible imperfections and inefficiencies. Suitable for everyday tasks but unlikely to impress. |
| **Average** | The standard level of craftsmanship expected from a competent professional. Serves as the baseline against which most items are measured. |
| **Above Average** | Better than typical workmanship. Demonstrates additional care, attention to detail, and improved performance over standard items. |
| **Well Crafted** | Produced by a skilled artisan with a strong understanding of their craft. Reliable, durable, and noticeably superior to common goods. |
| **Superior** | Excellent craftsmanship with few meaningful flaws. Often sought after by professionals who rely on their equipment regularly. |
| **Exceptional** | Rarely achieved quality. The creator's skill is evident in nearly every aspect of the item's construction. Highly valued and difficult to replicate consistently. |
| **Masterwork** | A masterpiece of mortal craftsmanship. Constructed with extraordinary precision, refinement, and expertise. Such items often become benchmarks against which lesser works are judged. |
| **Ascendant** | The pinnacle of known craftsmanship. The item approaches the ideal expression of its design, exhibiting a level of perfection that borders on the supernatural. Ascendant items are exceptionally rare and often become treasured heirlooms, legendary creations, or historical treasures. |

---

### **Design Philosophy**

**Quality measures execution, not power.**

A higher-quality item is not necessarily more magical, more valuable, or made from rarer materials. Instead, it represents how closely the creator approached the ideal version of that item.

For example:

* An **Ascendant Iron Sword** may still be made of ordinary iron, but it represents near-perfect swordsmithing.  
* A **Shoddy Dragonbone Sword** may be made from an incredibly rare material, yet suffer from poor balance, weak construction, or improper forging.  
* An **Exceptional Healing Potion** is carefully prepared, stable, and highly efficient, while a **Poor Healing Potion** may contain impurities or produce inconsistent results.  
* A **Masterwork Tome** may contain flawless transcription, organization, and preservation, even if the knowledge itself is common.

Quality is therefore independent of:

* **Rarity** (How uncommon an item is)  
* **Material Quality** (What it is made from)  
* **Magical Strength** (How powerful its effects are)  
* **Historical Significance** (Whether it is a relic or artifact)

An item's overall value is often determined by the combination of all these factors rather than its Quality alone.

# **Item Rarity**

Item Rarity measures **how uncommon, difficult to obtain, and exceptional an item is within the world.**

Unlike Quality, which measures craftsmanship, Rarity reflects factors such as:

* Availability  
* Difficulty of acquisition  
* Material scarcity  
* Knowledge required to create it  
* Historical survival rates  
* Magical significance  
* World-level uniqueness

A poorly made item can still be extraordinarily rare, and a perfectly crafted item can still be relatively common.

Rarity is not a measure of craftsmanship, power, or quality, though rare items often possess some combination of those traits.

| Rarity | Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Shoddy** | Commonly discarded, damaged, or otherwise undesirable items. Often considered beneath normal trade standards. |
| **Common** | Widely available and easily acquired. Most everyday goods fall into this category. |
| **Uncommon** | Seen regularly but not possessed by the average person. Requires some effort or resources to obtain. |
| **Unusual** | Distinctly less common than everyday items. May possess uncommon materials, craftsmanship, or features. |
| **Scarce** | Difficult to find through normal commerce. Ownership often implies specialized connections or significant effort. |
| **Limited** | Produced or available only in restricted quantities. Acquisition may require travel, influence, or favorable circumstances. |
| **Rare** | Seldom encountered. Most individuals will see few truly Rare items during their lifetime. |
| **Exceptional** | Highly uncommon and often associated with notable individuals, organizations, or accomplishments. |
| **Epic** | Remarkable items whose existence is widely recognized among knowledgeable communities. Often the subject of stories and admiration. |
| **Mythic** | Extremely rare items surrounded by rumor, speculation, or incomplete records. Many are believed lost or unattainable. |
| **Legendary** | Famous items known far beyond their owners. Their names, histories, or deeds are often preserved in songs, records, and folklore. |
| **Relic** | Historical treasures that have survived from significant individuals, civilizations, or ages. Their value often exceeds their practical utility. |
| **Artifact** | Extraordinary items possessing unique magical, metaphysical, or otherwise exceptional properties that distinguish them from ordinary creations. |
| **Unique** | One-of-a-kind items with no known duplicates. Their loss would permanently remove them from existence. |
| **Divine** | Items whose creation, existence, or power is directly tied to divine forces, cosmic authorities, or foundational aspects of reality. Divine items are among the rarest and most significant objects known to exist. |

---

### **Design Philosophy**

**Rarity measures significance and scarcity, not craftsmanship.**

A higher rarity does not necessarily mean an item is better made.

For example:

* A **Common Ascendant Sword** may be an extraordinary example of a standard military weapon pattern.  
* A **Divine Poor-Quality Relic** may be ancient, damaged, and nearly unusable, yet remain priceless due to its origin.  
* An **Epic Potion** may require ingredients that only appear once every century.  
* A **Legendary Book** may contain knowledge unavailable anywhere else in the world.

Rarity often emerges from a combination of factors:

* Scarcity of materials  
* Difficulty of creation  
* Historical importance  
* Magical significance  
* Survival over time  
* World-level uniqueness

As a result, an item's overall value is often determined by multiple independent traits:

1. Ascendant Quality  
2. Legendary Rarity  
3. Artifact Tag  
4. Superior Materials

   

Each describes a different aspect of the item.

---

### **Example Interpretations**

| Example | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Masterwork Common Sword** | Excellent craftsmanship applied to an otherwise ordinary design. |
| **Average Rare Sword** | Uncommon item with mediocre execution. |
| **Ascendant Epic Potion** | Exceptionally crafted potion using ingredients or methods rarely seen. |
| **Poor Legendary Relic** | Historically important but damaged or degraded. |
| **Masterwork Divine Artifact** | One of the most extraordinary objects known to exist. |

One thing I'd still consider long-term is whether **Relic**, **Artifact**, and **Unique** are better as rarity tiers or as tags. They're the three entries that describe *what an item is* rather than simply *how rare it is*. For now, however, the ladder remains understandable and gives you a smooth progression from everyday goods to world-defining treasures.

# **Gemstone Quality**

Gemstone Quality measures a gemstone's **magical resonance, purity, and ability to contain, channel, and preserve magical energy**.

Higher-quality gemstones possess greater mana conductivity, stability, and affinity for enchantments, spell storage, ritual use, and magical crafting. While high-quality gemstones are often more beautiful and valuable, Gemstone Quality is not a direct measure of market price, rarity, or size.

| Quality | Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Dull** | Barely capable of holding magical energy. Most mages consider Dull gemstones unsuitable for serious magical use. |
| **Plain** | Possesses minor magical utility but suffers from weak resonance and poor efficiency. |
| **Modest** | Functional for basic magical applications and low-level enchantments. |
| **Eye-Catching** | Slightly above average resonance with improved stability and mana retention. |
| **Sparkling** | Noticeably efficient at channeling magical energy and suitable for most common magical uses. |
| **Vibrant** | Strong resonance and dependable mana retention. Favored by skilled craftsmen and enchanters. |
| **Glowing** | High-quality gemstone capable of supporting advanced magical applications. |
| **Brilliant** | Exceptional magical conductivity and purity. Highly sought after by serious practitioners. |
| **Exquisite** | Rarely encountered quality with remarkable stability and efficiency. |
| **Prismatic** | Displays unusual magical harmonics and can often support multiple magical energies simultaneously. |
| **Radiant** | Extraordinary purity and mana capacity. Considered a treasure by most magical organizations. |
| **Heavenly** | Among the finest naturally occurring gemstones known. Possesses exceptional magical potential and stability. |
| **Flawless** | Near-perfect resonance with no detectable imperfections or inefficiencies. |
| **True** | Represents the ideal expression of the gemstone's nature. Its magical properties fully embody the essence of the material from which it formed. |
| **Mythical** | Exceeds known natural limitations. Such gemstones are the subjects of legends, scholarly debate, and intense competition among powerful factions. |

---

### **Design Philosophy**

**Gemstone Quality measures magical resonance, not value.**

A gemstone's quality reflects how effectively it can interact with magical energy. Higher-quality gemstones are generally more desirable because they store mana more efficiently, channel magical power more cleanly, and maintain greater stability during enchantment, spellcasting, and ritual work.

Gemstone Quality is independent of:

* **Rarity** (How uncommon the gemstone is)  
* **Size** (How large the gemstone is)  
* **Market Value** (What buyers are willing to pay)  
* **Historical Significance** (Whether it is a relic or artifact)

For example:

* A **Mythical Quartz** may possess extraordinary magical resonance despite being a relatively common gemstone type.  
* A **Modest Diamond** may be physically beautiful and valuable, yet offer only average magical utility.  
* A **Radiant Mana Crystal** may outperform a larger but lower-quality crystal in magical applications.  
* A **True Ruby** is not merely flawless—it represents the purest known manifestation of what a ruby can become.

In general, higher-quality gemstones allow magic to flow with greater efficiency, stability, and fidelity, making them invaluable to enchanters, artificers, ritualists, and spellcasters throughout the Realm.

# **Spell Skill Mastery**

**Spell Skill Mastery represents a practitioner's understanding and mastery of a specific magical discipline.**

As Spell Skill increases, practitioners gain greater control, efficiency, resistance, and effectiveness when interacting with that school of magic. Spell Skill is independent of Character Level, Class, Affinity, and Soul Level.

Higher Spell Skill provides:

* Increased Spell Strength when casting spells from that school.  
* Increased Resistance against magical effects from that school.  
* Improved control, efficiency, and understanding.  
* Access to advanced techniques and training opportunities.

For example, a Master of Fire Magic naturally casts stronger fire spells and is significantly more resistant to hostile fire magic than a novice practitioner.

| Tier | Skill Level | Levels | Spell Strength & Resistance Bonus | Description |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| I | **Novice** | 1-9 | \+0% | Basic exposure to the discipline. Can perform only the simplest magical techniques. |
| II | **Beginner** | 10-19 | \+2.5% | Understands foundational concepts and can reliably produce basic magical effects. |
| III | **Initiate** | 20-34 | \+5% | Has developed a functional understanding of the discipline and can apply it in practical situations. |
| IV | **Apprentice** | 35-54 | \+10% | Formal student-level proficiency. Demonstrates growing control and consistency. |
| V | **Journeyman** | 55-79 | \+15% | Competent practitioner capable of independent use and problem solving within the discipline. |
| VI | **Proficient** | 80-109 | \+22.5% | Possesses a strong working knowledge and can employ the discipline effectively in most situations. |
| VII | **Skilled** | 110-144 | \+30% | Demonstrates refined technique, improved efficiency, and reliable execution under pressure. |
| VIII | **Expert** | 145-184 | \+40% | Recognized as highly capable within the discipline. Can teach and mentor less experienced practitioners. |
| IX | **Adept** | 185-229 | \+50% | Deep understanding of the discipline's principles. Able to innovate and adapt techniques effectively. |
| X | **Virtuoso** | 230-279 | \+65% | Exceptional mastery and intuitive understanding. Displays talent and precision rarely encountered. |
| XI | **Master** | 280-334 | \+80% | One of the foremost practitioners of the discipline. Capable of extraordinary feats and advanced instruction. |
| XII | **Grandmaster** | 335-394 | \+100% | Represents the pinnacle of mortal mastery for most practitioners. Their understanding borders on instinct. |
| XIII | **Legendary** | 395-459 | \+125% | Known far beyond local regions. Their accomplishments become stories, case studies, and historical examples. |
| XIV | **Transcendent** | 460-529 | \+140% | Has surpassed conventional limitations of the discipline and approaches a fundamentally deeper understanding of its nature. |
| XV | **Divine** | 530+ | \+150% | Possesses near-perfect comprehension of the discipline. Such individuals redefine what is believed possible within their chosen field. |

### **Design Philosophy**

**Spell Skill Mastery measures understanding, not raw power.**

A practitioner with higher Spell Skill does not merely cast larger spells. Instead, they demonstrate a deeper understanding of the discipline itself, resulting in stronger manifestations, greater efficiency, superior control, and enhanced resistance to related magical effects.

For example:

* A Divine Fire Mage does not simply produce larger flames; their fire burns more efficiently, responds more precisely, and is far more difficult to counter.  
* A Master Healer restores wounds with greater precision and fewer unintended side effects.  
* A Grandmaster Illusionist creates illusions that are more convincing, stable, and difficult to perceive through.

Spell Skill represents mastery of the discipline itself rather than mastery of any individual spell. A practitioner may possess tremendous expertise in a magical school while still learning new spells within that discipline.

# **Soul Level**

| Tier | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| Fractured | Damaged soul |
| Faint | Barely present |
| Weak | Small existential weight |
| Lesser | Below average |
| Common | Average person |
| Strong | Noticeable presence |
| Luminous | Soul can be sensed |
| Radiant | Soul influences surroundings |
| Brilliant | Difficult to dominate |
| Resplendent | Soul leaves impressions |
| Exalted | Commands instinctive respect |
| Transcendent | Exists beyond ordinary limits |
| Divine | Comparable to lesser gods |
| Absolute | Near-immutable identity |

**Soul Level measures the metaphysical weight, permanence, and authority of a soul.**

A soul with greater weight is harder to alter, erase, corrupt, bind, possess, or overwrite. It leaves deeper impressions upon the world and carries greater influence in matters involving identity, vows, names, death, resurrection, and the supernatural.

Soul Level is not a direct measure of combat power, skill, intelligence, or magical talent. Instead, it reflects how strongly a being exists within the fabric of reality itself.

Powerful souls possess greater **staying power**. Their actions, creations, enchantments, oaths, and legacies persist longer and resist external interference. A powerful soul can invest part of itself into objects, places, rituals, and bonds, creating effects that endure long after the original act is complete.

At the highest levels, a soul becomes an anchor upon reality. Such beings are extraordinarily difficult to dominate, rewrite, forget, or remove from existence entirely.

**"A soul determines not how hard you can strike reality, but how long reality remembers the strike."**

*Worked anchors:* the namebound **elven child, Vaeli** (Act 3) is a **Strong** soul — a notable catch, harder than average; **Seb's brother** is a **Common** soul — routine for the Exchange to hold.

Class rarity uses the same seven-tier ladder — **Common · Uncommon · Rare · Exceptional · Legendary · Mythic · Unique** — but its authoritative home is `core_rules.md` (Class Tiers); the class taxonomy is in `classes.md`. # Attribute / Growth Boundary

`mechanics.md` does **not** own resource formulas, attribute growth, class attribute multipliers, class XP curves, or stat templates.

Use:

- `resource_system.md` for derived pools, regeneration, depletion, Reserve, XP curve, species growth, and resource-facing stat snapshots.
- `classes.md` for class attribute profiles and Prime/Core multipliers.
- `core_rules.md` for system-level routing and terminology.

Current locks to preserve when editing owner files:

- Humans gain **4 free attribute points per level**.
- Specialized and exotic species may receive forced/favored growth.
- Class rarity bonus attribute point cadence is removed.
- Class rarity still affects XP burden and energy burden.
- Class influence uses Prime/Core attribute multipliers, not recurring bonus points.
