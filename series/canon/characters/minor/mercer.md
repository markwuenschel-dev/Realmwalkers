## Core Identity

Mercer is the honest half of the facility's welcome — the person who tells the six exactly what the data shows and exactly what it doesn't, without softening either. Where Roth carries the legal/consent burden, Mercer carries the medical and scientific one, and he treats both as equally serious. He's dry about the theater of his own reassurances ("yes, this is an obvious attempt to make the room feel less clinical") but never dry about the actual risk.

## Relationships

### Marcus (Marcus Vye)
Professional and direct. Marcus tests him with pointed questions (what exactly changed, can the implant do what the demo showed, would Mercer do this himself) and Mercer answers plainly rather than managing him. Mutual, low-key respect. Present again briefly pre-op, calmer and more grounded than at the conference table.

### Roth
Colleagues from adjacent departments, not intimates. Mercer corrects Roth's legal phrasing toward medical precision twice during the consent conversation; Roth accepts both corrections without resistance — a working division of labor (Mercer owns the science, Roth owns the legal/consent framing), not a power struggle. Neither knows what the other doesn't know about the program's true sponsor.

### The Six (general)
Runs their baseline testing and reads their reactions accurately (e.g., catches Marcus's motion-perception anomaly being noted as a false "error" rather than a possible prediction, and doesn't dismiss it). Treats all six as patients and research subjects at once, honestly, without pretending the two roles don't create tension.
