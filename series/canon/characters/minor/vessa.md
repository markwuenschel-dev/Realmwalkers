# Vessa ("Ves") — Minor Character File

**Status:** minor; **likely recurring → Book 2** (flag for a fuller file if she returns).
**Where:** the dungeon town beside the delve — Serra's Book-1 solo arc.
**Function:** the young-Serra mirror; route-knowledge agent; the recognition that gives Serra her reason to stay (resolves the open "why does Serra stay in the dungeon town?").

---

## Who She Is

A **thirteen-year-old tunnel-runner** in the dungeon town: bright with the younger children, dry with adults, terrifyingly competent underground. She knows the delve's tunnels the way Serra knows a fight — from the inside: marks, air, slope, moisture, monster movement, doors, route habits, changed geometry.

**The tell — and the wound:** she **does not ask for help.** She *gives Serra the information Serra would need to help, then turns to go alone* — because she has already learned that asking people to stay or follow is wasted breath. She has made her own competence the reason no one has to choose her.

**She is the beginning of Serra.** The warmth-to-function switch, the hidden fear under the competence, the refusal to ask — Serra recognizes all of it, because it is her own oldest armor worn by someone too young to have earned it yet.

---

## Why She Matters — the recognition beat

Serra, fresh off rationalizing her own *leaving*, watches Vessa switch from warmth to function, sees the fear under the competence, sees the refusal to ask — and recognizes herself. She stays not from generic protectiveness but because she **cannot walk past someone enacting the choice she just failed to make.** Getting Vessa through the delve alive is how she pays down the guilt of leaving Marcus.

---

## The changing dungeon

**Background cause:** broader Realm instability — ley lines becoming less stable; ley-line shifts inform or alter dungeons. The change is **not** visible collapse or melodramatic malfunction. It is small, local, practical, survival-relevant.

**Vessa notices first** — before the adults — because she knows the routes from the inside. Serra **believes her** before the adults fully do. This is a major early trust beat.

**Serra's failure mode:** she almost repeats the adult pattern — she accepts Vessa's *perception* but not Vessa's *agency*. She tries to keep Vessa off the dangerous route because she wants to protect her. The failure is not screaming or cruelty. Serra uses calm adult certainty, warmth, competence, and the room's trust to make disagreement socially unwinnable.

**Vessa's accusation (child-plausible):** *"You made it so if I argue, I sound like a kid."* Avoid overly polished dialogue.

**The repair:** Serra tells Vessa, in substance: *"I don't want you going. That's not the same as saying no."* She explains the danger without predetermining the answer, then lets Vessa's knowledge matter under real constraints.

**Outcome:** Vessa and Serra go through the changed route / new door and find an **Eriadne fragment or clue** — Serra's independent pull toward Eriadne, not merely Marcus-following motivation.

**Guardrails:** Do **not** use toll-seals, road-pass systems, namebinding, or Court-of-Seals logic in this arc unless required elsewhere. Vessa is not a passive child Serra simply saves — she has route knowledge the fight requires.

---

## The boss

Tests Serra as a **Warrior before she has her interface**. Preferred type: dungeon predator, ambusher, guardian, or terrain-adapted threat that uses the **changed dungeon layout** better than Serra can initially read. Not simply "another warrior" Serra out-fights.

**Fight function:** Serra can handle the physical danger, but she cannot make the arena legible alone. She must **trust Vessa's live read under pressure.** Victory proves the emotional lesson physically: Serra wins not by protecting harder or overruling Vessa, but by letting Vessa's knowledge matter while Serra does the fighting.
