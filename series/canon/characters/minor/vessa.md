**Status:** minor; **likely recurring → Book 2** (flag for a fuller file if she returns).

**Function:** the young-Serra mirror; the recognition that gives Serra her reason to stay (resolves the open "why does Serra stay in the dungeon town?").

---

## Who She Is

A **thirteen-year-old tunnel-runner** in the dungeon town: bright with the younger children, dry with adults, terrifyingly competent underground. She knows the delve's tunnels the way Serra knows a fight.

**The tell — and the wound:** she **does not ask for help.** She *gives Serra the information Serra would need to help, then turns to go alone* — because she has already learned that asking people to stay or follow is wasted breath. She has made her own competence the reason no one has to choose her.

**She is the beginning of Serra.** The warmth-to-function switch, the hidden fear under the competence, the refusal to ask — Serra recognizes all of it, because it is her own oldest armor worn by someone too young to have earned it yet.

---

## Why She Matters — the recognition beat

Serra, fresh off rationalizing her own *leaving*, watches Vessa switch from warmth to function, sees the fear under the competence, sees the refusal to ask — and recognizes herself. She stays not from generic protectiveness but because she **cannot walk past someone enacting the choice she just failed to make.** Getting Vessa through the delve alive is how she pays down the guilt of leaving Marcus.
