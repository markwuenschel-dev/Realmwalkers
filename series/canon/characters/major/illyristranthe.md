## The Name — Three Forms, Three Registers

The three names are not equivalent alternatives. They carry different weight and different relationship register:

- **Illyristranthe** — what she was, what she remembers, what she is recovering toward. Vast, inhuman, not suited to spoken language. She invokes it sparingly. It names something larger than the fragment Marcus knows.
- **Illyri** — the gift. The portion of the name she allows others to use; the form that lets someone speak to her as a person rather than a remnant. Appropriate. Not intimate.
- **Ily** — the nickname close friends earn. Ridiculous. She hates it. The arc: she hates it → she finds she has stopped hating it from someone who earned it → she hates that she's stopped hating it.

## Physical / Appearance

> *First impression: ancient, vast, reduced. Something that used to be larger than the space it now occupies.*

Illyristranthe does not need a stable form unless she chooses one. Default: a presence attached to Marcus's implant — **voice first, image second.** When she takes shape, she approximates personhood without fully committing to it.

Her light is old — not warm, not angelic, but the kind of light that has traveled very far and carries the distance in it. Her edges don't end cleanly; they bleed into something that suggests the space between stars rather than mist or smoke. When she has been still too long, she stops looking like a person and starts looking like a question about depth.

Her eyes are fixed points that emit rather than reflect — no pupils, just depth. Her face holds together at casual register but loses resolution when she stops tending it. When speaking drily she's almost small. When her full weight surfaces — when the fragment lets something of what she was come through — the depth becomes hard to look at directly, the way you can't hold your eyes on a star.

The damaged aspect reads as structural, not decorative: like a constellation with missing stars, or ruins that imply, by their shape, what must have stood there.

**Not angelic.** No seraphic resonance, no cathedral imagery, no -iel / -ael flavor, no divine-in-the-religious-sense. She suggests age in the cosmic sense — the kind of old that does not comfort.

## Voice & Role

Mentor, but unreliable and incomplete. She helps Marcus ask better questions rather than handing him easy answers — she should not solve too much. Her established register (the dry "*No idiot, an inanimate machine is not spontaneously talking to you*" introduction) is canonical; see `voice_guide.md` and `dialogue_rules.md`. Relationship beats: `relationship_map.md`.

Casually: dry, sharp, exasperated. Ancient but not formal — contractions, bluntness, no speechifying. When something matters to her, she interrupts. When she pauses, the pause means something.

Beneath the dryness: something very old that has lost most of what it remembered and is not at peace with that.

## Cosmic Nature & Cosmological Position

She remembers being larger than the space she now occupies. She is what remains after **defeat in battle** — an entity that could not fully annihilate her reduced her to this **celestial fragment** instead. That antagonist is not named in current canon; the identity is deferred (series-level antagonist lane).

Her knowledge of the Realm's deeper cosmology — the five-faction structure, the Pale Imperium, older orders — is oblique: familiarity that does not arrive as clean memory. Structural fragment damage bounds what she can recall; gaps are not deliberate sabotage by a trapper. She does not clearly belong to any named faction; she predates or exists outside the current cosmic categories.

**Resurrection & attachment:** She **spent permanent power** to resurrect Marcus after Xazzidiuk killed him — that cost is why his return is exceptional, not cheap. The implant attachment was the mutual bargain: she needed an anchor; he needed a guide. Life for a foothold.

Her relationship to **Meszkhal** (the entity the *Eyes of Meszkhal* item is named for) is an open question. She does not confirm or deny it cleanly.
