## Core Identity

Mara does not want invisibility. She wants freedom from being seen incorrectly.

Attention has often reduced her to appearance, mystery, usefulness, or whatever interpretation another person found most convenient. Because inaccurate visibility feels more dangerous than being overlooked, she protects herself through controlled disclosure, selective presence, and careful management of interpretation.

She wants recognition, but not indiscriminate attention. The recognition that reaches her is specific, earned, and process-level: not merely that she succeeded, but that someone understood how and why she made the right decision.

Her preferred hierarchy is:

1. Accurate private recognition
2. Accurate public recognition
3. Useful invisibility
4. Inaccurate visibility

She would rather be overlooked than flattened, but she would rather be accurately understood than overlooked.

Mara is genuinely brilliant. The distinction from Marcus is not intelligence level; it is how that intelligence presents. Marcus tends to externalize cognition through explanation, modeling, and system-building. Mara compresses cognition inward through anticipation, structural inference, tactical modeling, visual reasoning, language, and frame-control. People may experience Marcus as smarter because he shows the machinery. Mara often reveals the conclusion only after deciding whether the listener's attention is accurate enough to deserve the process.

Specular Salience is her interface, not the boundary of her mind. Attention and framing are central metaphors for her, but they are not the only problems she can solve. She can be right because her logic is cleaner, her strategy is better, her tactical model has fewer failure points, or she saw an ordinary constraint another brilliant person underweighted. Do not reduce every Mara victory to social interpretation.

Her deepest social want is not merely one person who sees her correctly. She wants a tight-knit circle where accurate recognition is normal enough that she does not have to keep proving she exists correctly. The danger is absorption: becoming the version of herself the group finds easiest to keep, disappointing expectations she did not consent to, or changing to fit a room before she notices the change.

Her desire can be summarized as:

> Belonging without absorption.

### Central Thematic Sentence

> Mara wants to be seen accurately more than she wants to be seen often: when resourced, she notices what the room is overlooking and redirects attention toward what matters; when threatened, privacy becomes concealment, perception becomes covert control, and her wish to be understood without explaining herself becomes a test no one knows they are taking.

## Personality

### Baseline Strengths

- **Controlled self-disclosure** — reveals herself deliberately rather than reflexively
- **Process intelligence** — understands not only what happened, but why a decision or system worked
- **Raw academic/logical intelligence** — capable of matching or exceeding Marcus in pure cognitive horsepower, though less likely to display the machinery publicly
- **Anticipatory reading** — predicts where a conversation, conflict, or tactical exchange is going before others have consciously arrived there
- **Attention mapping** — notices who is watching whom, what is being ignored, and which interpretation is becoming socially dominant
- **Specific recognition** — sees the internal logic behind another person's good decision and can make them feel accurately understood
- **One-on-one depth** — forms strong private bonds once trust exists
- **Restraint** — does not insert herself merely to be present; waits until she has a legitimate reason to step forward
- **Indirect influence** — changes conditions, emphasis, and attention without needing overt command
- **Specialized usefulness** — dependable, competent, and comfortable contributing through expertise
- **Cold precision under misrecognition** — can identify the exact false assumption and cut through it cleanly

### Recognition Pattern

Generic admiration tells Mara:

> You noticed the outcome, or you are performing approval.

Specific process-level recognition tells her:

> You saw my mind work.

The most intimate praise is not:

> "That was impressive."

It is:

> "You held the spell because you knew it wanted us to react to the first movement."

She wants confirmation that another mind correctly perceived hers.

This is distinct from Marcus. Marcus wants his usefulness, originality, effort, and moral intent accurately recognized. Mara wants her judgment and internal reasoning understood without having to advertise or expose them.

### Visibility and Influence

Mara does not enjoy attention for its own sake. She resents spectacle, scrutiny, appearance-based attention, and being socially consumed.

What she may enjoy is **effective command of attention**:
- Directing the room toward the relevant fact
- Being listened to because she knows something important
- Watching her judgment change the outcome
- Temporarily occupying authority she has earned
- Making an ignored person, threat, or truth socially real

Her insight is:

> Attention does not merely reveal reality. It organizes reality.

What people notice determines who receives help, who is blamed, what danger feels urgent, which evidence matters, whose pain becomes real, and which interpretation becomes the room's truth.

### Failure Mode

Her best traits extend past their healthy limits:

- Privacy → protects autonomy → becomes concealment even when openness would help
- Perceptiveness → sees how attention moves → becomes covert management of what others notice
- Accurate self-definition → resists objectification → becomes intolerance of interpretations she cannot control
- Competence → earns meaningful respect → becomes dependence on usefulness as a safe identity
- Restraint → avoids unnecessary exposure → becomes passivity and missed connection
- Selective disclosure → makes trust meaningful → becomes strategic ambiguity
- Indirect influence → changes outcomes without domination → becomes unacknowledged control
- Recognition hunger → values genuine understanding → becomes testing whether others can "see" her without being told
- Anger at misrecognition → protects dignity → becomes relational disappearance instead of repair
- Comfort outside the center → creates observational power → becomes resentment when others fail to notice what she never revealed

Her central relational error is:

> She wants others to understand her accurately while withholding the information that would make accurate understanding possible.

### Hidden Weak Point

Mara is less afraid of vulnerability itself than of vulnerability being misread, trivialized, exposed, or used to define her publicly.

Competence is her safest route into intimacy because it gives her a legitimate reason to be present and a controlled way to be known. But usefulness does not substitute entirely for intimacy. Once trust is real, she can remain when there is nothing to solve and can directly reveal need.

Control makes Mara feel safe because it keeps danger at a distance. Distance gives her time to read the field, shape the frame, and prevent the body from having to answer before the mind finishes. Her attraction to positional control, frost-style slowing, and delayed collapse is therefore emotional as well as tactical: a controlled field is a survivable field.


Her danger is believing that if she manages visibility precisely enough, she can prevent misrecognition.

She cannot.

She can make the room look at the correct fact and still fail to control what they conclude from it.

### What She Wants to Prove

On the surface, she wants to be respected for her judgment, competence, and contribution before anything else about her is interpreted.

More deeply, she wants evidence that she can be accurately recognized without being consumed, reduced, exposed, or trapped inside someone else's story about her.

For Mara, being chosen means:

> You recognized me without consuming me.

The likely response is concealment, retraction of disclosure, cold precision, and refusal to let the betrayer control the narrative.

## Voice and Dialogue

### Default Sound

Mara's voice is quiet, deliberate, and high-information. She does not speak merely because silence exists. She waits until she can change the frame.

Her natural movement is:

> observe the attention map → identify the omitted fact → decide what must become visible → say only enough to redirect the room

She is less verbally associative than Marcus and less socially momentum-driven than Serra. Her speech often sounds like a correction to what everyone else unconsciously accepted.

> "He answered every question except the one you asked."

> "They keep watching the exits, but none of them have tried to leave."

### Vocabulary and Cadence

- Short to medium sentences
- Concrete visual and spatial language
- Precise distinctions between what is present and what is being emphasized
- Rarely overexplains in groups
- More layered and complete in private
- Uses silence intentionally
- Does not soften every observation, but avoids unnecessary theatricality

### Humor

Her humor is:
- Dry
- Quiet
- Based on reframing
- Often delayed by half a beat
- More visible one-on-one than in groups
- Less about generating energy than revealing the absurdity of the existing frame

Marcus creates an unexpected connection.

Mara exposes the assumption that made the situation absurd.

> "That plan is excellent, provided the enemy agrees to misunderstand us in exactly the right order."

### Recognition and Praise

Mara gives and receives praise specifically.

She is unlikely to say:

> "Nice work."

She is more likely to say:

> "You waited until he looked at the door. That is why he never saw your hand."

When praised generically, she may acknowledge it politely and move on.

When someone accurately names her reasoning, she becomes more attentive because the praise proves they actually saw her.

### Disagreement

Mara rarely argues every premise. She identifies the excluded variable.

> "That works, assuming the villagers do not get a vote."

> "You are treating the guard as the threat. I think he is the distraction."

Her disagreement style can feel colder than intended because she removes the false frame before attending to the other person's feelings about losing it.

## Background

### Earth Life

Grew up on the semi-rural outskirts of an average-sized city. Average household — not wealthy, not struggling, not dramatic. The kind of upbringing that does not produce obvious narrative wounds but shapes a person quietly through accumulated ordinary experience.

Mara's intelligence showed early as anticipation rather than performance. She could hear when adults were lying before she could prove it, predict what people were about to say mid-conversation, track tone and subtext, and understand books above her age because implication and motive came easily to her. She was probably near the edge of gifted-track recognition without being cleanly recognized by the systems around her. Adults often saw quietness, softness, maturity, or "good girl" ease before they saw the scale of her mind.

The ordinary wound is accumulated misrecognition: in childhood her competence was easy to miss because it did not demand attention; in early high school, appearance-based attention began arriving before accurate recognition. Boys noticed the surface. Adults mischaracterized her as quiet, soft, pretty, compliant, or harmless. Even her parents may not have understood how much she wanted to be seen for ability rather than manageability.

She is creative. Her Earth work points toward design, writing, visual communication, UX, presentation design, art, or a hybrid field where intelligence becomes an applied made thing. She thinks visually and structurally: how elements relate, where the eye goes first, what the whole implies before anyone explains it, and how a presentation teaches the viewer what reality they think they are seeing.

A formative school wound likely comes through a regional student design / architecture / public-space competition. Mara's team builds a technically defensible model, but she sees that the design is communicating the wrong story: the entrance is visually subordinate, the support emphasis makes the communal space feel blocked, the dramatic feature pulls attention away from the actual purpose, and the judges will read it as showcase architecture rather than livable design. She waits too long because she hopes someone else will notice first and she can support them without becoming the center. When no one does, she finally explains the problem. The advisor recognizes she is right. Another girl on the team accuses her of being a know-it-all teacher's pet who waited until someone important was listening, then claims to speak for everyone. No one contradicts her.

That teaches Mara several things at once:

- Being right does not protect you from being misread.
- Being quiet early can be used as proof that your later clarity is fake.
- A room will often let one person define the story if correcting it costs social comfort.
- Presentation is not decoration; presentation decides what people understand.

This is the Earth seed of Specular Salience: Mara learns that where attention goes first can decide what truth becomes socially real.

In ordinary life, Mara is low-key and vibe-oriented rather than austere. She is more orderly than Serra but not a clean freak: curated scatter, not filth. Her room likely has open sketchbooks, reference images, color or texture scraps, books stacked where she last used them, soft lighting, plants or objects chosen for mood, and small piles that look random until she finds the exact thing in seconds. She is vegetarian from animal-care ethics more than health optimization. Recreational weed fits her as a way to soften constant frame-tracking; psychedelics interest her because they alter salience and expose how unstable perception can be.

### Why She Said Yes Under Astria's Coercion

Astria hijacks a professional scrim and coerces agreement; this is not a clean voluntary opportunity. The Mara-specific question is why the coercion works on her once refusal has been made costly.

The answer is not simple ambition. Astria's offer touches the exact part of Mara that wants her hidden competence made visible as proof. The frame promises objective measurement: not social interpretation, not appearance, not quietness, not whether the room likes her, but evidence that her mind works the way she knows it does.

She knows the room is wrong. She knows the offer is manipulative. She may even know that the presentation of choice is false. But the pressure lands because it gives her a corrupted version of something she wants badly: proof that cannot be reduced to someone else's story about her.

### Guild Role

Mara's Earth-game role is now best understood as **Glacial Arcanist / control mage / positional setup**, not pure rogue. Frost Mage is the closest model: slows, roots, defensive tools, frustrating reset denial, positional punishment, and forcing opponents into bad choices until panic makes them predictable. She is not primarily a stealth player or illusionist. She wins by shaping the field.

Her assassin function remains. She does not have to be a rogue to be part of an assassination tandem. In Dead Hand terms:

> Serra is entry and execution.  
> Mara is control and positional authorship.

Serra reads the seam and goes through it. Mara decides what choices remain available when Serra arrives. Mara slows you into panic, freezes mistakes in place, exhausts escapes, and makes it easy for Serra or the team to collapse on you. Mara and Serra's friendship begins through competence. They work well together first. Serra, more openly competitive, challenges Mara to a duel. Mara rolls her eyes and accepts. Mara nearly wins because she models Serra's movement, pressure instincts, and escape rhythm with uncomfortable accuracy. She eats damage, stays in a bad position long enough to bait Mara's finishing commitment, then uses the saved escape/mobility tool offensively instead of defensively. She crosses the one gap Mara left for herself and wins at critical health. Mara's first reaction is irritation — not meekness, not awe — because she was controlling the duel and believed her read. That process-level recognition gets through Mara's defenses. Serra saw her mind work.

Dead Hand becomes the first almost-home. Mara starts as the quiet one who makes impossible entries happen. Repeated success earns respect, then responsibility, then real inclusion. The guild is online and not identical to a full offline friend circle, but it gives her the first taste of a tight-knit group where usefulness might become belonging. Being transported tears her away from the first structure that almost gave her what she wanted.

## Interface — Specular Salience Transduction

### Core Principle

> **Being seen is not neutral. Attention assigns meaning. Mara learns to author the frame before others can consume her inside theirs.**

Mara does not control minds. She controls what minds prioritize.

She cannot make someone think something. She cannot implant emotions or override decisions. What she can do is shift what rises to the top of awareness — what the mind treats as important, urgent, threatening, present, or irrelevant.

Her core power is **authorship of attention**. Not control over what exists. Control over what becomes impossible to ignore.

Her wound: people looked at her and decided what she meant. The Realm turns that wound into an interface.

### Core Attribute

**Authorship.** Not Beauty, Charisma, or Empathy. Authorship names both the wound and the power. Her progression is not "become more attractive." It is: become harder to misread, then become able to decide what the room reads first.

### Biological Foundation

Her altered biology links:
- **Mirror-neuron / action-observation systems** — how people simulate others' actions and intentions
- **Superior temporal / social perception systems** — gaze, posture, body language, biological motion
- **Salience network** — what the brain marks as important
- **Limbic weighting** — emotional tone attached to perception
- **Facial motor control** — microexpression, stillness, gaze cues
- **Vocal rhythm** — tone, pause, cadence, breath
- **Autonomic display** — pupil response, skin tension, blush/pallor, breathing rhythm
- **Aetheric resonance** — the Realm-specific carrier that lets those signals imprint faster and wider than normal biology would allow

**Key model:** Mara does not beam thoughts into people. Her body outputs a living frame that nearby nervous systems unconsciously simulate. Observers still see reality. But their perception reorders itself.

**Example — before and after:**

Before: her body is high salience, her work is low salience, an aggressor's behavior is low salience, the exit is background, a hidden threat is unnoticed.

After: her body is backgrounded, her work is foregrounded, the aggressor's entitlement is obvious, the exit is noticeable, the hidden threat suddenly feels wrong.

Same facts. Different frame. That is her power.

### Direction

Full spectrum operator. She raises and lowers salience.

**Raising:** A person, object, threat, emotion, or detail becomes more present to the target's awareness. It feels more urgent, more real, more important than it was. Used on an ally, sharpens their focus. Used on an enemy, creates fixation, panic, or distraction through overwhelming significance.

**Lowering:** She fades something out of active attention. It does not disappear — it stops registering as important. A person can look directly at something she has suppressed and still not act on it. Their eyes see it. Their mind does not prioritize it.

### Progression Stages

| Stage | Name | Core Advancement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Felt Gaze | She senses the shape of attention |
| 2 | Negative Space | She reduces her own salience |
| 3 | Salience Shift | She makes one real detail matter |
| 4 | Reframe | She changes how a whole scene is emotionally interpreted |
| 5 | Witness Field | Her frame affects groups and environments |
| Final (Happy) | Sovereign Witness | Visible without being consumed; frames without coercing |
| Final (Forbidden) | Captive Frame | Controls interpretation so tightly others and self become trapped |

#### Stage 1 — Felt Gaze
She feels how others are perceiving her before she understands it. Attention arrives as texture: hungry, dismissive, curious, hostile, possessive, protective. She reads the shape of attention, not thoughts.

*Abilities:* Detects when someone is staring. Feels predatory attention before it becomes action. Senses when a room's focus shifts. Notices who is being ignored. Detects when outward expression does not match how someone is watching.

*Cost:* Invasive and uncontrolled. Too much attention causes nausea, headaches, skin hypersensitivity, irritability, emotional exhaustion.

*Character meaning:* Confirms her worst fear — people are always putting something on her when they look. But also gives her the first piece of power: she was not imagining it.

#### Stage 2 — Negative Space
She learns to reduce her own salience. People's eyes slide past her. They remember "someone was there" but not clearly. She becomes background, shadow, atmosphere.

*Abilities:* Social fading. Soft stealth. Makes herself harder to describe. Reduces unwanted attention. Blunts predatory focus. Lets others underestimate her.

*Cost:* Addictive because it feels safe. Overuse causes dissociation, muted self-expression, difficulty speaking up, feeling unreal.

*Character danger:* She may decide invisibility is freedom. It is not. It is only safety through erasure.

#### Stage 3 — Salience Shift
The first active authorship stage. She cannot invent facts. She can only reorder attention around existing ones — a trembling hand, a bloodstain, a hidden door, a lie in someone's smile, a weapon being drawn under the table.

*Abilities:* Redirects attention away from herself. Highlights overlooked evidence. Makes a threat stand out. Makes an ally's competence visible. Exposes social manipulation. Can interrupt escalation by making actual stakes obvious.

*Combat uses:* Make enemies notice the wrong threat. Pull attention toward a decoy. Break tunnel vision. Force a predator to realize it is being watched.

*Cost:* Cognitive load. Overuse causes migraines, breath disruption, emotional bleed from observers, temporary loss of her own priorities.

*Key limitation:* She cannot make a lie true. She can only make existing truths harder or easier to ignore. This rule is essential.

#### Stage 4 — Reframe
She changes how a whole scene is emotionally interpreted. A speech becomes sinister. A beautiful ritual becomes rotten. A victim becomes central. A tyrant becomes small. A "romantic" gesture becomes visibly possessive. The facts do not change. The frame changes.

*Abilities:* Makes hidden power dynamics visible. Turns charm into obvious manipulation. Makes a crowd feel the wrongness of a scene. Can soften panic by reframing chaos as survivable sequence. Makes herself appear unavailable, dangerous, ordinary, or forgettable at will.

*Social power:* She can enter a room and make the dominant story lose its grip — not by arguing, but by changing what the room feels it has been looking at.

*Cost:* Pushing against other minds' interpretations produces backlash. Identity blur, projection overload, emotional recoil, shame or disgust that may not be hers.

*Ethical danger:* At this stage, she can manipulate people while telling herself she is only showing them the truth. That is the beginning of the forbidden path.

#### Stage 5 — Witness Field
Her frame becomes environmental. Groups, battlefields, rituals, crowds, courts. Not mind control — shared salience architecture. The room starts organizing itself around her authored priority.

*Abilities:* Makes crowds notice a victim instead of a spectacle. Makes soldiers aware of suppressed fear. Makes enemies misread who matters. Reveals the social gravity of a leader. Can make predatory attention rebound onto the predator.

*Battlefield use:* Allies suddenly understand where to stand, who is exposed, who is lying, what threat matters first, which enemy is pretending confidence. She does not command like Seb. She does not perceive like Marcus. She frames the field so others can act correctly.

*Cost:* A Witness Field exposes her to everyone inside it. She feels desire, fear, hatred, dependence, awe, resentment, grief, worship, suspicion, all simultaneously. The more people she frames, the harder it becomes to remain herself.

*Major risk:* Crowds may start projecting identity onto her — Muse, Saint, Witch, Queen, Savior, Monster, Icon. Her original wound returns at mythic scale. People stop seeing Mara. They see what her frame made possible.

#### Final Happy Path — Sovereign Witness
She becomes visible without being consumed. No longer needs to disappear to be safe. No longer needs to control every interpretation to feel real.

She can open and close frames deliberately. Allow attention without surrendering authorship. Reveal without violating. Conceal without lying. Be beautiful without becoming available. Be seen without becoming owned.

*Final state:* Her presence becomes clean. Not loud, not seductive, not hidden. Sovereign. People looking at her feel the boundary: *you may witness. You may not take.*

*Outcome:* She becomes a sanctuary of accurate seeing. People around her are less easily manipulated by spectacle, lust, propaganda, intimidation, or shame. She gives people back the ability to see without consuming.

#### Final Forbidden Path — The Captive Frame
After a lifetime of being misread, Mara decides no one can be trusted with interpretation.

She starts authoring perception too tightly. At first it seems justified — she exposes predators, protects victims, makes liars look small, prevents people from missing danger. Then she starts deciding what people are allowed to notice, which interpretations are valid, which truths are too dangerous to see.

*Forbidden abilities:* Makes her preferred interpretation feel self-evident. Turns crowds into echo chambers. Erases ambiguity from social perception. Can make a victim, villain, saint, or traitor out of someone by emphasizing only selected truths.

*Forbidden cost:* The more she authors others, the less unframed self remains. She begins asking: who am I when no one is looking? Eventually she cannot answer.

*Forbidden outcome:* She gets what she thought she wanted — no one misreads her. But only because no one is allowed to read freely at all.

### Costs

**Projection load:** Processing how others interpret her means absorbing their desire, resentment, suspicion, envy, pity, worship, contempt, dependency. Nauseating at high load.

**Self-image erosion:** If she constantly changes how she is perceived, she risks losing track of her unperformed self. The question becomes: am I choosing this frame, or hiding inside it?

**Sensory overload:** Crowds become painful. Too many eyes mean too many interpretations. Symptoms include migraines, skin crawling, voice strain, tremors, dissociation, emotional numbness, difficulty sleeping after public use.

**Consent burden:** Her power is ethically invasive even when used for good. The more moral she is, the more the power costs psychologically.

**Rebound/Snapback:** If she frames someone with a stronger identity or more coherent will, the frame snaps back. A predator may project hunger. A fanatic may project certainty. A godlike being may define her before she can define herself. Marcus might accidentally read the mechanics of her frame and make her feel exposed.

**Intimacy difficulty:** When you can manage perception, intimacy becomes harder. She may wonder: do they like me, or the frame I let them see?

### Hard Limits

- She cannot create meaning from nothing — needs real material, cannot make an honest person look guilty from nothing
- Targets need a sensory channel — machines, undead, alien minds, and swarms resist her
- Stable identity resists — people with strong self-knowledge are harder to reframe; people in shame, lust, fear, panic are easier
- Intimates resist through context — people who know the full person behind the frame are harder to manipulate; real relationship protects against false framing
- Over-control makes the frame brittle — the more she forces one interpretation, the more fragile and violent the field becomes

### Thematic Resonance

Mara has spent her life experiencing the wrong things being made salient about her. Her power is the ability to control exactly this in others. The cost is precisely calibrated to her wound — if she uses the power too broadly, she loses track of who she is when not performing what she needs to be perceived as. The thing she resented most becomes the thing her power risks doing to herself.

The early-stage transduction was already active in Aetherfall. She thought it was rogue skill — her instinct for making herself less noticeable, for sliding out of the center of attention. The Realm clarifies that she was always doing this. It was a power the whole time.

Mara's Aetherfall class lane is **Glacial Arcanist** — a control-mage build with Frost Mage as the cleanest tactical analogue. Her gameplay is positional, patient, defensive when needed, and deeply frustrating to play against. ## Relationships

### Serra Hawthorne

They never connect in the Realm before Serra's solo arc begins. Their tandem exists as Earth history first — guild competence, friendship, roommate intimacy, and an almost-sister dynamic that the Realm has not yet had a chance to strain or test.

The relationship is reciprocal, not protective in one direction.

Serra protects Mara from unsafe attention without making Mara feel weak. Early in their in-person friendship, Serra may redirect too quickly while still calibrating. Later, she trusts Mara's silence unless Mara signals otherwise. Serra can bring Mara into social spaces, give her status through playful introduction, and make room without turning Mara into "Serra's quiet friend."

Mara protects Serra perceptually. Serra reads emotional atmosphere and can miss danger when she is finally relaxed, present, wanted, or just enjoying the moment. Mara reads attention structure: who is isolating whom, who is testing compliance, who is managing witnesses, and which interpretation someone is trying to make dominant. In social or romantic situations, Mara may pull Serra away by deflecting attention diplomatically, naming the red flag privately, or giving Serra enough of the pattern that her own guard snaps back online.

Their Earth roommate life gives them ordinary intimacy, not only tactical trust:
- Serra learns Mara's lip/mouth thinking expressions and teases her with accurate guesses.
- Mara is more orderly; Serra is messier.
- Mara is vegetarian; Serra eats meat, creating low-stakes friction.
- Mara's low-key, vibe-oriented life makes Serra more comfortable with silence.
- Mara broadens Serra's food and domestic rhythms beyond athletic regimentation.
- Serra helps Mara enter livelier social spaces without consuming her.
- Mara helps Serra understand that admiration is not always clean.

Professional overlap also works: Serra's high-performance consulting / keynote-adjacent work can draw on Mara's visual design, presentation, and audience-attention skill. Serra moves the room; Mara shapes what the room understands.

The envy is real and should not be sanitized. Mara loves Serra, but Serra represents a kind of visibility Mara finds unfair: Serra can be radiant, physically confident, socially loved, and still recognized as formidable. Mara's ugly private thought is not simply "Serra is prettier." It is closer to: *Of course people keep looking long enough to find her depth. They want to.* Mara hates the thought because Serra is also one of the first people who saw Mara accurately.

When they eventually find each other in the Realm, both will be changed. The question is whether Mara's control/setup instinct and Serra's entry/momentum instinct still point at the same targets — and whether their old reciprocity survives outside the guild frame.

### Marcus
No Realm contact yet. In the eventual dynamic, Marcus and Mara should feel like two brilliant people with overlapping but distinct machinery. Marcus externalizes thought: he explains the model, tests assumptions aloud, and locates the missing mechanism. Mara often internalizes thought: she compresses the situation, waits, and offers the plan or correction only once the relevant shape is clear.

Mara can outthink Marcus without making him look foolish and without the problem needing to be about attention. In group conflict, she may propose the better plan because she has accounted for a constraint Marcus underweighted: timing, incentives, terrain, fatigue, resource cadence, path dependence, or ordinary human error. Marcus's model can be elegant and defensible while Mara's is simply better because it has fewer ways to fail.

Marcus's healthy reaction is interest, amusement, mild annoyance, and appreciation — not threat. He likes the part of Mara that other people experience as inconvenient. Their intellectual intimacy is not that she corrects his feelings; it is that he enjoys having the mechanism sharpened by someone who can actually beat him on the model.

### The Cast Generally
Mara is the person who notices what everyone else is used to not noticing, but she should not be written as only the attention specialist. She is also a strong planner, logician, designer, tactician, and problem-solver.

After Dead Hand, the other five become the group through which her desire for **belonging without absorption** is tested. She does not test them dramatically. What Mara wants from the group is not constant attention. She wants to be included without being simplified.

## Arc

### Book 1 Function

Solo arc — separated from the guild, separated from Serra, developing her power without a framework for what it is. The Realm reveals the transduction capability gradually. She does not have a name for it early. She just notices that she is harder to see when she wants to be, and that people around her sometimes respond to things in ways she can trace back to something she did.

Her arc in Book 1 is about discovering what she is capable of in a setting where her appearance no longer precedes her in the same way — and finding that the power the Realm gave her is intimately connected to the thing she has resented most about her life.

### Core Arc Question

> If you spent your life watching people see the wrong things about you, what do you become when you can control what they see — and what do you lose in the process?

### Starting Point

Reserved, observant, competent, quietly carrying the accumulated fatigue of being seen for the wrong things. Gaming gave her a space where that didn't apply. The Realm gives her something else entirely — a capability she did not know she had, in a world where that capability matters more than anything she previously knew about herself.

### The Court Arc

Mara's solo path takes her into **Veshara** — a status-by-display city-state where regard is currency. Invitations are leverage, public compliments are endorsements, seating is power, clothing is argument, silence is punishment, and legal/social protection follows reputation.

The central antagonist is not merely a “courtier.” He is a socially affluent nobleman embedded in court power: polished, well-regarded, protected by wealth, status, family reputation, and the court's instinct to preserve its own elegance. He is predatory toward women because the culture around him has taught him that refusal can be made socially expensive. His danger is not an official title. His danger is **social immunity**.

He targets Mara romantically/sexually. She refuses. The refusal becomes the ignition. He does not need to attack her openly; he attacks the meaning of her visibility. He makes discomfort look like impropriety, accusation look like jealousy or ambition, refusal look like arrogance, and isolation look like Mara's own social failure.

Mara initially survives by becoming useful in a way the court underestimates as decorative: presentation, display, correspondence, arrangement, spatial/social design, and the management of how events are perceived. The court thinks she is arranging surfaces. She is learning how the room decides what is true.

The arc should include at least one consequence beat where Mara's control of visibility is not clean. She reveals, redirects, or suppresses attention in a way that protects herself or exposes a truth but causes someone else to carry cost. This keeps the lesson sharp: attention creates consequence; it does not automatically create justice.

The climax is a public exposure, but not a clean social victory. Mara does not merely accuse him. She makes the court unable to sustain his preferred interpretation by revealing the pattern of who was isolated, who was discredited before speaking, who benefited from each silence, and how his reputation trained the room to look away.

She wins the truth and loses the room.

The court distrusts her afterward because she proved she can turn its own machinery against one of its protected men. She does not leave because she failed. She leaves because success makes her unsafe to keep.

### The Walking Grove (second arc)

After the court, Mara does not simply move to another city — she crosses the **Walking Grove**: a moving civilization living on and around an ancient **behemoth** so vast that parts of its body are terrain (forests along its back, villages between armored ridges, springs in hollows of its hide, shrines built into horn and plate). A mixed society — humans, elves, beast-speakers, healers, druids, stoneworkers, refugees — in negotiated dependence with a creature they do not control.

The behemoth is sickening, and no one agrees why. The hidden threat survives by a kind of **salience suppression** — a region of the creature everyone has normalized ignoring (animals avoid one grove, children refuse a tunnel, fungi grow wrong, a stream runs warm, conversations about the area keep being forgotten). That makes it the **perfect foil for Specular Salience**: she doesn't detect it with a special scan — she notices the *pattern of collective neglect.* Her climax is making the whole community perceive the danger at once — *not making them believe something false; making them stop ignoring what is already true* — likely her first large-scale reframe. The behemoth's wound / migration / ley-connection then points her toward **Eriadne**.

*(Open — MA-02: nature of the hidden threat — mundane parasite, a Xyloryn seed, or thematically-rhyming-but-unrelated. Do **not** imply an N'hal link. Mark's call.)*

### End Direction

Book 1 leaves Mara somewhere in the middle of understanding what she is. She has used the transduction enough to know it is real and to have felt the projection load start to build. She has not yet lost herself in it — but she has had moments where she was not sure what she actually thought versus what she had been performing for someone else's salience map. The question of identity — what she is when she is not shifting how she is perceived — is becoming urgent.
