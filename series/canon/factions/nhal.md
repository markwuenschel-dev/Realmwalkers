# The N'hal

> **Status:** Canon · working draft
> **Type:** Ontological threat — not a faction, empire, or army
> **Scale:** Cosmic/extra-planar — denatures reality wherever presence advances
> **Role in series:** Hidden existential threat — the condition that makes every empire's answer inadequate; series-level in Book 1
> **Canon confidence:** high (denaturation model + interface failure); medium (trigger, Xyloryn relation)
> **Owns:**
> - N'hal nature, denaturation symptoms, interface degradation, beatability impossibility, Book 1 dosage
> **Does not own:**
> - Nightbound founder's full knowledge (`nightbound.md`); Xyloryn biology (`xyloryn.md`)
> **The spine:** The N'hal are not a rival civilization — they are **ontological predators** that denature reality. Names detach, places stay visible but stop being locatable, oaths go static, gods and interfaces lose coherence. Reality horror, not monster invasion. Every other power argues what reality should become; the N'hal erase the premise that reality can stay coherent enough to become anything.

## One-Sentence Identity

The locked in-world name for the ontological threat — the force that unmakes meaning, category, and locatability before it unmakes bodies.

## Core Principle

> Reality can lose coherence.

Coherence is not guaranteed — and when it fails, **every empire's supreme truth becomes irrelevant** at once.

## What They Are Right About

- Not applicable in moral terms — they are not an argument but a **condition**.
- They expose that structure-dependent power assumes a stable legible layer — that assumption is false near them.

## Where They Go Wrong

- Not an empire — "where they go wrong" is a category error. The horror is that **there is no bargain**.

## Scale and Reach

- Extra-planar / episodic advance on a gradual schedule — reasons not fully grasped.
- Not summoned by a single avoidable act; slowness is the only reason preparation is possible.
- Realm appears in their path or has "registered" — **working hypothesis**, not locked fact.

## Aesthetic / Texture

- Reality horror — language itself may feel unstable near presence (`../style/voice_guide.md`).
- Places wrong, names slipping, records surviving without meaning.
- No army aesthetic — **wrongness**.

## Power Model

- Denature: names detach, places unlocatable, oaths static, gods/spells/interfaces lose coherence, categories fail, identity unstable.
- Near N'hal: **legible layer fails first** — for implant bearers, interface degrades (skills, levels, bars, notifications misread or go dark).
- Refined structure-dependent power fails; instinct and substrate-underneath survive.
- Beatable in principle if every cosmic power's apex beings unified — **no one will in time**.

## Society / Internal Structure

- None — not a society. Not a swarm, army, undead host, demon court, or chaos cult.

## Relationship to the Realm

- Hidden threat — Xyloryn are the *known* apocalypse; N'hal are what almost nobody understands.
- Cast escalation (resurrection, interface ignition, reality-bending) may be quiet trigger condition — series architecture; not on-page Book 1.

## Relationship to the Six

- Not direct Book 1 antagonists — series-level threat only in Book 1 (`WF-006` Book 2+ on-page).
- Six as keys assembled partly because structure-dependent civilization fails against N'hal — Nightbound logic.
- Marcus's lesson *the interface is not the world* is survival skill against denaturation.

## Relationship to the N'hal

- N/A — this file defines the threat.

## Relationship to Other Powers

- **Nightbound:** built around evidentiary N'hal knowledge; founder understands trigger better than expected.
- **Xyloryn:** separate; "failed immune response" theory **not confirmed**.
- **Endless Reach:** chaos offers desire; N'hal remove frame for desire to matter.
- All empires prepare for wrong war if they only refine in-paradigm strength.

## Story Function

- Hidden second layer of threat structure — keeps series from being only Xyloryn war.
- Justifies Nightbound coercion without making them correct morally.
- Interface-darkening set piece when they eventually appear — `../litrpg_system/core_rules.md`.

## What They Are Not

- Not an empire, swarm, army, undead, chaos, demons, or the Xyloryn.
- Not invulnerable — one was killed/trapped/dormant somehow (Nightbound hope).
- Not on-page Book 1 antagonists.

## Canon Locks

- **LOCK:** In-world name = **N'hal** (never Eldrazi, Zalgoran, Zalgorin as canon terms).
- **LOCK:** Ontological predators — denature reality, not conquer it.
- **LOCK:** Interface degrades near them; cannot render N'hal accurately (`core_rules.md`).
- **LOCK:** Distinct from Chaos/Endless Reach and from Xyloryn.
- **LOCK:** Book 1 = series-level only, not on-page.
- **LOCK:** Beatable only by impossible apex unity — gap Nightbound exploits.
- **LOCK:** Advance on gradual episodic schedule; not simple summon-avoidance plot.
