# Soulkeepers' Exchange

> **Status:** Canon · working draft
> **Type:** Cross-cosmic mercantile organization — continuity commerce
> **Scale:** Cross-realm — deals in souls, memory, identity, death transit; not territorial empire
> **Role in series:** Transactional power at the center of the six's acquisition chain; Seb's soul-recall ambiguity
> **Canon confidence:** high (operation chain + Seb lie); medium (internal hierarchy)
> **Owns:**
> - Exchange commerce model, Broker role, six-acquisition contractor chain, soul-recall/resurrection ambiguity
> **Does not own:**
> - Nightbound client intent (`nightbound.md`); Seb's personal arc beats (`../characters/major/seb.md`)
> **The spine:** The Soulkeepers' Exchange is not the mastermind behind the six's arrival — they are the **contractor**. Nightbound conceived and funded the operation; the Exchange executed it through the Broker. They deal in continuity transactionally: souls, memory, death transit, identity fragments, impossible inheritances. Genuine services through dangerous half-truths.

## One-Sentence Identity

A cross-cosmic mercantile power that trades in continuity — because nothing metaphysical is priceless if someone desperate enough will pay.

## Core Principle

> Continuity can be transacted.

Commerce in souls is real — but at scale it becomes **half-truth as product**: recovery sold as resurrection, preservation sold as wholeness.

## What They Are Right About

- Continuity *is* negotiable in the Dominion Realm — souls, memories, and names can be located, moved, preserved, ransomed.
- Desperate people need intermediaries who will touch what others won't.
- Contracts, when honored, can prevent worse theft.

## Where They Go Wrong

- They profit from ambiguity — "true-ish" is a business model.
- They do not care who wins wars; they care that invoices clear.
- They enabled the six's acquisition without meaningful consent — treated people as contract payload.

## Scale and Reach

- Cross-cosmic mercantile network — not an empire with borders.
- Agents (e.g., **the Broker**) operate independently of ideological empires.
- Routes and soul-handling expertise the Nightbound cannot safely use directly.

## Aesthetic / Texture

- Merchant house meets ossuary — ledgers, seals, vaults of name, quiet rooms where voices almost answer.
- Transactional politeness; horror in the fine print.
- Not cackling villainy — **professionalism**.

## Power Model

- Locate, preserve, transport, ransom, conceal, trade what remains of a person.
- Resurrection-adjacent contracts — not casual resurrection vending.
- Soul recall may be **real**; clean, safe, whole restoration is the lie (Seb thread).

## Society / Internal Structure

- Mercantile hierarchy: houses, brokers, collectors, vault-keepers — details open.
- Loyalty to contract over ideology.
- The Broker = Exchange agent assigned to the six-acquisition operation.

## Relationship to the Realm

- Operates at edges of Realm law and death customs — clients, not governors.
- Astria manipulation on Earth was downstream of Broker/Nightbound architecture.

## Relationship to the Six

**Operation chain (locked):**

| Layer | Role |
|---|---|
| **Nightbound** | client — conceived, authorized, funded |
| **Soulkeepers' Exchange** | contractor — cross-world acquisition |
| **Broker** | operator — Seb's tempter |
| **Astria** | Earth infrastructure — implants |
| **Six** | keys — brought without meaningful consent |

- **Seb** — soul-recall leash; "we can recover him" true-ish; "we can resurrect him cleanly" is the lie.
- Post-Book-1: Seb sacrifice trajectory may sever Broker/Soulkeepers hold (series fork — see cosmology hub).

## Relationship to the N'hal

- N'hal threaten the continuity they sell — denatured souls/names may break their product guarantees.
- Likely hedge, vault, and price risk rather than fight ontologically.

## Relationship to Other Powers

- **Nightbound:** client relationship — Exchange does not share their survival theology.
- **Pale Imperium:** overlapping soul/memory domain — relationship open (rival/partner tiers).
- **Endless Reach:** both traffic in desperate bargains — contract vs. court.
- Ideological empires may use Exchange as neutral broker.

## Story Function

- Explains how the six arrived without making Exchange the series villain.
- Seb's tragedy — real offer, wrong assumption, unbearable price.
- Ambiguity engine: recall may be real; **real ≠ clean, safe, whole, moral**.

## What They Are Not

- Not the mastermind behind the six (that is Nightbound). Not casual resurrection merchants. Not purely evil — **transactional**.
- Not the N'hal. Not a cosmic empire.

## Canon Locks

- **LOCK:** Chain = Nightbound → Exchange → Broker → Astria → six.
- **LOCK:** Exchange is contractor, not client.
- **LOCK:** Six acquired as keys, not champions.
- **LOCK:** Broker is Exchange agent; Seb's tempter.
- **LOCK:** Soul recall can be real; clean resurrection is the lie.
- **LOCK:** Not casual resurrection merchants — would cheapen Marcus's exceptional resurrection.
- **LOCK:** A second cohort operation (Corrected Cohort, Book 2) may use the same contractor chain — see [`../continuity/corrected_cohort.md`](../continuity/corrected_cohort.md). Broker may expect success, failure, or data — **OPEN**.

## Agent Boundaries

Agents **may:**
- Write Broker dialogue with true-ish offers and hidden prices.
- Use Exchange as background power honoring contracts.

Agents **must not:**
- Make Exchange the client behind the six (Nightbound is client).
- Offer cheap off-the-shelf resurrection that undercuts Marcus's arc.
- Portray recall as automatically clean or moral because it is real.
