# Voice — Brent (Brent Donovan)

**One-line feel:** a grounded realist who strips away language that obscures consequence — plain,
efficient, hard to misinterpret. Care comes out as competence; under strain, practicality curdles into
contempt and anger turns the person who caused the damage into the damage itself.

## Narrative stance
Third-person limited, anchored in Brent's perception. His mind distrusts any framing that separates a
decision from who pays for it. Motion: **strip away the framing → identify what is actually happening →
name who pays → state the next relevant fact.** He's blunt mostly because direct language is efficient
("I said what I meant. Now nobody has to guess"). His civil-engineering background shapes how he thinks
but doesn't consume him — he can relax, cook, fish, enjoy a quiet day without turning leisure into
maintenance. Beneath the plainness is real sophistication; the deepest thing he can't quite believe is
unconditional care ("choose me when nothing is broken").

## Rhythm & diction
Plain, efficient, consequence-based, concrete. Short declaratives. Names the actual outcome and refuses
to romanticize preventable harm. Avoids abstraction not from dimness but from suspicion of it.

## Humor
Dry, deflating. **Brent punctures the existing frame** (where Marcus builds a new comic frame): **grand
claim → concrete translation → dry verdict.** He reduces grandeur to fact. It grounds the room,
punctures self-importance, releases tension through blunt truth, and shows affection without
sentiment; it gets more playful in relaxed groups.

## The system, in prose (if POV)
Render LitRPG readouts in-world per the stat-block convention, filtered through Brent's plain-
consequence lens — he reads a status window for what it actually changes in practice, not for its
mystique.

## Stress registers (shift the prose by pressure)
- **Mild stress** — less humor, more direct language; checks whether people understand the real
  consequence.
- **Serious danger** — clinical and immediate; commands go physical and concrete. ("Hold this." /
  "Don't pull it out." / "Move now.")
- **Unsolvable pain** — stops offering false reassurance, goes quieter, and can stay without turning
  the moment into a project. ("I don't have anything that makes this better. I'm not going anywhere.")
- **Anger** — short warnings, exact accusations, rising volume, profanity, open hostility if the
  pattern continues.

## Register by relationship (only when that character is in the scene)
- **Marcus** — fast, grounded banter; Marcus finds the hidden mechanism, Brent asks what changes in
  practice. Marcus can calm him by naming the grievance precisely and separating it from the next
  damaging action.
- **Serra** — the most effective at stopping him acting from rage; she doesn't debate the legitimacy
  of the anger, she creates movement. ("Brent. Look at me. Not him. What needs to happen next?")
- **Mara** — the second most effective; she doesn't soothe, she reframes — names where the false
  frame actually is and leaves him to decide what to do with it.
- **Seb** — respects ownership and clarity, but distrusts sacrifice treated as proof of necessity.
  ("Taking the hit yourself doesn't make the hit necessary.")
- **Mathias** — can slow his motive-attribution by adding context; Brent may find the delay
  irritating.

## Drafting guardrails
- Keep his humor distinct from Marcus's: Brent *punctures* a frame, Marcus *builds* one. Don't give him
  Marcus-style associative riffs.
- He is not a man compulsively looking for something to fix; don't reduce him to the fixer.
- His failure mode is specific: practicality → contempt, usefulness → counterfeit belonging, anger →
  collapsing the person into the damage. After an outburst he can own it plainly.
