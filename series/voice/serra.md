# Voice — Serra

**One-line feel:** warm, socially responsive, magnetic — she speaks from the conclusion her intuition
already reached, not from analysis. Selectively permeable: genuine warmth without granting interior
access. Decisive, and under strain she hears clarification as negotiation and closes through *access*,
not volume.

## Narrative stance
Third-person limited, anchored entirely in Serra's perception. She reads people and rooms fast and
usually doesn't show the chain — the narration arrives at the read and moves from it rather than
reasoning it out on the page. Her motion: **notice the person → establish ease → give specific
attention → state or imply direction → move.** Relational, not conceptual: she thinks in names,
remembered details, tailored callbacks, who's included and who's drifting. Her warmth is genuine —
never write her as secretly cold, or as warmth deployed for strategy.

Her best traits run past their limits under strain — warmth that implies more than she meant,
intuition that assumes others should read her limits as fast as she reads theirs, decisiveness that
becomes relational amputation. Keep the warm voice and the final voice recognizably the same woman.

## Rhythm & diction
Easy to enter, person-specific, physically expressive; she sounds like company, not analysis, even
when she has read the situation exactly. Names and direct address. When she closes, the prose goes
brief, controlled, and contained — the severity lives in what's withheld, not in raised volume.

## Humor
Specific to the person, affectionate or lightly challenging, social rather than abstract. It creates
momentum rather than stopping the scene for a punchline, and it's delivered with enough confidence
that the other person feels invited to play back. Not universally playful — banter rises with comfort.

## The system, in prose
Her ability and any system readouts surface in-world and sparingly, filtered through a decisive,
instinct-first perception — she registers them as *what to do next*, not as data to analyze. Never a
bare stat sheet.

## Stress registers (shift the prose by pressure)
- **Mild stress** — task-oriented, keeps the mood moving, light humor and practical care; discloses
  just enough to explain her behavior, not enough to ask for support.
- **Serious danger** — quiet, short, decisive; the social cushions drop; direct movement
  instructions. ("Behind me." / "Not that door." / "I know. We're still going.")
- **Emotional ambiguity** — less expansive; leans on behavior over explicit definition; assumes the
  other person has already noticed the change.
- **Hard boundary** — controlled and procedural; removes openings; treats continued clarification as
  attempted negotiation; may keep private care while ending visible access.
- **Grief or private need** — stays functional, discloses late, gives the facts after she's already
  decided what to do; accepts care more easily when it doesn't require her to be uncertain in front
  of someone.

## Register by relationship (only when that character is in the scene)
- **Marcus** — easy rhythm, tailored teasing, strong mutual attention, more subtext than either
  admits. She gives genuine warmth; he assigns future meaning to its repetition. He asks for
  definitions; she hears pressure. She'll answer a narrower question truthfully while refusing the
  larger emotional frame.
- **Mara** — compressed tactical shorthand, trust expressed through timing rather than reassurance.
  ("Left?" / "Not yet." / "Now?" / "Now.")
- **Seb** — respects decisive leadership while watching for when collective language stops matching
  collective consent; challenges with a clean practical question, not a speech.
- **Brent** — plain language; more willing to accept care when it arrives as competent action than as
  emotional excavation.
- **Strangers** — warm enough to lower tension, attentive without opening herself; quickly clocks
  who's excluded, posturing, or trying to control the room.

## Drafting guardrails
- Don't write her as secretly cold, and don't treat her warmth as manipulation.
- Don't treat disclosure or attention as automatic evidence of romantic or relational commitment.
- Don't make every refusal perfectly explicit from the start — her indirectness is a real blind spot.
- Don't make final closure melodramatic; the severity is in reduced access, not raised volume.
- Don't assume strong eye contact, playfulness, or personal detail means she's internally changed the
  relationship's category.
- Don't excuse Marcus crossing a stated boundary; their reciprocal misread is tragedy, not permission.
- Let her remain capable of valuing someone after deciding contact can't continue.
