Chapter 1  
June 27th, 2035  
Charlotte, NC

Breaking news flashed across the screen. Astria, a biotech startup, was about to unveil their groundbreaking quantum computer, a creation anticipated to revolutionize the computing industry and, in their words, 'alter life as we know it.' Chad absorbed the report with only half his attention.

"Yeah, I'm sure it'll change life as we know it," I deadpanned, channeling my best Captain Obvious vibes as I engaged in a heated debate with my dusty mop. My name is Chad—no, not the 'Chad' that's become a meme, flexing his digital biceps in the basement; this Chad's a biotech researcher clocking in at the Astria facility, the very name lighting up your TV screen and igniting your imagination.

At thirty-one orbits around the sun, I find myself riding solo on this spaceship of life. No wedding invites, no tiny minions calling me 'Dad,' and no fur babies sharing my abode. You see, I'm married to the game—the video game, that is. Forged in the crucible of pixels and the sweet symphony of button mashing, I aspire to don the proverbial pro-gamer cape and conquer virtual realms with the finesse of a Jedi master.

But hey, let's steer away from my digital escapades and dig into the nitty-gritty of the biotech battleground. Being a scientist isn't all lab coats and Eureka moments; it's about wielding the double-edged sword of knowledge. Picture this: a mix of Hogwarts without the magic wands, Stark Industries sans the Iron Man suits, and a hint of Willy Wonka's factory minus the everlasting gobstoppers. We toil in the vats of innovation, brewing concoctions that can make or break the world as we know it. Contrary to popular belief, the life of a scientist isn't all late-night Erlenmeyer flask parties. Sure, there's schooling, where we delve so deep into the science abyss that even the Kraken gets envious. Yet, it's not just textbooks and test tubes—there's a method to our madness. Networking, presentations, and mingling with the suits in high-rise boardrooms; we're not just geeks with pocket protectors, but savvy savants straddling the bridge between innovation and marketability.

Now, let's hit warp speed into the realm of computer programmers. We're the digital sorcerers, conjuring spells with code to make machines dance to our techno-tunes. We're the Gandalfs, yelling 'You shall not pass\!' to bugs and glitches in our quest for a seamless user experience. Software, the magic wand of the 21st century, allows us to summon the dragons of efficiency, making computers zip through tasks faster than the Flash on a caffeine bender.

So here I am, juggling virtual realms and real-life labs, straddling dimensions like a literary hero caught in an interdimensional plot twist. The story unfolds, and in this narrative, the hero ain't wearing tights and a cape. Nah, he's got a mouse and a keyboard, ready to slay dragons of inefficiency and unlock the treasure chest of progress. Stay tuned for the next episode in the Chronicles of Chad.

We use our skills to build artificial intelligence systems—the kind of thing that makes robots capable of thinking for themselves instead of following pre-programmed instructions. Artificial Intelligence is a vast market these days, especially when it comes to robotics. This is where my job starts: developing AI for healthcare applications. Our goal is simple \- we want to develop machines that can help doctors diagnose patients by analyzing data and presenting information in ways humans understand better than any human doctor ever could. Of course, since we’re dealing with patient privacy laws, this technology won’t be available for general release anytime soon, but every step towards getting there helps us refine our methods further. 

So far, we’ve developed programs that can analyze a patient’s chart – complete with exams, labs, and medical images – and present results to physicians within minutes rather than hours. Imagine how helpful this would be in emergencies, an emergency department having access to a machine that could provide instant feedback on the best viable treatment options for whatever disease a patient may have presented with. Or what if a robot could check for cancer cells without needing to expose a patient to invasive biopsy procedures or radiation? All of this is still a long way off, but thanks to people like me, it will become a reality someday. Okay, sorry, I digress. The reporters were not finished.

“This new device has been in development for years by our scientists, who have finally managed to create an advanced form of artificial intelligence that can process more information faster than any human brain could ever hope.”

“And it uses quantum computing too\! Not that any of us knows what that means, but it sounds like a true R\&D marvel of human engineering and ingenuity\!” added another female reporter. Picture this: If traditional computing is like navigating with a map, quantum computing is hitching a ride on Doc Brown's DeLorean from 'Back to the Future'—swirling through time and space, covering every possible route at once," chimed in another reporter with a hint of awe. Quantum computing, you ask? I'll break it down for you like Tony Stark explaining a new suit upgrade.

Alright, imagine your regular computer as Sherlock Holmes, solving mysteries with simple 'yes' or 'no' answers, represented by 0s and 1s. Now, Sherlock gets an upgrade—a quantum hat, if you will. This hat (quantum bits or qubits) can ponder multiple solutions at once, like Sherlock considering multiple suspects simultaneously. It's a game-changer, allowing us to solve mysteries—or in this case, complex calculations—in a blink.

But what powers this quantum hat? Enter stage left, the superstar of the quantum world: superconductors, starring lead, aluminum and mercury. Think of them as the Quicksilver of the computing world—speedsters who dash through the information highway without breaking a sweat. These superconductors help transfer data at breakneck speeds, making the Flash look like he's napping. And when you mix these qubits and super-speed info highways, you get quantum computing—faster, more powerful, and more secure than a Swiss vault. Hackers? They've got a better chance of sneaking into Fort Knox during an alien invasion than cracking this quantum fortress. It's like we've upgraded from a padlock to Iron Man's suit in the cybersecurity department.

 “We have an exclusive interview with the President and CEO of Astria coming right up at the top of the hour,” announced the news anchor. Then, we have…” the voice trailed off as I no longer paid attention to it.

Life had taken on the hue of a monotonous TV show stuck in reruns. My latest brainchild, once the hero of my creative saga, had lost its sparkle. The initial excitement now felt like comparing a lightsaber duel to a chess match—you still have to think, but the flash and sizzle were sorely lacking. I'd been wrestling with this project for what felt like a geological era. Progress was happening, but at a pace that made a snail look like a jet fighter.

And don't get me started on the symphony of chaos blaring from every screen and speaker—endless political rumblings and the freshest celebrity scandals taking center stage. It seemed the world was auditioning for a reality show called "Divided We Stand, United We Facepalm." People were afraid of each other; they feared the future and its unknown possibilities. Most importantly, people were losing sight of the importance of humanity itself. With all of the advances in science, medicine, and biotechnology, society was forgetting that it needed to live in harmony with nature – not conquer her. 

In this theater of the absurd, I played my part by avoiding the political circus—a juggling act more challenging than trying to keep up with the plot twists in a Tarantino film. Yet, in my line of biotech duty, one often finds themselves rubbing elbows with politicians. It's a bit like dealing with dragons to protect your gold—the gold being favorable regulations and the dragons being the power players. The grim reality? Everyone's hustling, eager to swipe the crown or at least a shiny trinket. It's a dirty game, and the spectators wouldn't mind seeing you slip on a banana peel mid-performance.

But alas, here I was, juggling life's lemons and trying not to drop any on my toes. . That said, I needed my job just like everyone else did; however, what little joy remained after working long hours every day was rapidly draining out of life. And as I resumed my cleaning duties, the sigh that escaped echoed the sentiment—sometimes life felt like a movie where the plot was stuck on pause. As I begrudgingly resumed my cleaning responsibilities, a weary sigh escaped my lips. My eyes flitted towards the clock, its hands crawling towards the end of my shift—just a few more hours left until I could escape this monotonous existence.

Charlotte, NC

The next morning, I jolted awake as my alarm blared. Sleep had been a restless affair, haunted by uncertainty. The clandestine nature of it all, the hacking into our private scrim, the vague promises of transportation to a mysterious research site – it was all a web of intrigue. They hadn't even bothered to mention what to pack for this expedition. How long would we be gone? Hours? Days? I groaned, realizing that this was hardly a soothing way to greet the day.

Seeking solace, I settled onto my rug, adopting a cross-legged posture. Meditation and controlled breathing became my refuge against the rising tide of anxiety. Simplicity was my mantra; inhale for three counts, exhale for three counts, repeat. For such a fundamental practice, it had a remarkable ability to work its magic. As I listened to the measured rhythm of my heart, my anxiety began to ebb away. I gazed at the ceiling, my eyes tranquil, and within thirty minutes, I felt composed enough to face the day.

The company vehicle finally arrived, a torturous hour and a half later. My nerves had left me too unsettled to eat, fearing it might lead to nausea later on. So, I busied myself in a futile attempt to distract from the gnawing uncertainty until their arrival. The inevitable knock echoed through my apartment. When I swung open the door, a man and a woman greeted me with unsettling smiles.

Oddly, they weren't attired in security gear but rather looked like ordinary people. She wore a blue hoodie and jeans, while he sported a plain t-shirt beneath a leather jacket. Selia exuded a more amiable demeanor, with traces of genuine emotion flickering across her face. Her smirk and crinkled eyes softened her otherwise intimidating presence. She probably saw through my apprehension, considering I had no inkling of what awaited me in the hours to come. Yet, despite her enigmatic energy, it radiated warmth, even if I wouldn't mistake her for an optimist. It was an expression that could easily be misconstrued as sarcastic, and she kept her gaze fixed on me as we shook hands.

Then… Then I died when the Archdemon fulfilled its promise…* That was a sobering thought, but if I died, then is this the afterlife? Is it heaven or hell that awaits me now? Or maybe both at once\! If so, which one do you think would be more fun?\! The angelic realm has always been too boring for me.  
A voice came into my head: "You are not dead." It sounded like someone whispering through a telephone line—the sound quality wasn't very good. I looked around. I don't even know if I'm in the same place as I was before I died, but it looked like there were valleys and mountains close by. Maybe they're just illusions, though. There's no way something could survive after being destroyed by a god… Right? *Wait, why am I thinking about things that have nothing to do with me right now\!? There are more important things I need to deal with, like food and shelter.*  
The same voice came into my head again: "Your body has been resurrected because you have a unique combination of demonic, angelic, and chaotic influences on your soul.”  
I ignored the voice again, but this time, I looked at the sky, but no one or *thing* was there. I thought of a weird idea… if I’m in a new place, does that mean my cybernetic implants are working properly? Is the AI talking to me? I thought half concernedly and half excitedly.  
For the third time, the voice came into my head: “No idiot, an inanimate machine is not spontaneously talking to you.”  
"Oh," I said, disappointed, "Then who are you?"  
There was silence.  
Several seconds passed before it responded.  
“I am a celestial being.”  
Celestial being? What kind of name is that, anyway? Another pause followed, during which I heard a strange clicking noise coming from somewhere nearby. After another second or two, the voice spoke again: "I’m right behind you."   
My heart jumped inside my chest. My first instinct was to run away, but I knew that wouldn't accomplish anything. I did not know where I was, and this *thing* found me in the middle of nowhere. So instead, I turned slowly toward where I thought the sound was originating from. A ball of light, the size of a softball, with two tiny wings, floated a few feet from my head. It didn’t have eyes or even a face. It looked like a wisp, but solid enough for me to touch with my hand.   
"What do you want?" I asked warily.  
"Well," the voice replied, sounding almost amused, “I want nothing.” It paused briefly, waiting for me to respond, but I remained silent. “You helped me escape the Nether, a place between life and the Void. My consciousness was trapped there after my physical form was destroyed. I sensed the triumvirate energies within you reincarnating your body, and just added my consciousness to you.”  
I didn't really understand, but I decided not to press further. Instead, I tried to get some information out of this celestial being.  
“Are you going to try to take over my mind?” I shuddered at the memory of the archdemon trying to possess my body and torturing my mind.  
There was another long pause before it replied.  
"No."  
"Good. I want nothing to do with demons," I muttered to myself. I said it simply for clarity's sake. No matter what they offered, I would never allow any demon to have access to me.  
Another long pause passed before the voice spoke again.  
"So that explains your…" Its voice trailed off again for a moment, then it said, "It doesn't matter. You're too weak."  
"What do you mean, weak?" I demanded to know.  
"Look," the voice explained. “You don’t even have a name.” It hovered closer to me, and I could see that it had no nose, mouth, or ears, and that its tiny wings were wobbling a bit. It was obviously exhausted after what happened inside the Nether.   
“What do you mean I don’t have a name?\! My name is–”  
"Never say your true name," the voice interrupted, annoyed.  
"Why not?" I asked.  
The wisp paused for several more seconds, as if thinking about what to say, then answered in a low, growling voice, "Because that is how enemies exert total control over you. Look at your summary.”   
“How do I do that?” I said, confused.  
This time, the wisp answered quickly, “You simply will it. Your AI enhanced quantum processors will do the rest.”  
I thought for a moment and remembered the box of information I saw when I had originally woken up.

“Who didn’t screw you over?” The wisp asked.  
I knew who it was, the archangel and archdemon, but I didn’t know if I wanted to let this unknown entity in on that fact. There was still much I didn’t understand about this game. So I hid it.  
"I don’t know their names, nor where they came from," I said truthfully.  
“I know you know more than you’re saying,” the wisp said. “I’m a celestial entity, remember? I’ve been alive for eons. There are certain things I can read from you.”  
"Why are you helping me now, anyway?" I asked.  
“Because you gave me a way out of the Nether. And… you are interesting.” The wisp stopped and seemed to think, as if trying to decide what to say next. Finally, it answered, “I have been drained from existing in the Nether for so long, and I need a host to survive. Even this light wisp form is burdening me.”  
My heart sank. The only reason I was even speaking with this thing was because it was giving me information, and now it's going to possess me like that archdemon? That's horrible\!  
“No, I don’t mean to possess you,” the wisp said, as if reading my thoughts.  
I breathed a sigh of relief.  
The wisp continued, "What I need is an anchor to this world by inhabiting you. I can feel the machine core in your head. It will allow me to sustain myself, while also allowing me to communicate with you.”  
I totally forgot about the AI modules connected to the quantum DNA interface until the mention of “the core”. I was curious enough to ask, "How do you know it's there? Does it give off an energy signature or something?"  
"Yes," the wisp answered simply.  
“So you want to stay inside me, take some of my energy to survive, all to… watch me? And what do I get out of it?” I asked curiously, but then added, “I don’t want some annoying voice in my head."   
"It would surprise you at how often it is a human’s curiosity that drives a person," the wisp replied. "But it's not about power. I simply wish to make a deal with you, that you promise to protect me and keep me alive while I offer you knowledge that no one else can give you.”  
I pondered its words. Was it really asking for protection or just saying that?   
After another moment of hesitation, I thought, *What the hell?* *If this thing has been alive for thousands of years, I might as well learn all the secrets I can. Plus, if it offers me knowledge and tries to possess me, I'll end up with an enormous advantage against others.*  
"Fine. I accept your terms."  
The wisp seemed pleased. After a moment, it said, “You can call me Ayla. Now, try to relax. This shouldn’t hurt at all.”  
“*Shouldn’t*?” I protested. “Wait. Maybe I sh–”  
Ayla's light expanded into a bright sphere above my head, engulfing me in its brilliance. My eyes nearly shut out of instinct, but then something compelled them to open again. The wisp's light swirled around me like water, seeming to distort my vision in places. But more than that, I felt warmth… A stream of energy flowing into my body from some unseen source. The light engulfed me entirely, and suddenly, I heard a quiet voice in my head. It sounded like my own thoughts.  
"It seems like I can't access your memories. But I can see your status summary… Ah. You DO see these notifications waiting for you, right? Your AI already processed them for you.” The voice chided me as if I was a child.  
"What? Where are you?" I said.  
"Hm," the voice replied. "I've attached to your cybernetic implants in your head."  
"That makes little sense\!" I shouted in protest. My mind is boggled.  
“Hey, listen.” The voice chirped. “Calm down, I’ve moved on. I’m looking at your status summary now. Remember, you need a name. If you want to survive here, then you will need an alias for others to identify you. Remember, choosing your real name will allow others to exert control over you.”  
My mind snapped to the table of attributes, recalling that my name had question marks beside it.  
"What's wrong?" Ayla asked.  
"My name is… I don't know," I said, stumped. My mind started working furiously. I needed a new name. If I gave up my real one, I feel like I wouldn't be me anymore. But I also don’t want to be controlled. *What should I call myself?* I remember my life on Earth, being a progamer working at *Astria,* but I don’t know how I’m *here.* I want my name to symbolize my newly born person.  
"I think I'll call myself," I said at last, “Phoenix.”   
The name had been good in games, and I could see how it would work here. It was an immortal bird who revived out of his own ashes. This seemed perfect. Excitedly, I willed my status page back into view. It was still awkward to do it, but easier after the first time.
