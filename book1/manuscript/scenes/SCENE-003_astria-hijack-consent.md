# Chapter 1 — Scene 3
For half a second after the wind hit, comms stopped being language.
404’s channel blew open. Thirty voices tried to solve the same impossible thing at once.
Who triggered boss?
Back up, back up—
Timer’s wrong—
Cyclone already?
Brent’s down—
I can’t hear Seb—
Logout just flickered—
What the hell is that?
The words stacked until they stopped being words.
I pushed myself off the cracked stair and found Brent through the storm. He was still in the open, still on one knee, still at the edge of the lane Serra had carved through us.
BRENT DONOVAN: 6%
The number dropped while I watched. Not from a blade. From the wind.
“Brent!” I shouted.
He lifted one hand. Not a wave. Not an answer. Just proof he could still move.
I had one mobility skill left. Not there and back. One way.
I used it anyway.
Predator drove off the broken stair in a red-black burst. The dash cut low across the lane, claws scraping sparks from the stone where the wind shoved me sideways. It took two seconds to reach him. It felt longer because the whole arena had started moving in pieces: banners snapping loose, gravel sliding toward the left-of-center basin, chains lifting from the floor as if the air had found hooks in them.
I hit the ground beside Brent and caught him before the next gust folded him flat.
“Spam self-heals,” I said. “Anything you have.”
He laughed once, and it came out wrong. “Great plan. Wish I’d thought of having cooldowns.”
“Brent.”
“I have scraps.”
“Use scraps.”
A weak white shield snapped over his body first, then stretched enough to catch part of my shoulder when I hauled him up. Not a barrier. Not real cover. A personal mitigation bubble forced to pretend it was shelter.
The wind hit it and hissed.
I got Brent’s arm over my shoulder. “Walk.”
“I am currently exploring several exciting alternatives to walking.”
“Pick walking.”
We moved.
Every step cost. The wind did not feel like air anymore. It felt like sharp threads drawn tight across the lane, needling through armor seams, catching under plates, dragging across exposed skin the game should not have been letting feel that clearly.
MARCUS VYE: 39%
Five seconds. Another cut of pressure.
MARCUS VYE: 38%
To my right, Serra and Mara were still in the open. Mara’s ice path had not collapsed yet. The pale line still curled behind Serra toward the fallen arch, slick and bright, but the storm was eating at it from the edges. Mara reinforced it with both hands, higher ridges rising along the sides, turning the path into a narrow chute.
Debris hit the first ridge and exploded into white cracks.
Serra looked back at her. “Can you still get us out?”
Mara drove one palm down and forced the ice higher. Another chunk of broken stone slammed into it. The ridge held. Barely.
“Not like this,” Mara said.
The next gust shoved Brent and me sideways. I dug Predator claws into the stone and dragged him the last few feet toward a broken corner of ruined masonry, two walls still standing where part of the basilica had collapsed into itself. No roof. Not safe. But it gave us something between Brent and the worst of the wind.
Seb reached us at the same time.
He came in hard from the left, Warlord armor already lit with a defensive aura, red-black command lines bracing around his shoulders and spreading outward. The buff settled over me like extra weight. Not force. Not a leash. Just a layer of protection where there had been none.
He took position in front of Brent.
“Behind me,” Seb said.
Brent slid down against the broken wall. “I love when people say that like I have options.”
Seb raised his weapon into the wind and activated a guard skill. The aura widened. For three breaths, the corner held.
Mathias dropped from the causeway above us.
He did not land cleanly. The wind caught him halfway down, shoved him into a hanging chain, and spun him off-angle. He caught the edge of a cracked support with one hand, kicked off it, and hit the ground near us in a low skid.
His bow was still in his hand. His eyes were already on the exits.
“Outer stairs are gone,” he said.
Seb did not turn. “Gone how?”
“Blocked by wind and debris. Left bridge is worse. Basin edge is suicide.” Mathias looked past us, toward the routes that should have been usable. “We can see the exits. We can’t cross to them.”
The ruined corner shook.
Something huge moved in the basin.
The Storm Warden was rising left of center, half-hidden behind the growing funnel. Brass ribs unfolded from its chest. Roots tore out of the stone around its shoulders. The dark core inside it pulsed once, and the tornado widened again.
MARCUS VYE: 36%
Then the Astria seal opened over the Warden’s core.
Clean blue-white geometry nested itself inside the brass ribs. No dust touched it. No wind bent it. The mark rotated once, perfectly smooth, while the boss tore pieces of the arena into the air around it.
A panel opened in my vision.
White. Blue ring. Astria.
A man’s voice entered the instance. Calm. Male. Close.
“Participants. Astria is initiating a controlled emergency transition for eligible candidates. Non-candidate players may log out at any time. For candidates, the logout function is under temporary suspension. Candidate transition authorizes transfer into the neuroquantum implant receiving framework upon affirmative consent through the interface. That consent is optional and may be declined.”
No name. No face. No urgency.
He sounded like the storm had arrived on a schedule.
A second panel replaced the first.
ASTRIA NEUROQUANTUM IMPLANT PROTOCOL
Eligible candidate detected.
Emergency transition authorizes:
— transfer to implant receiving framework
— neural interface preparation
— biometric continuity preservation
— post-transition onboarding
DO YOU CONSENT?
YES / NO
“Do not touch anything,” Seb said.
His voice cut through guild channel and open air at the same time.
I tried logout. The icon responded for half a second. Then grayed out.
LOGOUT UNAVAILABLE
CANDIDATE STATUS: PENDING
“Mine’s locked,” I said.
“Same,” Seb said.
Brent looked up from under the weak shimmer of his shield. “Same, and I would like to register that as rude.”
Mathias’s eyes flicked once. “Same.”
Behind us, Kip’s voice cut through the channel.
“Mine works.”
He sounded ashamed.
Across the arena, players started vanishing.
One of ours disappeared near the broken bridge in the normal blue motes of logout. Then another from Kip’s pressure lane. Then two Dead Hand frontliners near the canal. The effect was ordinary. Safe. Familiar.
A body stood there. Then light. Then nothing.
The system worked. Just not for us.
More players left. The battlefield thinned in uneven flashes, green and red markers dropping from my HUD. Dead Hand’s leader vanished. One of our brawlers. A caster. A defender limping behind a cracked pillar.
Kip stayed.
On Dead Hand’s side, the empty space gathered around two figures.
Serra. Mara.
On ours, it gathered around four.
Me. Seb. Brent. Mathias.
The tornado widened again.
The corner wall above Brent took a direct hit from a broken stone block spinning out of the funnel. The impact blew half the masonry apart. Seb’s guard aura caught some of it. Brent’s shield caught less. The rest came through as dust and stone fragments.
“Move,” Seb said.
We moved. There was no cover left.
MARCUS VYE: 34%
The number pulsed in the corner of my vision. Then 33%. Then 32%.
The Astria panel stayed centered anyway.
DO YOU CONSENT?
YES / NO
Kip shoved through the wind toward us, glaive tucked low.
“No,” he said. “No, I’m not leaving you in this.”
Seb turned his head just enough to look at him.
“Kip. Preserve logs. Wake the guild. Contact everyone outside. Contact Dead Hand.”
“I can do that after—”
“No.”
The wind dragged Kip a step sideways. He planted the glaive and forced himself back.
“Seb, I’m not abandoning—”
Seb cut one hand through the air.
Kip vanished mid-sentence.
LOGOUT CONFIRMED
Blue motes scattered into the storm.
For a moment, the empty space where he had stood was the loudest thing in the arena.
Brent looked at Seb. “Cruel.”
Seb did not look back. “Useful.”
Discord died next.
Not disconnected. Blocked.
The overlay opened as a gray rectangle and collapsed. Desktop access did the same. Stream tools. Browser. Anything outside Aetherfall. Gone.
In-game voice stayed.
Because Astria still needed an answer.
The panel changed.
A demonstration opened beneath the consent prompt.
Not a weapon. Not power armor.
A break room.
Astria Tower. Sixth floor. White counters. The vending machine near the door grinding something it intended to call coffee.
I knew the memory before the rest of it loaded.
A woman from neural interface compliance stood by the sink with a paper cup in both hands.
We had talked before. Not much. Enough for first names. Enough for a few jokes that had worked and a few that had landed beside the point and politely died there. Enough that I knew she took the stairs down two floors when the elevators were crowded. Enough that I had started noticing which mug she used and pretending that was normal data to retain.
That afternoon, the machine jammed halfway through her drink. Neither of us wanted to be the person who cared enough to fix it, so we stood there while it clicked and complained and gave us a reason not to leave.
I asked if she wanted to grab coffee sometime.
She looked down at the cup already in her hand. Then back at me.
There was a pause.
A long pause.
My brain started filing the incident under evidence.
“Maybe,” she said.
No smile. No softening rescue.
Then she added, “I can give you my number. We can text.”
Which should have helped. It did not. Because maybe still sat in front of it, and I had already decided maybe meant no wearing better shoes.
The memory froze.
Not on her face. On the movement after.
Her thumb shifted against the paper cup. Her shoulders turned slightly toward me instead of away. Her pulse rose, then steadied. Her pupils widened by a fraction. Her free hand moved toward her pocket before she seemed to notice it and stop.
The implant added layers.
RETREAT SIGNAL: ABSENT
CONTACT OFFER: PRESENT
TORSO ORIENTATION: +4° TOWARD SPEAKER
HEART RATE: +5%
PUPIL DILATION: +3%
FACIAL AFFECT: CONTROLLED / LOW READABILITY
REJECTION CONFIDENCE: INSUFFICIENT
OPENNESS: POSSIBLE
Then the panel changed.
Not analysis. Recommendation.
RESPONSE MODELING ENABLED
Highest-probability path to desired outcome: accept contact offer / preserve low pressure.
Recommended tone: warm, brief, non-claiming.
Escalation risk: elevated.
Retreat risk: missed continuation.
The memory moved. I took the number. I remembered that much. I had managed the obvious part.
The implant showed the part after.
My answer came too fast. Too grateful. Relief packed into a harmless sentence until it stopped being harmless. I accepted the number like it was a consolation prize and made the room responsible for making me comfortable.
Then it showed the cleaner version.
A breath first. Shoulders open. Eyes still on hers. Not forward. Not hungry. Just present.
“Yeah,” past-me said. “Texting sounds good.”
The simulation adjusted my voice down half a step. Less eager. Less defensive.
“If coffee survives the texting phase, we can make future mistakes in person.”
Not perfect. Still me. Just me with the panic taken out of the steering wheel.
The implant did not tell me she wanted me. It showed me the version of the moment I had not known how to stay inside.
My health flashed over the break-room tile.
MARCUS VYE: 25%

ASTRIA NEUROQUANTUM IMPLANT PROTOCOL
DO YOU CONSENT?
YES / NO
NO was still there.
Not grayed out. Not hidden.
That made it worse.

Seb went still in the way he did before giving an order that would hurt someone. Brent stared at his panel with his jaw clenched, white shield flickering around him as the storm chewed it thin. Mathias looked at the exits, then at whatever Astria had put in front of him, then back to the exits again, like the panel had become another route and he hated that he had to count it.
Across the lane, Mara’s ice path finally failed.
Not all at once. First the outer ridge cracked under debris. Then the slick center buckled as the stone beneath it shifted toward the basin. Serra stabbed one sword into the ground and caught Mara by the forearm before the broken path could take her feet out from under her.
Mara looked furious. Not scared. Furious that the world had stopped respecting the work.
“Serra,” she said.
“I know.”

Serra heard him. She still checked the route herself before accepting it.
Good.

BRENT DONOVAN: 3%

Blue.
Available.

Consent first. Exit second.
Brent saw it too. “Now?”

“Thank you. Your consents have been recorded. Logout is restored.”

Available.

The arena vanished.
