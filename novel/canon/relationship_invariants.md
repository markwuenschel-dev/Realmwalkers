# Relationship Invariants — Dominion Realm

> **Status:** Canon. **Owner file** for relational facts — standing, recognition, and dynamic between characters.
> **Home:** `novel/canon/relationship_invariants.md`
> **Precedence:** For any fact about how two characters stand relative to each other, THIS file wins. Scenes, dossiers, `relationship_map.md`, and `cast_index.md` reconcile *to* it, never the reverse. "Newest artifact wins" does **not** apply here — a scene that contradicts an invariant is the thing that's wrong, even if it's the most recent draft.
> **Why this exists:** the recurring failure is *axis collapse* — a relationship that's true on two independent axes gets flattened to one under correction pressure, and an agent retires a true half along with the false half. (Origin: the Serra "equals → asymmetric/earned" drift, where a fix to *recognition* wrongly deleted *parity*.) Mechanics drift is already controlled by separating variables and stamping DO-NOT-CONFLATE; this extends that discipline to the relational layer.

---

## How an invariant is written (the format is the safeguard)

Every invariant is stored as **named axes**, not narrative description. Each axis is tagged:

- **[FIXED]** — true now, not earned, not in question. A scene may *reveal* it; never *create* it.
- **[EARNED]** — built across story time. A scene shows it *in motion* or *just achieved*; never handed over free at the start.
- **[PRESENT·SUBSURFACE]** — true and live, but must not appear on the surface yet.

Each invariant also carries an **Independence** line (which axes are orthogonal and must never be collapsed) and a **Both poles** line (the *two* wrong answers — drift has a direction, and naming only the right answer lets an agent over-correct toward whichever pole the last instruction pointed at).

## INV-1 — Marcus ⇄ Serra

- **Axis A — Skill parity. [FIXED]** Equals in skill and standing. True before the scrim, unchanged by it. Not earned, never in question.
- **Axis B — Recognition. [EARNED · ONE-DIRECTIONAL]** Marcus rates Serra going in — studied the benchmark world-PK, never once in the room when she lost. Serra does **not** rate Marcus going in — a body on a #25 guild, not a name. Flow is Serra→Marcus across a meeting: the exchange earns *her* recognition of *him*.
- **Axis C — Romantic charge. [PRESENT · SUBSURFACE]** Charge exists from Earth (the emotional center of the book). On a first read it must **not** be visible — recognition lands as "this specific person has meaning," not "love interest."
- **Independence:** A and B are orthogonal — parity can be total while recognition is zero. *The duel reveals parity; it does not confer it.* C is present whether or not it is surfaced.
- **Both poles:** Marcus is **never her superior** (dominance drift). Marcus is **never her underdog who levels up to her** (earned-parity drift — the exact collapse that started this file). Equal in fact; unequal only in *her awareness*, and only until a meeting closes that gap.
- **Name discipline:** **Serra** in all prose and dialogue. "Sarah" retired (surname retained → Serra Hawthorne).
- **Supersedes:** `serra_hawthorne.md` SCENE-001 section and `relationship_map.md` Earth-dynamic line ("recognition through play, as equals" — both halves inverted: implies *mutual* recognition of an *earned* parity).
- **See also:** `forbidden_drift.md` #10 (Serra Flattening — independent agenda, INV-3).

## INV-2 — Marcus ⇄ Seb (mirror-rival)

- **Axis A — Drive. [FIXED · SHARED]** Same engine: high agency, terror of helplessness.
- **Axis B — Direction. [FIXED · OPPOSITE]** Seb spends it on *control/power* to escape helplessness; Marcus must learn *connection*. Same drive, opposite vector — that is the whole mirror.
- **Axis C — Org role. [FIXED]** Seb leads the guild (Earth).
- **Axis D — Peer standing. [FIXED · EQUAL]** Between them, **not** hierarchical. Mirror, not chain of command.
- **Axis E — Moral standing. [FIXED]** Both good men. Seb is a good man rationalizing a dangerous power — **not a villain**. Marcus is **not morally superior**; his own failure mode is control too. The difference is trajectory, not virtue.
- **Independence:** C and D orthogonal (leading ≠ Marcus subordinate in standing). A and E orthogonal (sharing the flaw ≠ either is the bad one).
- **Both poles:** Seb never an antagonist/villain (grimdark drift), never a frictionless ally whose divergence is cosmetic (the rivalry is load-bearing tragedy). Marcus never the clean foil to Seb's corruption — they share the wound.

## INV-3 — Marcus ⇄ the ensemble (his place in the group)

- **Axis A — POV. [FIXED]** Marcus is the narrative lens.
- **Axis B — Centrality. [GUARDED — not fixed]** POV is **not** center of gravity. He's the X-factor — "the part of the plan they couldn't show the other teams" — not the leader (Seb leads) and not the sun the others orbit.
- **Independence:** A does not grant B. Being the lens confers no leadership, no dominance, no default claim on the others' attention or arcs.
- **Both poles:** Marcus-centrism (every character exists to advance his arc — Serra Flattening generalized to the whole cast), and observer-erasure (writing him passive when he *is* the decisive variable in his lane). Each of the five others must want something that is not about Marcus.

## INV-4 — Marcus ⇄ Ayla (guide bandwidth)

- **Axis A — Function. [FIXED]** Mentor/guide; helps him ask better questions.
- **Axis B — Completeness. [FIXED · LIMITED]** Unreliable and incomplete; says less than she knows, withholds on purpose. Does not solve too much.
- **Independence:** A does not imply answers. A guide is not an oracle; her dryness is flavor, not function.
- **Both poles:** Ayla as exposition machine / answer-dispenser (`forbidden_drift.md` #6), and Ayla as pure comic foil whose lines are a duo bit (#5). A withholding guide with a dry edge — both at once.

## INV-5 — Marcus ⇄ the three foils (Mara / Mathias / Brent)

Shared shape: **each foil corrects a Marcus strength that is also his blind spot.** Collapse risk is reducing the foil to one trait.

- **Mara** corrects *systems-thinking applied to people* — treating persons as variables/puzzles. **Both poles:** "the quiet/nice one"; or Marcus dismissing her. (Reserved *and* the one who reads people as people.)
- **Mathias** corrects *build/grow at any cost* — asks what the land can survive, not just what the settlement needs. **Both poles:** naive/soft. His warmth is conviction, not weakness.
- **Brent** corrects *abstraction* — turns problems into constraints. **Two axes, do not collapse:** manner [blunt, reads rude] vs. intent [looking out for you]. **Both poles:** comic grump / casual asshole; or secretly-gentle softie. The bluntness is real; the care is realer.

## INV-6 — Seb ⇄ his brother ⇄ the Soulkeepers' Exchange (temptation)

- **Axis A — Consent origin. [FIXED]** Seb took Astria's deal out of grief/desperation about his brother; the brother dies in the opening event → consent is **grief, not rescue.**
- **Axis B — The Broker's offer. [FIXED · HONEST WORDING, MISREAD]** The Broker does **not** lie: restoration is genuinely possible — *"your brother can be restored," "death is not the wall your world believed."* The deception lives in the gap between the words and what Seb hears — he assumes **restored = whole, unchanged, truly alive**, when resurrection can return a body without guaranteeing identity, memory, sanity, or continuity. The tragedy needs the offer to be *real*, not a bluff.
- **Axis C — The hidden cost. [FIXED]** The price isn't a thing Seb hands over; it's what he must **become**. To reach his brother he must turn himself into the *mechanism* — surviving states that should kill him, drawing vitality/soul-force from others, deepening his bond to death and thresholds, growing less anchored to ordinary life. Core tragedy: **becoming capable of the rescue may destroy the kind of person his brother would have wanted back.** (His Aetherfall **Warlord** + Realm **Reaver** + **Threshold Cascade** are this transformation made mechanical. This supersedes the earlier provisional "surrender your true name" framing.)
- **Both poles:** the offer written as an outright lie/bluff (kills the tragedy — it must be genuinely possible); and Seb written as duped/stupid (he is desperate and rationalizing, hearing "whole and alive" in honest words that never promised it).

## Triage — what belongs here

Harden a relationship here only if it is **both** (1) **load-bearing** — wrong corrupts many downstream scenes — and (2) **multi-axis** — two or more independent truths that can be collapsed. Most relationships are neither and stay in `relationship_map.md` as ordinary description. This file is deliberately short; a single-axis fact added here dilutes the signal that everything in it is a tripwire.

## How to use this file

- **Coder:** read before any scene with two named characters. Hold *every* axis — FIXED as already true, EARNED in motion.
- **Critic:** enforce via `relational_clarity_rules.md`. An invariant violation is CHANGES REQUESTED, not APPROVED.
- **Documentor / reconcile:** when scene and dossier disagree on a relational fact, reconcile *to this file*, not to the newest artifact.
- **Correction protocol (human + orchestrator):** when correcting an agent on any invariant, **always say the preserve-half out loud — "keep X; change Y" — never "X is wrong, it's Y."** Silence on the half that should survive reads as condemnation, and the agent retires it. This one habit would have prevented the Serra collapse.
