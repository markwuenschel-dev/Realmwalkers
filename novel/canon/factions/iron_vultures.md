# The Iron Vultures — Faction File

> **Status:** Active — operating in the Eriadne region
> **Last updated:** working draft
> **Type:** Mercenary / survivor company

---

## Who They Are

The Iron Vultures are not a guild, not a army, and not a gang — though they have elements of all three. They are a company of survivors: people from elsewhere who banded together out of necessity and are still in the process of figuring out what they want to become.

They are relatively new. They did not rise with Eriadne and they are not part of its power structure. They arrived in the region as individuals or small groups who had lost whatever they previously belonged to — destroyed settlements, failed expeditions, fleeing something worse — and found each other on the margins. The name came after the fact, a piece of dark humor that stuck: they were picking over the remains of other people's disasters to survive. Vultures. The iron came later, when they started to harden into something more deliberate.

They are independent of Eriadne's importance. The city is a resource to them — a market, a place to sell services, a source of contracts — but they don't operate inside it and they don't owe it anything. This gives them a freedom that Eriadne's internal factions don't have, and a fragility that comes with having no permanent home.

---

## Origin

No single founding moment. The Vultures cohered over time from multiple streams:

- Survivors of a settlement that was overrun — exact location and nature unresolved, but the destruction was significant enough that the survivors couldn't go back and had no obvious place to go forward to
- A handful of experienced fighters who had been operating as independent contractors and found safety in numbers
- People who came to the Eriadne region for reasons of their own and discovered the emerging company was the most viable option for people without roots here

The thing that holds them together is not ideology, not loyalty to a leader, and not a shared goal. It is the practical recognition that alone they would have died already. Together they have not.

---

## Structure

Loose. The Iron Vultures operate closer to a company of equals than a hierarchy — the people who have been there longer have more weight, but nobody holds absolute authority. Decisions that affect the whole group tend to happen through rough consensus, which is slow and sometimes dangerously slow.

**The problem this creates:** When speed matters, the lack of clear command structure is a liability. There have been moments where a faster decision would have cost fewer lives, and the memory of those moments is a permanent undercurrent in how the Vultures relate to their own governance question.

**Current leadership:** A small core of older members who have survived long enough to have institutional memory. No single leader. The closest thing is whoever is trusted enough to make a call when there isn't time to ask everyone — and that trust is earned case by case, not granted by title.

---

## What They Do

Contracts. They take work that pays, and they are not particularly selective about the source as long as payment is reliable and the job doesn't require things that would fracture the company's internal trust. This means:

- Escort and protection work
- Recovery of goods or people from dangerous locations
- Scouting and intelligence gathering
- Occasionally, fighting — but they are not a military force and don't pretend to be

They are competent. They have to be; they've survived long enough to develop real operational expertise. They are not the best mercenary option in the region for large-scale military contracts. They are possibly the best option for jobs that require people who know how to stay alive in bad situations and think under pressure.

---

## Resources and Limitations

**What they have:**
- People who have survived things most people haven't
- Operational experience in the region
- No allegiances that constrain them
- A hard-won ability to function under duress

**What they lack:**
- A permanent base
- Enough members to take large contracts
- Financial stability — they live contract to contract
- The kind of institutional weight that makes Eriadne's power nodes take them seriously

## Disposition Toward Marcus

Pre-contact. They have not yet encountered him.

The likely entry point: Marcus's work in the ruined district creates overlap. The Vultures operate in the region around Eriadne; the ruined district sits at the edge of territory both of them might claim an interest in. Whether first contact is cooperative, hostile, or somewhere between depends on circumstances not yet determined.

**The structural tension:** The Iron Vultures are survivors who banded together out of necessity. Marcus is building something for the same kind of people — the ones Eriadne's power structure doesn't have room for. They could be natural allies. They could also see his project as competition for the same limited pool of desperate people who might otherwise join them.

**What would make them allies:** Marcus demonstrating that what he's building is complementary rather than competitive — that there's a place for people who want roots and a place for people who don't, and the two can coexist.

**What would make them hostile:** Perceiving his growing influence in the ruined district as a power grab that threatens their operational freedom in the region.

## The Name

Iron Vultures. Dark humor that became identity. They were scavengers who learned to become something harder.

The name carries a specific implication: they are not predators in the apex sense. They are survivors who learned to pick over the remains of collapsed things and find value where others found only loss. That skill — reading the wreckage, knowing what's worth salvaging — is their core competency and their core self-image.

The irony, and the thing they don't always say aloud: they are themselves wreckage of something else. They are the remains. They are surviving themselves.
