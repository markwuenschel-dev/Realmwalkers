## Physical / Appearance

> *Visual shorthand: charismatic Black leader with deep-voice command presence. First impression: people listen because his presence implies competence.*

**Core look — Idris Elba lane** (that energy, not literally him): Black, tall, composed, attractive, deep-voiced, naturally charismatic-looking. ~6'1"–6'2"; broad-shouldered but not bulky; deep voice; well-kept appearance; strong facial structure; calm, confident eyes; attractive in a mature, grounded way; looks like someone who belongs in charge. He doesn't need to posture — his genuine leadership (warm, persuasive, strategic, responsible) is visible before he speaks.

**Body language:** open posture; calm hand gestures; makes people feel included; direct eye contact that doesn't feel confrontational; under stress becomes more *controlled*, not more emotional.

**Realm:** as he bends toward dangerous power the warmth becomes managed — "I trust him" → "I trust him… but I'm not sure I should." Not a dramatic body change; his presence simply stops feeling purely safe. His failure mode ("what we need" quietly becoming "what I need, dressed as what we need") shows in eyes and voice before actions. As Threshold Cascade / Reaver deepen: flatter eyes after the cascade, predatory stillness, red-black stress lines and cold-blue vagal imagery in power-active states; the baseline keeps the original charisma. The horror is that the leader was real.

## Core Identity

Seb is leadership under desperation. He is genuinely good at what he does — charismatic, empathetic, strategic, responsible. His guild leadership was real and the people under him knew it. His younger brother's illness gave Astria the lever they needed, and Seb knew it was a lever and accepted the pressure anyway, because the alternative was doing nothing while his brother ran out of time.

He is not a villain in waiting. He is a person who has decided that the ends justify the means when the end is someone he loves — and who has not yet encountered the cost of that decision at full scale.

## Personality

### Baseline Strengths

- Genuinely warm and persuasive — drawing people toward him is his natural state, not a technique
- Frames goals collectively — "what we need" rather than "what I want"; this is real, not performed, at the start
- Empathetic and strategically intelligent — reads people and situations at a high level
- Responsible — the guild trusted him because he earned it, not because he took it
- High-agency — like Marcus, he moves toward problems rather than away from them

### Failure Mode

- The collective framing gradually becomes a rationalization tool — "what we need" starts meaning "what I need, dressed as what we need"
- His empathy becomes instrumental — he reads people to manage them rather than to understand them
- Warmth pulls back under pressure and something more managed appears — the rhetoric stays but the feeling leaves
- He becomes willing to justify anything if the justification is noble enough
- His corruption point: **power is only evil if you use it selfishly — I'm using it to save someone**

### Hidden Weak Point

He fears helplessness above everything else. His brother's illness is the specific face of that fear — the situation he cannot fix through competence, leadership, or force of will. The Realm gave him power and took away the person he came here to save. What he does with that displacement is his arc.

### What He Wants to Prove

That care is enough justification. That wanting to save someone you love makes the cost acceptable. That he is still the person his guild trusted even as his choices become unrecognizable.

## Background

### Earth Life

Guild leader of a top-25 Aetherfall roster. His brother's cancer treatment costs created the financial pressure Astria exploited. He knew the offer was coercive and accepted it anyway. **But the brother dies during the Day 0 scrim — before Seb ever signs.** The lever collapses in real time: the person he came for is gone before the choice is even formalized, and he goes anyway, from grief.

What this means for his arc: his original motive loses its object at the very start. He is left with the drive — save someone, make the cost worth it — and no one to point it at, until the broker reveals he holds the brother's soul and dangles recall.

### Guild Role

Leader. Not just in title — in practice. He held the roster together, made the hard calls on composition and strategy, managed the personalities. Marcus was his most valuable player because Marcus filled gaps Seb couldn't predict. Serra was his most dangerous player because she was irreplaceable and impossible to replace if she walked.

He trusted Marcus's reads. Marcus trusted Seb's direction. That dynamic is the foundation of their mirror-rival relationship — they were genuinely aligned, which makes the divergence meaningful.

### SCENE-001 — Guild Leader, On-Page (Earth canon)

SCENE-001 establishes Seb as the **404: Aim Not Found** guild leader at the pre-Worlds scrim against Dead Hand. His on-page register is **warm and collective** — the "we" leader: "the version of the night where everybody won," "*We've got this*… We, all of us, together — watch." He watches the spawn point for Marcus specifically; he sets the night's strategy (the "kill route": hunt the casters, don't out-boss the top teams); his warmth folds back "a degree — not gone, just managed" when he has thirty people to point in one direction. He calls Marcus the secret weapon, "but only once it's working." He decides to wait when Dead Hand's rogue is missing.

> **Pending lever — NOT introduced in SCENE-001:** Seb's sick brother is the coercion lever Astria will use, but he is **not** introduced or referenced on-page here (per the scene boundary, that belongs to SCENE-002's coercion). In SCENE-001 Seb's stakes are the **team and Worlds** only. Do **not** canonize the brother as introduced — record it as a reserved future lever.

## Power System

### Interface — Threshold Cascade

**Vago-adrenal self-consumption.** Seb's interface burns *himself* — pushing past vagal/adrenal thresholds to spend his own body for power. It consumes the self.

### Class — Warrior (base) → Reaver (specialization, broker-granted early)

His Realm class is **Warrior**; the cross-world broker grants the **Reaver specialization early**. Reaver is a *standard Warrior specialization* (`classes.md`) — the broker's actual "gift" is **early access** at a level Seb shouldn't have it, **not** a unique class. **Reaver consumes *others*** — reclaims enemies' vitality.

### The Intertwined Loop (unique to Seb)

Class and interface advance **as one**: self-damage (Threshold Cascade) powers what the Reaver strips from enemies; the spoils repair the self-damage; the repair enables more cascade. He breaks and mends himself in a tightening spiral, going down the hole. He is the only character whose class and interface advance intertwined. By the finale he is the closest of the six to taking the Myrmidon alone — and still can't. Among the most powerful of the six.

### The Noble Corruption

The power **amplifies** his existing gray fall — the drive that no one close to him dies again — rather than altering him. It rewards the calculation that a cost is worth it, then quietly redefines whose cost. *(The old Oath/Command/Banner/Sacrificial design and the chart's "Champion / empower-others" framing are retired.)*

## Relationships

### Marcus (Soren)
The mirror dynamic. Both care deeply. Both are high-agency. Both fear helplessness. Seb's answer to helplessness is control — manage every variable, make every sacrifice worth it. Marcus's answer is connection — build something real with people and let the uncertainty be bearable.

They are the same wound expressed as opposite strategies.

Seb sees Marcus as someone who could be more if he stopped second-guessing himself. Marcus sees Seb as someone who could be better if he stopped justifying everything. Both reads are partially correct.

The collision point: Seb's path eventually puts him in direct conflict with what Marcus is building. Unresolved — when and how is an open question.

### His Brother
The dead center of his arc. **The brother dies during the Day 0 scrim — before Seb ever consents.** The medical-cost lever Astria built the offer around collapses in real time: the person he came to save is gone before he signs, and he signs anyway, from grief and freefall. Later the **cross-world Broker** — an agent of the **Soulkeepers' Exchange** (the mercantile continuity-power; its client is the **Nightbound**, `nightbound.md`; see `cosmology.md`) reveals he has *grabbed the brother's soul* and offers a path back. **The Broker does not lie:** restoration is genuinely possible — *"your brother can be restored," "death is not the wall your world believed."* The deception is in what Seb assumes away. The real price is **what he must *become*** — to reach his brother he has to turn himself into the *mechanism* (survive states that should kill him, draw vitality/soul-force from others, deepen his bond to death and thresholds, grow less anchored to ordinary life). And **"restored" does not guarantee continuity** — resurrection can return a body without identity, memory, sanity, or the person his brother was. The tragedy: *becoming capable of the rescue may destroy the kind of person his brother would have wanted back.* The leash is not a bluff — it is a true thing with a cost Seb refuses to look at. (INV-6, `relationship_invariants.md`.)

## Arc

### Book 1 Function

Mirror to Marcus. Mostly in satellite arc — his path runs parallel and intersects at key moments. His presence in Book 1 is primarily through POV interludes that show what he's becoming. The reader should understand why Seb says yes before they understand what saying yes costs. **Solo arc (shortest of the six):** lands desolate → enslaved / tortured / forced labor → kills his captor barehanded → *then* the broker speaks → straight to Eriadne.

### Core Arc Question

> At what point does justifying everything for someone you love become indistinguishable from justifying everything for yourself?

### Starting Point

Genuine. His warmth is real, his leadership is real, his care for the guild and his brother is real. He is not yet rationalizing — he is deciding, with full awareness of the cost, that the cost is acceptable.

### End Direction

Book 1 ends with Seb having accepted or approached a power source that looks noble and has begun to show its real cost. He has rationalized the first warning sign. The reader sees it. He does not — or he does and decides it's acceptable.
