## Physical / Appearance

> *First impression: warm confidant / balance point.*

Approachable before he reads as competent — the warm confidant, the balance point, the person people end up telling things to without quite meaning to. **Not** a rugged ranger or hardened scout. His Earth impression is wavy-haired intellectual warmth — David-Krumholtz / *Numb3rs* energy: intelligent, expressive, slightly rumpled, emotionally available, more perceptive than he advertises. Dark wavy hair with enough volume to be memorable, kind or curious eyes, a face that looks open even when he's thinking hard. Average height or slightly below the more imposing members; soft-to-average build, not visually coded as a fighter. Body language steady, angled toward people, listening with his whole face. If Marcus's attention feels intense, Mathias's feels *safe*.

**Earth.** Dresses like a thoughtful IT/infrastructure person who forgot fashion exists but still has taste: layered shirts, soft jackets, practical shoes, maybe glasses. Looks like someone who solves problems by understanding how systems connect — and like someone you'd trust with an embarrassing confession at 1 a.m.

**Realm.** His Scout class should **not** turn him into a leather-clad wilderness archetype. Gear stays approachable and practical: travel layers, map cases, cord, small tools, a battered notebook or interface-marked route sketches. Enteric Lattice manifests through posture and visceral reaction more than flashy magic — a hand to the abdomen, a pause before entering a room, a pained look when a group's bond is about to snap. Golden-green dependency threads appear around him only in active-interface visuals.

## Core Identity

Mathias is warm from confidence, not from need. He is curious about everything — people, systems, places, creatures, histories — and he pursues that curiosity without agenda. The Realm is not a threat to him or a game or a punishment. It is the greatest mystery he has ever encountered, and his response to that is the same response he has had to everything since he was a kid asking every adult in the room what their job was: *tell me more.*

His drive is contact. Not understanding as an abstract goal, not mapping or documenting, not analysis. He wants to reach things — people, creatures, factions, ancient presences, things no one else has managed to touch. The reaching is the point.

## Personality

### Baseline Strengths

- Warm without performance — the warmth comes from genuine interest, not social strategy
- Curious about everything — people are one of the most fascinating things in the world, and the Realm adds several more categories
- Confident openness — he is not open because he doesn't know better; he is open because he has seen enough of the world and chosen openness anyway
- The question that opens an hour — he asks one thing and somehow you end up talking for an hour, and it doesn't feel like he did anything except listen
- Finds groups fast — his solo arc in the Realm is short because he is constitutionally incapable of being around people without becoming part of something
- Not a leader by dominance — he does not take charge, he does not compete for authority; people turn toward him when they decide to tell the truth

### What He Is Not

Not naive. Not a golden retriever. Not innocent. His warmth is not the warmth of someone who hasn't been tested — it is the warmth of someone who has seen enough and chose this anyway. That distinction is important and should be felt.

Not a therapist or group conscience. He is not in the cast to draw out other characters' arcs. He has his own thing he is after.

Not the heart of the group in a sentimental sense. He is the person who makes contact possible — with new places, new people, new things. That is a function, not a personality substitute.

### The Social Superpower

He asks one question and somehow you end up talking for an hour.

Not because it's a technique. Because he is actually interested in the answer, and that interest is legible, and people feel it. When Mathias asks what's going on with you, the question doesn't land like social obligation. It lands like he actually wants to know.

The result: people tell him things they didn't plan to tell anyone. Not because he manipulated them into it. Because he made it feel safe to say the true thing.

When someone new enters the group, everyone else asks variations of *can we trust him* or *what's his angle.* Mathias asks *what's his story.* That's the difference.

### Failure Mode

The contact drive has a dark side. He reaches toward things that don't want to be reached, and then he has to decide whether to keep going.

This creates a specific kind of problem: he is bad at taking no for an answer when the thing saying no is interesting enough. Not in an aggressive way — in the way of someone who genuinely cannot believe that something doesn't want to be understood. His assumption is that contact is always possible if you approach correctly. The Realm will eventually show him something that breaks that assumption.

The cost lands hardest when he reaches something and the reaching changes it — or changes him. Some things in the Realm resist contact for reasons. His curiosity does not naturally include the question *should I be doing this.*

## Background

### Earth Life

Average suburban background. Nothing dramatic, nothing exceptional about the circumstances. What was exceptional was the kid inside them — the one who asked every adult in the room what their job was, who wanted to know how the plumber figured out where the leak was, what the teacher did on weekends, how the neighbor knew so much about cars.

Not precocious in an annoying way. Genuinely interested. People could tell the difference. Adults talked to him like a person earlier than they talked to most kids that way, because he treated their answers like they mattered.

He grew up collecting stories without realizing that was what he was doing. By the time he was in his twenties he was the person in any room who somehow already knew something about whoever had just walked in — not because he researched people, but because he remembered things. The conversation from three years ago. The detail someone mentioned once. The thing you said that you forgot you said.

His Earth job is TBD but the flavor is something that combines people and discovery — wildlife biologist, archaeologist, anthropologist, travel journalist, documentary filmmaker, park ranger, game designer. Something where curiosity about the world is the core competency.

### Why He Accepted Astria's Offer

TBD — but the emotional logic is clear. An offer to go somewhere completely unknown, to encounter something no one has encountered before, to be in contact with a world that hasn't been mapped yet. For someone whose engine is wonder and whose drive is contact, that offer is not a risk. It is an invitation.

### Gaming and the Guild

He ended up in a top-25 guild because he is a gifted scout and observer. Not the highest DPS. Not the best mechanical player. The one who knew where the enemy was, what they were planning, which strategy would work given who was in the raid that night and how tired they were.

He reads people as naturally as he reads terrain. In Aetherfall that combination made him irreplaceable for any fight that required actually understanding the situation rather than executing a pre-planned approach.

He probably knows more about the private lives of his guildmates than anyone except possibly Seb. Not because he pries. Because he asks one question and somehow people end up talking for an hour.

## Aetherfall Class — Ranger/Hunter (Scout Specialization)

Not pet-focused. Not animal-bond dependent. Scout-focused, observer-focused, explorer-focused.

The Ranger class fits him because Rangers are the guild's eyes. They notice things. They move ahead, they read terrain and enemy composition, they bring back information the raid needs to make good decisions. Mathias chose this class because it suited how he already thought — wide awareness, patience, the ability to be present in a space without disturbing it.

He is not a Ranger because he likes killing things. He is a Ranger because Rangers get to discover what's beyond the next hill.

**SCENE-001 confirmed (Earth canon):** Mathias is the **404 Ranger/scout** at the pre-Worlds scrim against Dead Hand — measured, reads the room "like he was reading weather off a tree line," with the pause "newcomers mistook for slowness and the rest of them had learned to read as Mathias deciding the thing was worth saying." He is the **first to flag the missing enemy rogue**: he clocks that Dead Hand has stopped milling and is pooling toward their leader ("Something's not settled over there"), then names the actual gap — "They've got one assassin warming up over there. Where's the other one?" His read is what surfaces the night's hinge: the rogue (Serra) hasn't shown **yet** — she logs in late, then duels Marcus on-page before the hijack cuts it short.

## Power Interface — Enteric Lattice

**Enteric Lattice** (locked). A connection-sense interface rooted in the enteric nervous system and vagal pathways — he feels relationship and connection between people, places, and presences in his gut, and can open channels to things that resist ordinary approach. ## Relationships

### Marcus (Soren)
Natural dynamic. Marcus reads systems analytically and spirals inward. Mathias reads people experientially and moves outward. When Marcus is stuck in analysis paralysis, Mathias is probably the one who asks the single question that unlocks him — not because he diagnosed the problem, but because he was genuinely curious about what Marcus was thinking and Marcus ended up talking for an hour and figuring it out himself.

Marcus's pattern-reading and Mathias's contact-making are complementary in specific ways. Marcus finds the hidden structure. Mathias makes contact with what's inside it.

### Serra
She processes fast and decides. He processes by making contact and accumulating. They will occasionally want to move at very different speeds — she is ready to go before he has finished being interested in the current thing. That friction is productive rather than hostile.

### Seb
Interesting dynamic. Seb's warmth is becoming instrumental — he reads people to manage them. Mathias's warmth is genuine — he reads people because he is interested in them. Mathias will probably notice the shift in Seb before most of the others do, because he is paying attention for different reasons than everyone else. Whether he says anything about it is an open question.

### Mara
They are both observers. But Mara observes to manage how she is perceived and to read threat. Mathias observes because he wants to know. Their overlap is in the quality of attention — both notice things others miss — but they use that attention for opposite purposes. Interesting potential for a scene where they clock each other doing similar things for completely different reasons.

### Brent
Brent's anti-abstraction directness and Mathias's open-ended curiosity will create specific comic friction. Brent asks *what does it do.* Mathias asks *what is it.* Brent finds that exhausting. Mathias finds Brent's impatience genuinely interesting, which makes it more exhausting.

## Arc

### Book 1 Function

His solo arc is short — he finds a group fast and becomes part of it. His Book 1 function is partly relational glue across whatever cast members are present, but more specifically he is the person pursuing contact with things the others haven't managed to reach. Ancient presences in the Realm. Factions that resist outside contact. Creatures that other people can't get near.

The dark turn in his arc comes when he reaches something that doesn't want to be reached and has to decide whether to keep going. That decision — and what it costs — is his Book 1 stakes.

### Core Arc Question

> What happens when contact requires more than curiosity — when the thing on the other side of the reach decides what you become by letting you in?

### Starting Point

Genuinely warm, genuinely curious, constitutionally inclined toward people and discovery. Arrives in the Realm and experiences it as an invitation rather than a threat. Finds a group quickly because that is what he does.

### End Direction

Book 1 leaves Mathias having made contact with something significant — something no one else managed to reach — and carrying the weight of what that contact cost or changed. His warmth is intact but it has been tested by something that didn't respond to it the way things usually do. The question of whether to keep reaching, and what the right conditions for that reaching are, is unresolved.
