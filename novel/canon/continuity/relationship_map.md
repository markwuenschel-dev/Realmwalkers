# Relationship Map — Dominion Realm

> **Purpose:** Tracks the emotional state, current status, and trajectory of every significant relationship in the story. Update when a relationship meaningfully changes. Cross-reference `character_state_log.md` for situational context and `unresolved_character_threads.md` for open questions.

> **Emotional center of Book 1:** Marcus and Serra. Everything else orbits this.

---

## How to Read This Map

**Charge:** the emotional current running through the relationship — what makes it alive
**Status:** where things stand at the most recent tracked checkpoint
**Tension:** the specific unresolved thing that gives the relationship its pressure
**Trajectory:** where it's heading if nothing intervenes

## Tier 1 — Load-Bearing Relationships

### Marcus ↔ Serra

> **Defers to INV-1 (`relationship_invariants.md`).** Standing/recognition/dynamic facts reconcile to that file. **Skill parity is FIXED** — Marcus and Serra are equals in fact, true from the start; the Earth duel *reveals* parity, it does **not** confer it (he is never her superior, never her underdog who levels up to her). **Recognition is EARNED and one-directional (Serra→Marcus)** — Marcus rates her going in; she does not rate him until the exchange earns it. The old "recognition through play, as equals" framing is **retired** (it inverted both halves — implying *mutual* recognition of an *earned* parity).

**Charge:** Recognition. They see each other more clearly than anyone else does — and that is both the draw and the danger. (Direction discipline: on Earth this clarity is *earned* and flows Serra→Marcus first; parity itself is a given, never argued.)

**Earth foundation — the kiss.** At a guild party, late, alcohol in it, the air changed and they kissed. It almost went further. Then it didn't — they got interrupted, or got shy, or both, and the moment closed. What happened *next* is the part that actually matters: in the seconds after, each made a small protective move at nearly the same instant — a half-step back, a deflecting joke, eyes away. And each of them only registered the *other's* pullback, never their own. So both walked away believing they were the one who'd been downgraded — or the one who should downgrade it before it got weird. Neither wanted to be the one caught wanting more.

That misread sealed itself. There were attempts to circle back, but reopening it had come to feel like begging, and circumstance never offered a clean opening, and time passed, and the silence calcified into something neither could break without admitting how much they'd thought about it. It didn't damage what they were in the guild. It made it more charged — a permanently live wire everyone agreed not to touch.

**The asymmetry — not depth, but wiring.** The tempting read is that Marcus felt more. He didn't. The difference isn't wattage; it's their relationship to hope. Marcus hopes outward — he felt something real and his instinct was to build on it. Serra guards inward — she may feel exactly as much, but she is constitutionally unable to *claim* a thing this uncertain. Her read was never "this was casual." Her read was "this is unclaimable." She isn't sure whether what she feels is terrifying or the thing she's been waiting for, and not-knowing, for her, defaults to not-reaching. Same current, opposite circuitry.

**What each is afraid of.** Marcus is afraid that if it's ever named, Serra will say: *I was curious, I wanted to see what would happen, we got interrupted, and that's kind of it* — that the kiss was a flare, not a beginning. Serra is afraid that Marcus expects a clean declaration she isn't sure she can give: *I want this, romantically; the kiss meant more than a fun thing* — and that her uncertainty will land on him as a no. Each is braced against the version of the other that would make wanting it cost too much.

**Realm reunion.** They find each other on the earlier side of Book 1 (the False Rescue arc). The relief is real. So is the unaddressed thing, sitting exactly where they left it — except now every comfortable reason to leave it alone has been stripped away. They survive something together; the shorthand returns. So does the wire.

**The Realm makes the old pattern visible.** Both their interfaces broadcast emotion — his limbal ring leaks, and the Eyes let him read micro-detail; her Pressure-Severance intensifies with the very commitment she's trying not to show. When the Earth thing finally surfaces, Marcus's Eyes catch Serra's physical reactions *accurately* — and interpret them *wrong*. He reads her uncertainty as disinterest. This is his core flaw aimed at the worst possible target: **seeing data is not knowing truth.** And it didn't start in the Realm. It started that night. The Realm just turns the old silence into something neither can hide behind anymore.

**The separation as dry run.** When Serra splits from Marcus during the False Rescue arc, it rehearses the real wound in miniature: he extracts and saves people (builds threads); she kills the magistrate and breaks the structure (cuts knots); she leaves with no speech. That parting is the shape of what's coming — the same engine, lower stakes.

**The severance — late Book 1, inside the finale.** The second parting happens during the Myrmidon fight and its immediate aftermath, not a quiet reunion scene. Working in tandem again — a callback to the first real teamwork they built in the false-rescue/naming arc — Marcus and Serra find an opening (Brent's interface having exposed the Myrmidon's weakness), and Marcus has to **hold** it, taking real, mounting, could-actually-die damage to keep the opening live. Serra lines up the critical blow and **misses the depth** — it doesn't go deep enough; she needs more time. Marcus is failing, telling her he can't hold, genuinely at risk of dying from what he's doing. Serra **brushes him off and keeps going**, and finishes the thing. Marcus releases and barely survives. She was arguably right — letting go might have lost the kill and doomed everyone — but she gambled his life on her own execution and the gamble came out uglier than it needed to.

This is where Marcus's **Neurochromatic eyes first turn on** — and where the **Eyes of Meszkhal item first lies to him about her.** His true perception reads the tension in Serra's body as *conflict*: she can't forgive herself. The Meszkhal item overlays the opposite verdict — *she's doubling down; she'd do it again without a thought.* Two contradictory readings of the one person he most needs to read right. **He chooses correctly: it's the conflict.** He doesn't hold it against her. But she owns the choice, can't look at him — being *seen* in that moment is what she can't survive — and she has a larger faction to hunt. And the party is in the room with them: alone together for the first time since, the old uncertainty is live again — both pulled away that night, each certain the *other* had, neither ever sure what it was. That not-knowing compounds everything she's already failing to hold; the kiss-at-the-party isn't the trigger, it's one more thing unresolved between two people who have never resolved anything. The route between them was never mapped; neither knows what it looks like. So she goes. (The item's lie here is what plants Marcus's distrust of the Meszkhal eyes — a bad taste he carries forward as he leans into his own interface.)

**Current status (Book 1 end):** Severed. Both understand their paths aren't connected right now — which the False Rescue split already foreshadowed. The thread is cut, not dead.

**Core tension:** He sees too much and she keeps the real stuff guarded. He ruminates on what he reads; she's already decided. He wants warmth without conditions; she gives warmth freely but not the inside. They work. They are also genuinely hard for each other. This is not a problem to solve — it is the relationship.

**Trajectory:** The Book 1 emotional climax is the finale fight that becomes a severance — working as a team one last time is the very thing that breaks them open. Book 2 inherits the question of whether a cut thread can be re-tied — pointed, given that Soren is the man who reads and walks threads and Serra is the woman who severs them. The romance and the plot are the same engine in two registers: **Soren builds threads; Serra cuts knots.**

**What she gives him that no one else does:** She sees courage in him before he does. Specifically: she sees that he acts despite panic, not because he isn't afraid. He thinks his fear makes him weak. She knows otherwise.

**What he gives her that no one else does:** He is perceptive enough to see past the performance — to the person who is almost never actually known. She's not sure whether that's terrifying or the thing she's been waiting for. It is the same not-knowing that stopped her on Earth.

### Marcus ↔ Seb

**Charge:** Mirror energy. Two versions of the same drive expressing in opposite directions.

**Earth foundation:** Guild leader and core member. Close enough that the dynamic was less hierarchy and more partnership. Seb trusted Marcus's reads. Marcus trusted Seb's leadership. The emotional shorthand between them was real.

**Core difference:** Both care deeply. Both are high-agency. Both fear helplessness. Seb's answer to helplessness is control — if he can manage every variable, nothing can take what matters from him. Marcus's answer is connection — if he can build something real with people, the helplessness becomes bearable. They are the same wound expressed as opposite strategies.

**Current status:** Separated. Paths diverging.

**Core tension:** Seb's arc bends toward justification — the gradual replacement of genuine warmth with rhetorical warmth used to convince himself. Marcus will see it happening and not know what to do with what he sees. The moment Seb's framing shifts from "what we need" to "what I need, dressed as what we need" — Marcus will catch that seam. Whether he says anything is an open question.

**Trajectory:** Mirror becomes rival. The collision point is unresolved but the direction is set. Seb's path eventually puts him in direct conflict with what Marcus is building.

### Marcus ↔ Ayla

**Charge:** The oldest relationship Marcus has in the Realm. She was there when he had nothing else.

**Foundation:** She attached to his implant during or just after the resurrection. She needed an anchor; he needed a guide. The deal was practical on both sides. It has become something else.

**Dynamic:** She is not a mentor in any comfortable sense. Her knowledge is fragmented. She answers questions with better questions. She says less than she knows, always. When she does say something directly, it matters.

**What she is not:** She is not his mother, his conscience, or his exposition machine. She does not solve problems. She helps him ask better questions and occasionally tells him he's an idiot when he's being an idiot.

**Current status:** Active — internal. She is always present. The relationship develops through accumulated small moments rather than dramatic beats.

**Core tension:** She is ancient and her priorities are not entirely human. She has her own perspective on what Marcus is becoming and what the Realm is. Occasionally what she wants from him and what he wants from himself are not the same thing. She doesn't push. She waits. That is sometimes more unnerving than pushing.

**Trajectory:** Her fragmented knowledge has gaps that become significant later. Something she cannot remember — something the entity that trapped her deliberately destroyed — is relevant to what Marcus is building near Eriadne.

## Tier 2 — Significant Relationships

### Marcus ↔ Mara

**Charge:** Friction that's actually productive. She challenges the part of him that treats people as systems.

**Dynamic:** Mara sees communities where Marcus sees problems to solve. When he starts organizing the ruined district near Eriadne, his instinct is efficiency — who has what skills, what resources are available, what's the optimal arrangement. Mara's instinct is humanity — who is afraid, who doesn't trust each other yet, what the history between these people is. Both are necessary. Neither is complete without the other.

**Current status:** Separated — her arc runs parallel. Likely with Serra initially or on her own path.

**Tension:** She'll push back on Marcus treating the people he's building with as variables in a system. He needs to hear it. He won't always like hearing it.

---

### Marcus ↔ Mathias

**Charge:** The land as conscience.

**Dynamic:** Mathias challenges Marcus's building instincts from a different angle than Mara. Mara asks "what do these people need?" Mathias asks "what can this land survive?" When Marcus wants to grow the settlement, Mathias is the one tracking what that growth costs the ecosystem, the ley lines, the balance of the region.

**Current status:** Separated. His arc runs parallel — ecological tracking, early Xyloryn/Zalgoran warning signs.

**Tension:** Injured proving a warning others ignored; **he lives** (injured at the finale, not killed). The cost of not listening to him becomes visible when the threat he flagged finally arrives.

---

### Marcus ↔ Brent

**Charge:** Grounding. Brent turns abstract problems into practical constraints.

**Dynamic:** When Marcus is spiraling in analysis, Brent is the one who asks what the actual material problem is. He makes things. He fixes things. His presence anchors the settlement-building arc in physical reality.

**Current status:** Separated. His arc likely converges with Marcus's Eriadne path sooner than the others.

**Tension:** Possible death. Brent believes in solvable problems. The Realm has problems that cannot be fixed with craft or engineering. That gap between his worldview and the Realm's actual nature is where his arc ends.

---

### Serra ↔ Mara

**Charge:** The tandem that built itself into something neither of them planned.

**Dynamic:** Serra is force. Mara is setup. They've been tandem partners long enough that the shorthand is muscle memory. The operational dynamic is efficient: Mara reads the situation and prepares it, Serra executes. But their underlying philosophies are in friction — Serra asks who must be stopped, Mara asks what produced the threat.

**Current status:** Unclear — they may travel together after the separation from Marcus, or Mara may take a different path. ⚠ Unresolved.

**Tension:** Serra's arc bends toward harder and harder calls. Mara's arc bends toward understanding the system that produces threats rather than eliminating the threats themselves. These trajectories diverge. The question is when.

---

### Seb ↔ The Guild (Collective)

**Charge:** He was their center. They were his reason.

**Dynamic:** Seb's leadership was real — genuinely good at it, genuinely cared about the people under him. His brother's treatment need was the lever Astria built the offer around — a real lever on a real wound — but the brother dies during the Day 0 scrim, before consent. The lever collapses in real time: the reason Seb came is gone before he signs, and he signs anyway, from grief and freefall. In the Realm, with the brother dead and the guild scattered, the question becomes what he becomes when the thing he would have paid any price for is already lost — and a voice offers to give it back.

**Current status:** Separated from the guild. Brother recently dead — the wound the cross-world broker will exploit. Arc bending toward the power source that will define him.
