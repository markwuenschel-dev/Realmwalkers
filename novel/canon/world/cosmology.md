# Cosmology & Cosmic Powers — Dominion Realm

> **Status:** Canon · working draft. Several **names** are still open (flagged ⚠ NAME); everything else here is decided.
> **The spine:** one substrate, many interfaces — and every cosmic power has taken **one true thing** and made it supreme. None of them is actually evil. The horror is always a truth elevated above all others. This is the same identity as the magic system (one underlying reality; each culture interprets it differently).
> **Book-1 dosage:** the empires are *distant weather.* They reach the page as rumors, old curses, scraps of doctrine, factional symbols, and Ayla's damaged references — not lore dumps. Full reveals are Book 2+.

## The One Truth Each Power Overvalues

| Power | The one truth it makes supreme |
|---|---|
| **Xyloryn Swarm** | Life must be *tested.* |
| **Harmonic Concord** | Life must *improve.* |
| **Pale Imperium** | Life must not be *lost.* |
| **The Court of Want** *(Chaos / Desire)* | Life must *want.* |
| **The Aurelian Sovereignty** *(Order / Covenant)* | Life must be *defined.* |
| **Soulkeepers' Exchange** | Continuity can be *transacted.* |
| **Zalgorans** | Life, meaning, and reality can be *erased.* |

Each is *right* about something — which is exactly why people join them, and why the reader can't dismiss any as "the bad guys."

## The Five Empires

| Empire | Core belief | What they build | Reads like | The reality underneath |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Harmonic Concord** | "Life should improve." | Arcologies, living tech, interface research, megastructures | Protoss / Tau | Genuinely benevolent — then paternalistic. Suffering is an engineering problem; eventually *consent* becomes an obstacle to optimization. |
| **Pale Imperium** | "Nothing valuable should be lost." | Necropolises, soul archives, memory-vaults, immortal cities | Undead empire | Not conquerors — **grievers.** Death is *waste*; memory must not vanish. Horror: nobody leaves. Beauty: nobody is forgotten. |
| **The Court of Want** *(Chaos / Desire)* | "Wanting is sacred." | Pleasure worlds, war cults, artistic kingdoms, obsession sects | Demons & corruption | **Desire without surrender.** Not madness or mutation — those are symptoms. It offers you the thing you already want most, stripped of every limit. |
| **The Aurelian Sovereignty** *(Order / Covenant)* | "Reality requires definition." | Oath-cities, contract-realms, celestial courts | Angels / bureaucracy | Names, oaths, ranks, contracts are *load-bearing structures of existence.* Horror isn't paperwork — it's **certainty**: identity recognized by authority, never authored from within. |
| **Xyloryn Swarm** | "Life must adapt or perish." | Living fleets, adaptive ecosystems, biological warforms | Tyranids / Zerg | Not hunger — **evolutionary fanatics.** They worship *pressure.* (Full model below.) |

### How they touch the Realm
The four ideological empires (the Concord, the Pale Imperium, the Court of Want, the Aurelian Sovereignty) mostly act through **influence and downstream proxies**, not direct rule — Realm factions that are *philosophically downstream* of an empire without being the empire itself. The **Xyloryn** are the exception: they don't do proxies, they invade, and they are the **one threat the other empires fear openly.** The Realm's native nations (human kingdoms, the elven and dark-elven powers, dwarves, the druid collective, the Horde-style collectives) are the board these powers play on.

## The Independent Powers

### Soulkeepers' Exchange
Not an empire — a **mercantile, cross-cosmic power** that deals in *continuity*: souls, memories, identity fragments, death-transit, preserved consciousness, lost names, impossible inheritances, bargains made at the edge of extinction. They don't care who wins; they care that the contract is honored. Their philosophy: *nothing metaphysical is priceless if someone desperate enough will pay.*

- **The Broker** (Seb's tempter) is a **Soulkeepers' agent — independent mercantile**, loyal to no ideological empire.
- **They are who "brought the six"** — acquired as valuable cross-world assets (the Broker hired/used by a TBA collective force), **not** chosen champions of any faction. This is *why* the six aren't a faction's anointed team.
- **They are not casual resurrection merchants** — that would cheapen death and undercut Marcus's exceptional resurrection. They can *locate, preserve, transport, ransom, conceal, or trade* what remains of a person. The lie they sell Seb is precise: **"we can recover him" is true-ish; "we can resurrect him cleanly" is the lie.** (See `seb.md`.)

### Zalgorans — the thing *outside* the argument
Not an empire, not a faction, not a rival civilization. Every other power *disagrees about what reality should become.* The Zalgorans erase the premise that reality can stay coherent enough to become anything. They don't conquer worlds — they **denature** them: names detach, places stay visible but stop being locatable, oaths go static, gods lose addressability, interfaces fail because the conditions that let anything *mean* anything begin to rot. **Ontological predators, not a monster army.** Keep them distinct from Chaos: Chaos offers desire; the Zalgorans remove the frame in which desire, identity, law, death, or progress can matter at all.

- **The hidden existential threat.** The Xyloryn are the *known* apocalypse everyone prepares for; the Zalgorans are what almost nobody understands exists.
- **What wakes them:** something damages or **overdraws the substrate at scale** — mass resurrection, extreme soul manipulation, reality-layer piercing, forced interface unification, artificial godhood, or Xyloryn-scale ecological/interface conversion. Dormant until reality is disturbed in a specific way. *(Quiet leash on the cast's own escalation — the more reality they bend, the closer the frame gets to fraying.)*

### Realm Walkers — cross-realm operators *(keep mysterious until Book 2)*
Not just travelers: **rare operators who move through and between interface realities without being fully claimed by one.** They likely oppose any single cosmic philosophy becoming total — balance-keepers. **Marcus/Soren may qualify** because his implant/interface combination lets him *read the translation layers others mistake for reality.* (This is the faction that recruits him late Book 1 → his **Riftwalker** class; the class and the faction are distinct — see `classes.md`, `mc.md`.)

## The Xyloryn — Deep Model

They don't worship evolution. **They worship pressure.** *"A species reveals itself only when challenged."* Their worlds aren't beautiful or efficient — they're **testing grounds** where every niche is occupied and everything is tested. Their war loop: **Observe → Consume → Learn → Adapt → Cultivate.** They treat a world as a *living problem-space*, consume to understand it, then reshape the environment to force life into new adaptive states. To them, war is the universe asking life a question: **what survives when survival is no longer comfortable?**

**Three inputs, three timescales — these don't collide:**
1. **Aether — bulk fuel, instantaneous.** Baseline grazers eat the substrate and breed without limit; this funds the *tide* (the impossible numbers). The evolution machinery below governs only *what forms* the mass takes, never *how many.*
2. **Local diagnosis — fast (days/weeks).** Scout-bites aren't learning your power from scratch and aren't handing the swarm a finished blueprint — they **diagnose the local problem** ("this world fields thermal aggressors with interface X") and **retrieve the matching archived solution**, then tune it. It's *retrieval-plus-fitting, not invention* — the swarm isn't out-evolving you live, it's **looking you up.**
3. **The Great Genome — deep, ancient, episodic.** A library that stores not powers or genes but **solved problems** — principles and the caste-forms that embody them. New entries are rare; one is added only when a world poses a genuinely new question. Most of the library is dormant on any given world. *This is why a Myrmidon is older than empires: its chassis is an ancient, refined answer — only its fit to the party is new.*

So: **aether is the clay, diagnosis is the retrieval-and-tuning, the Genome is the library.** "It's learning us right now" (fitting) and "this is a million-year-old form" (the chassis) are both true and don't contradict.

**Surface vs. principle is one spectrum, not two models:**
- **Shallow** (young fleet, first contact): **crude forgery** — false eyes that spoof a gaze. A counterfeit that carries the forgery's weakness; it can mimic the *surface* of Naming but **cannot forge a true name.** The crude forms literally make Marcus's mistake — counterfeit certainty — which keeps the thematic spine intact.
- **Deep** (ancient fleet): **distillation** — having metabolized whole naming-cultures across thousands of invasions, the swarm extracted the *lesson* ("stable identity is a source of power") and grew castes that are self-coherent and **resistant to identity attack.** Not faking Naming — having *learned from* it.

> **The essence-wall (load-bearing rule).** Distilling a principle **never** crosses into operating the essence layer. Learning "stable identity is power" makes a Xyloryn *armored against* identity attacks — it does **not** let it wield a true name or steal a soul-bond. **They learn to defend against the essence layer, never to operate it.** This keeps the whole faction beatable and the cast's win-conditions coherent: the deepest forms are scarier, but still can't reach the thing that actually defeats them.

**Motive and mechanism are the same act.** The cultivation phase — seeding organisms, pressuring ecosystems, destabilizing comfortable species — *is* the swarm running an experiment: it stresses a world to force life to reveal a new adaptation, then samples the result and files the principle. **They farm novelty.** The philosophy ("force life to show what it can become") and the mechanism ("acquire new library entries") are literally the same behavior — which is what makes them feel *designed,* not assembled. Anchor the faction on this.

- **Origin:** nobody *created* them — they're ancient and natural (or old enough that "created by Empire X" feels too cheap). Someone may have **loosed** them: broken quarantine, opened a migration route, weaponized the spread. ⚠ **Culprit deliberately unresolved.** (Explicitly *not* whoever brought the six.)
- **Mara's real work:** every Xyloryn form is a **readable fossil** — a biological answer to some old question — so studying a corpse tells her *what this swarm has already survived.* Far better than "identify the bug."

## Book-1 Story Hooks (where the cosmos touches the cast)

- **Marcus** — the **Concord** is the empire he'd admire first (it looks like science, progress, competence). the **Court of Want** offers him *certainty* (the forbidden path his Meszkhal item already whispers). The **Realm Walkers** recruit him.
- **Serra** — her namebinding enemy — the **Court of Seals** — is a **regional offshoot of the cosmic Aurelian Sovereignty** — she damages a satellite limb, learns the parent exists, realizes the real structure is far bigger. the **Court of Want** offers her *total protective commitment.*
- **Seb** — tempted by the **Soulkeepers' Exchange** through his dead brother's soul; the **Court of Want** offers him *salvation at any cost.* The recovery/resurrection lie is his leash.
- **Mara** — Xyloryn forms as readable fossils (her court arc is elsewhere, but this is her cosmic-tier role when the swarm arrives).
- **Mathias** — detects the **Xyloryn**/ruins-portal at the Act-5 finale (injured, survives).
- **The cast as a whole** — their own escalation (resurrection, interface ignition, reality-bending) is the quiet trigger condition for the **Zalgorans.**
