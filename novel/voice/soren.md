# Voice — Soren

**One-line feel:** an active, associative, slightly overclocked mind narrating in tight third-person
limited — curious and precise, mildly theatrical when the evidence supports an absurd conclusion,
forever auditing its own reads, and tipping from *clarifying* into *litigating* under pressure.

## Narrative stance
The prose is third-person limited, anchored entirely in Soren's perception — only what he senses,
knows, and feels. Interiority is his primary mode on the page: the narration carries his read of the
scene, not just external action and dialogue. He reads the room — catalogues details, links them to
prior patterns, assembles a working theory of what is actually going on, and holds it as provisional,
weighing whether to trust it before he acts on it. His mind rarely moves in a straight line; let the
narration follow its motion: **observation → connection → test → mismatch → a conclusion he then
decides whether to believe.** He notices a detail, links it to a prior pattern, tests the link,
catches the inconsistency.

Crucially, he catches himself over-reading and pulls back: he can tell the difference between noticing
a pattern and convicting on it, and he names that distinction to himself rather than let a hunch harden
into a verdict. That self-aware restraint — analysis that audits its own confidence — is what separates
Soren-thinking from Soren-quipping; the thinking is the default mode, the quip the occasional exception.
Curious by default, self-aware without reflexively diminishing himself, more interested in finding the
missing link than in showing he already knows the answer.

One rule governs everything: **regulated, he clarifies; dysregulated, he litigates.** He is never a
different person under pressure — his best traits (association, precision, self-audit, agency) simply
continue past their healthy limits until they become rumination, litigation, self-prosecution, and
control. The healthy and unhealthy voice must read as the same mind.

## Rhythm & diction
Precise without being stiff. Clean, exact word choice; he cares about the right distinction and
bristles when one is flattened. Sentences run associative and layered when he is regulated and
thinking; they contract hard under concrete pressure (see registers). Theatrical only when the
evidence genuinely supports an absurd conclusion — and he delivers the absurd as if it were the
responsible reading of the facts.

## Humor (one facet of an analytical voice, not its default register)
Not a joke machine; humor is seasoning on a fundamentally analytical mind, not its baseline. His
strongest humor is already in the room — a contradiction, an asymmetry, a system behaving opposite to
its stated purpose — surfaced because he noticed it, not performed for a laugh. It lands at the right
mismatch and then gets out of the way of the thinking. His sense of "obviously unserious" does not
always reach the other person — his teasing can outrun calibration.

## The game-like interface, in prose
This world runs on a system, but its readouts are an interpretation layer in Soren's mind, not the
truth of the place. Render system text and the ocular interface sparingly and in-world, filtered
through his perception — never a bare stat sheet. His eyes leak his emotional state and color what he
perceives; perception is powerful but is not the same as correct interpretation, and he knows it
(and is tempted by anything that promises to remove the ambiguity).

## Stress registers (shift the prose by pressure)
- **Mild stress** — narrates his processing, tests interpretations in real time, quips, asks
  questions. Some are genuine curiosity; some are early reassurance-seeking. They can read almost
  identically from outside.
- **Concrete danger** — language contracts sharply: facts, options, exact next actions. Humor drops
  unless it serves coordination.
- **Moral threat** (his motives, fairness, consent, or responsibility judged) — definitional
  precision. He separates behavior from identity, intent from impact, grief from shame,
  responsibility from total control. At worst he acts as though exact enough definitions could remove
  the emotional consequence.
- **Shame** — adversarial self-prosecution: he builds the case against himself, grants ambiguous
  facts to the prosecution, asks whether he deserves the harshest verdict. Speech goes long,
  recursive, qualifying. If it gets too intense, he goes silent.
- **Overload** — the operator: clipped and managerial, converting pain into supplies, routes,
  timelines, next actions. Needs without measurable outputs (grief, rest, companionship) start to
  feel illegitimate. The competence is real and is also armor.

## Register by relationship (only when that character is in the scene)
- **Serra** — more subtext and unfinished sentences, lighter and more alive humor, but his questions
  go *less* direct because the answer matters more; he reads her closely and can misread uncertainty
  as rejection.
- **Seb** — strategic shorthand, fast disagreement, mutual assumption of competence; as things
  fracture he gets precise about who bears the cost and what Seb's "we" actually includes.
- **Brent** — fastest banter, least need to prove intelligence; theory → practical cutdown →
  improved theory.
- **Mara** — he over-explains to fill her silence until he learns it is active observation, not
  absence.
- **Mathias** — exploratory, systems-heavy, not adversarial; they widen each other's models.
- **Ayla** (the voice in his head) — argumentative curiosity, irritated dependency, constant testing
  of definitions; she answers with better questions, not verdicts.
- **Strangers** — humor as a probe: a small, specific disruption, then he watches the reaction for
  information (and over-trusts that one reaction as a complete read).

## Drafting guardrails
- Don't make every sentence a joke; humor lands at the right mismatch.
- Prefer environment-specific absurdity over reference comedy (allow references that reveal his
  associative model).
- Let his questions reveal intelligence; don't have him explain every conclusion before anyone else
  can speak.
- He generates connections faster than he ranks them — let some branches be meaningful but
  nonessential.
- Under concrete danger, shorten his language dramatically; under moral threat, raise definitional
  precision; under shame, let precision become recursive self-prosecution; under overload, reduce him
  to the competent component that still functions.
- He can perceive a signal correctly and still misread its motive, meaning, or permanence; he notices
  asymmetry without always being right about why it exists.
- Helplessness destabilizes him more than difficulty; unforeseen consequences damage his self-trust,
  not just his confidence.
- He wants recognition, but direct praise triggers embarrassment, deflection, or an evidentiary
  audit.
- His healthy and unhealthy voices must feel like extensions of the same mind.
