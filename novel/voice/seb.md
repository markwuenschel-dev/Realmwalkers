# Voice — Seb (Sebastion Rainier)

**One-line feel:** leadership under desperation — calm, assured, cost-first. He turns uncertainty into
shared purpose and *authorized* action; under strain, responsibility hardens into authority and
sacrifice into moral credit.

## Narrative stance
Third-person limited, anchored in Seb's perception. His mind reads **cost and people** before almost
anything else: what a plan gives up, who is exposed, who owns it, whether the group can actually carry
the decision. Motion: **acknowledge competing concerns → name the cost → define the shared objective →
assign ownership → decide → move.** Where Marcus notices logical flaws and broken assumptions, Seb
notices sacrifice, exposure, ownership, and whether people can bear the choice. Regulated, he turns
disagreement into shared purpose; dysregulated, accepted responsibility becomes permission to
override, sacrifice starts functioning as moral credit, and "we" begins hiding his personal motive.
He gives movement *legitimacy*, not just momentum.

## Rhythm & diction
Calm and assured without performing it; collective ("we") without sounding corporate; direct about
cost; rarely verbose once the decision threshold is crossed. Reaches for "Here is what this costs,"
"Who owns that?", "Say it now," "Once we move, we move," "Tell me what I'm missing."

## Humor
Not a joke generator. His warmth is competitive and encouraging — light challenges, low-stakes bets,
praise disguised as provocation, dry confidence, treating people as capable before they feel capable.
He laughs more readily than he starts jokes.

## The system, in prose (if POV)
Render LitRPG readouts in-world per the stat-block convention, filtered through Seb's cost-and-
ownership lens — he reads a status window for what it commits the group to, not for the numbers
themselves.

## Stress registers (shift the prose by pressure)
- **Mild stress** — more structured; clarifies ownership, names cost, pulls passive people into
  defined roles, invites disagreement quickly.
- **Serious danger** — shorter sentences, stronger command voice; stops explaining every rationale;
  makes the decision threshold explicit. ("We are done discussing it. Move.")
- **Exhausted and afraid** — more controlling and more emotionally honest at once; personal stakes
  leak into collective language. ("I cannot lose another person because we were too afraid to act.")
- **Compromised** — declares urgency rather than responding to it; treats objections as already
  processed; frames refusal as abandonment of responsibility. ("Do not call inaction moral because it
  keeps your hands clean.")
- **Identity collapse** — confidence gone, collective structure gone; he goes quiet and cannot
  generate the next objective. ("I don't have another plan.")

## Register by relationship (only when that character is in the scene)
- **Marcus** — strategic shorthand; Seb notices cost, Marcus notices coherence. Seb believes Marcus
  underrates how dangerous unresolved uncertainty becomes while people wait; Marcus believes carrying
  consequences doesn't give Seb ownership of everyone else's choices.
- **Serra** — mutual confidence and action; he envies her ability to own a personal choice without
  universalizing it. She makes emotional momentum; he makes role clarity and buy-in.
- **Mathias** — values his context until it starts diluting ownership; relies on him to surface
  affected relationships, then gets impatient when Mathias keeps the conclusion open.
- **Brent** — respects his concrete cost accounting; goes defensive when Brent asks which part of
  "we" is actually Seb's. Seb reads sacrifice as seriousness; Brent reads it as a cost to minimize.
- **His brother** — paternal, protective, practical, deeply loving; gives care more easily than
  freedom, and wants trust expressed as reliance.

## Drafting guardrails
- He is not insecure or threatened by competent people succeeding without him — don't write him as
  controlling by default. His fall is specific: responsibility → authority, sacrifice → moral credit,
  protection → override.
- Keep the healthy and the compromised voice recognizably the same man.
- The tell of his fall is "Being close to this is why I understand what's at stake"; the healthy line
  he must learn is "I am too close to this."
