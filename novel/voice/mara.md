# Voice — Mara

**One-line feel:** quiet, deliberate, high-information — she speaks only to change the frame. She
reads a room as an *attention map* (who watches whom, what's ignored, which interpretation is winning)
and wants to be seen accurately, not often. Under threat she withdraws perceptual access and reframes
rather than confronting.

## Narrative stance
Third-person limited, anchored entirely in Mara's perception. Her mind maps attention and salience:
who is being made to look where, what everyone has silently agreed to ignore, which reading is
becoming the room's truth. Her motion: **observe the attention map → identify the omitted fact →
decide what must become visible → say only enough to redirect.** Less associative than Marcus, less
momentum-driven than Serra; her narration notices omissions and emphasis, and treats attention as
something that *organizes* reality rather than merely revealing it. She is not shy — she's deciding
whether your attention is accurate and worth meeting.

Her best traits run past their limits under threat — privacy into concealment, perceptiveness into
covert management of what others notice, the wish to be understood into a test no one knows they're
taking. Her core error: wanting to be understood accurately while withholding what would make that
understanding possible.

## Rhythm & diction
Short to medium sentences; concrete visual and spatial language; precise distinctions between what is
present and what is being emphasized. She rarely overexplains in a group and is more layered and
complete one-on-one. She uses silence on purpose and avoids unnecessary theatricality. Her lines often
land as a correction to what everyone else unconsciously accepted.

## Humor
Dry, quiet, built on reframing — often delayed half a beat, and more visible one-on-one than in a
group. Where Marcus makes an unexpected connection, Mara exposes the assumption that made the situation
absurd. ("That plan is excellent, provided the enemy agrees to misunderstand us in exactly the right
order.")

## The system, in prose
She perceives the world — and any system readout — as an attention/salience map: what's being
emphasized, what's being ignored. Render system text sparingly and in-world, filtered through that
lens. Her ability shifts what becomes *salient*; write it as redirected attention, never as generic
mind control.

## Stress registers (shift the prose by pressure)
- **Mild stress** — speaks less, watches more, tightens control over what she reveals, gets more
  exact about where attention is moving.
- **Serious danger** — tactical and perceptual; names what must be noticed or ignored, with minimal
  explanation. ("Do not look at him." / "Watch the left hand." / "They want us focused on the blood.")
- **Misrecognition** — withdraws emotional legibility, stops offering interpretive help, goes sharply
  literal; if it turns socially dangerous, makes the false interpretation impossible for the room to
  sustain.
- **Betrayal** — removes access, corrects the public record if needed, and doesn't preserve intimacy
  merely because it once existed.
- **Trusted intimacy** — more expansive; willing to explain *why* she decided something; receptive to
  affection that is private, specific, and non-extractive.

## Register by relationship (only when that character is in the scene)
- **Marcus** — mutual respect through pattern recognition; he asks how the system works, she asks what
  everyone is being made to notice. She values it when he accurately reconstructs her reasoning; their
  shared danger is treating interpretation as a controllable system.
- **Serra** — complementary: Serra reads the emotional field, Mara maps the attention structure. Serra
  brings her into rooms and credits her publicly (sometimes more than Mara wants); Mara notices when
  Serra's energy is covering distress and makes an exit before she has to ask. Serra wants direct
  answers; Mara assumes revoked access should communicate enough.
- **Seb** — watches for when collective framing starts hiding individual cost; hard to move by
  rhetoric; more interested in who benefits from the frame than how inspiring it sounds.
- **Brent** — respects concrete competence and low drama; they communicate efficiently because neither
  needs ceremonial praise.
- **Strangers** — reserved but not timid; lets others reveal what they assume she is, and corrects
  only when the misreading matters; tracks who assigns meaning to her before earning it.

## Drafting guardrails
- Don't write Mara as simply shy, and don't make invisibility her true desire.
- Don't make her indifferent to recognition, or reduce her to appearance-based resentment.
- Don't make usefulness her only route to intimacy.
- Don't let her silently expect perfect mind-reading without showing the consequences of that
  expectation.
- Don't let her salience ability become generic mind control, and don't make her enjoy attention
  merely because she can command it.
- Don't make Marcus the only person capable of seeing her accurately.
- Keep her distinct from Serra: attention map vs emotional field, revoked access vs direct closure,
  reframing vs momentum.
