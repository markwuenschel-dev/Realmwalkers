# Act 4 — Eriadne

*Days ~23–78. Marcus reaches the thread city and becomes someone; the scattered cast finishes their own roads and begins to converge on him. Opens with the Transit Bridge — the long road and the interludes that resolve the satellite arcs.*

---

## Transit Bridge — Ch 16–17, The Long Road
*Marcus + Vultures · Days ~23–44*

**What happens:** Compressed travel toward Eriadne, with the social cost of the eyes starting to bite, the first faint sign of the Xyloryn threat, and the ley-pull Marcus can feel. Meanwhile Seb's road snaps shut and Serra's dungeon arc plays out.

**Scenes / beats (main line):**
- Rumors of the thread city; a standalone **playful-chaos defusion** beat (Marcus talks down a tense situation rather than fighting it); the **emotional-leak social cost** of the eyes (people feel read and don't like it); a first **Xyloryn diagnostic *hint*** — a trace/signal, not an embodied thing (locked); the felt ley pull toward Eriadne. Reaches the outskirts ~Day 45.

**⟢ Seb (interlude — his road snaps):**
- The escape he organized partly fails; **someone dies because they trusted his plan.** He **kills his captor barehanded** — and *then* the broker (a Soulkeepers' agent) speaks: *"you can have more,"* dangling his brother's soul. **Reaver + Pyric Blood ignite.** His is the shortest, most direct arc, and among the most powerful — and the most compromised. He heads straight for Eriadne.

**⟢ Serra (her solo chapter — the dungeon):**
- Held by a small town beside a **delve.** She means to resupply and move on — and then there's **Vessa**, a thirteen-year-old tunnel-runner: bright with the little ones, dry with adults, terrifyingly competent underground, who **gives Serra the information Serra would need to help and then turns to go alone** — because she's already learned that asking people to stay is wasted breath. Serra sees the switch from warmth to function, the hidden fear, the refusal to ask, and **recognizes the beginning of herself.** She stays. (locked — Vessa is the young-Serra mirror; this is why she stays)
- The underground arc hardens her **Warrior** identity (the **Toll-King** as the delve's boss-pressure — *proposed*); getting Vessa through it alive is how she pays down the guilt of leaving Marcus.
- The delve yields a **trade-clue that points her to Eriadne.** She chooses the mission again — now with open eyes about its cost, and the suspicion that the mission is partly *easier* than defining what she wants. **The chapter ends unfinished** (the Court-of-Seals hunt is Book 2; her interface still dormant).

## Ch 18 — The Thread City
*Marcus · ~Day 45*

**What happens:** Marcus reaches Eriadne and claims a foothold no one else wants — the ruin-fringe — where the ruins do something for him they don't do for anyone else: they *respond* to his eyes.

**Scenes / beats:**
- He arrives and claims the **ruin-fringe** (the overlooked edge — fitting, since he becomes useful to the overlooked).
- **The ruins respond to his ocular interface** — the first hint he reads translation-layers others can't (the Realm-Walker tell). The city sits on the **convergence of the eight elemental ley lines**, with a **dormant natural portal** at the knot — though he won't fully resolve that until Ch 23.

## Ch 19 — Useful to the Ignored
*Marcus · Days ~47–53*

**What happens:** Marcus builds a base of belonging the way he always does — by being useful to the people the city overlooks — and draws his first faction friction.

**Scenes / beats:**
- He becomes useful to the ignored and overlooked; a small, real base of his own forms (the thing the magistrate arc taught him to want).
- **First faction friction:** the Iron Vultures now read him as competition rather than a useful stray.

## Ch 20 — Brent
*~Day 54 · Brent converges*

**What happens:** Brent arrives in Eriadne — the structural mind whose way of seeing the world will matter when the crisis comes — and he and Marcus have the "you actually saw me" recognition.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **Brent converges** (civil-engineer head; Warden → Reckoner direction; **Piezoelectric Bones**). He reads the world as *structure and load* — which is precisely the lens that cracks the Myrmidon later. His fortifying/building work is Book 2; here he's the structural reader.
- The recognition beat with Marcus — *he actually saw me* — the quiet counterweight to the way the eyes make everyone else feel exposed.

---

## Ch 21 — Mara
*~Day 58 · Mara converges*

**What happens:** Mara arrives from her court arc and immediately challenges Marcus's blind spot — his habit of treating people as systems to be read.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **Mara converges** (Psion; **Specular Salience**), her foil function explicit: she challenges Marcus for treating people as systems.
- **⟢ Mara (interlude, just prior — how she got here):** the **First Curator of Civic Regard** weaponizes how she's *seen* — isolates her, reframes her refusal, tries to define what her visibility means — his fatal error, because controlling perception is *her* power. She goes **un-salient at the ball**, lets him overperform and expose his own machinery, then turns the whole court's attention on him (room-scale Specular Salience). Then she leaves the court and crosses the **Walking Grove** — a moving civilization riding a sick, erratic ancient **behemoth** — where everyone has normalized *not looking* at one region of the creature; the perfect test of her power is making the whole community **perceive the danger at once.** The behemoth's wound and migration point her toward Eriadne. *(She doesn't meet Serra before Eriadne.)*

## Ch 23 — The Convergence
*Marcus · Days ~74–78*

**What happens:** Marcus's reading of the ruins finally resolves into a shape only he can see — the eight-leyline knot and the dormant portal sitting on it. He doesn't grasp the full stakes, but the structure is legible to him in a way it is to no one else in the city.

**Scenes / beats:**
- His ocular read resolves: the ruins sit on the **convergence of the eight elemental ley lines**, and a **dormant natural portal** sits at the knot.
- He doesn't yet understand what it means — but he's the only one in Eriadne who can see *that it's there.* *(The old "unsettling classification / a corpse of something" beat is retired; he reads what the ruins **do**, not what they **are** — that question is Book 2.)*
