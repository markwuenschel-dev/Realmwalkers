# Act 1 — Earth

*June 2035. The world before the Realm. Marcus POV unless noted.*

The relationship that powers the whole book starts here, but most of it runs **under** the surface — the reader should feel weight without yet knowing its cause. The full origin (the celebration, the kiss, the Discord misread) is held back and delivered later as Serra's flashback in Ch 6A; Act 1 cold-opens on Marcus's Astria work and then the scrim, letting the romantic charge show only as pressure, rhythm, and disproportionate attention.

Act 1's structural job is not only to get the cast into the Realm. It should establish the book's first version of the **frame-vs-reality** problem: systems that produce clean outputs while hiding who or what is steering the outcome. Marcus begins the book seeing a model that may be accurate but cannot justify itself, and he lets the unresolved wrongness remain in motion because he cannot yet prove the deeper violation. The Book 1 ending mirrors this beat in emotional form: the Eyes/Meszkhal frame offers a clean read of Serra, and Marcus must finally understand that **accuracy is not authority, prediction is not understanding, and seeing does not grant ownership over what something means.**

# Ch 1 — "Aim Not Found"
*Marcus · Earth Day 0*

**What happens:** Marcus Vye — ML engineer at Astria by day, the anonymous shape-shifting Aspect of the #25 guild *404: Aim Not Found* by night — is up late on a model anomaly he can't explain: a sealed control cohort in an aptitude / performance / retention system is converging on outcomes it was never trained or permitted to influence. The results have the shape of intervention, but none of the authorized intervention channels explain it. It is as if something outside the system is arranging the outcome while the model takes credit.

The model uses neutral language — readiness, risk, recommendation, non-interventional cohort — but the people behind those labels are being sorted by a force the interface cannot show. Marcus distrusts a confident model that can't show its work, but the run is useful, the evidence is incomplete, and the scrim is starting. He logs the anomaly, marks it for escalation, and leaves it unresolved.

That night, 404 fights **Dead Hand (#3)** in the scrim. Seb arrives late because his brother has died before the match, and the team enters the session with its center already cracked. The match becomes a duel between Marcus and Dead Hand's assassin — Serra — that neither of them walks away from cleanly. Then Astria seizes the session, and the night stops being a game.

**Scenes / beats:**

- **The anomaly (cold open).** Marcus at work, late, looking at results that arrive *too clean*. The control cohort should be sealed from intervention, yet its outcomes keep converging toward the same readiness profile as the active candidate population. Marcus checks for leakage, label contamination, bad joins, hidden intervention paths, logging errors, cohort drift, and ordinary statistical noise. None of the normal explanations hold. The hook quietly rhymes with what the implant turns out to be: a receiver for something not-here.

- **Marcus's first choice-point.** He does not dismiss the anomaly, but he also does not stop the machine. He writes it up, preserves the evidence, and tells himself he needs more proof before accusing the system of steering people. This is the opening mirror to the ending: early Marcus sees a suspiciously useful frame and lets it stand; finale Marcus sees a painfully compelling frame and refuses to let it decide what Serra's choice means.

- **The hinge from work to game.** The scrim pulls Marcus away before the anomaly is resolved. He tells himself it can wait until morning — because 404 needs him, Worlds prep matters, and one more impossible system failure can wait a few hours. This makes Astria's later intrusion feel like the thing he postponed following him into the one place he thought was separate.

- **Seb arrives late.** Seb is not joking-late or logistics-late. He is wrong-late. The guild knows enough about him to know this does not happen. He gives the thinnest explanation first — hospital call, family thing, sorry, ready now — and tries to move straight into command cadence. The truth lands before the match starts: his brother died before the scrim. Seb does not ask for comfort. He asks for status reports. This is the first fracture in the old guild: everyone can see the human cost, but the structure of the team still routes them back through Seb because no one knows how to make the leader stop leading.

- **The 404 baseline.** Before the rupture, let the guild feel alive, but with the center already damaged. Seb is usually the warm, competent center who turns disagreement into plan; tonight he still does the work, but the warmth arrives late, like something manually switched on. Brent cuts through abstraction and names practical risk. Mathias watches the roster, terrain, missing pieces, and the human gap everyone else is trying not to press on. Kip wants forward pressure and may be the one most tempted to treat motion as solution. Marcus is the adaptive secret weapon, the part of the strategy they could not show other teams. The reader needs to feel the old guild before Act 1 breaks it.

- **The scrim begins.** 404 vs Dead Hand. Seb runs the kill route: hunt the players, not the boss. Brent on support, Mathias scouting, Kip frontline. Seb continues because command is the only shape he can still occupy, and the rest of the guild accepts that too quickly because they also need him to be Seb. The Dead Hand leader patches in because their star rogue is late, and Seb chooses to wait. Fairness and competitive integrity are easier to hold than grief. Mathias still flags the deeper roster problem: Dead Hand fields a second assassin no one can find. *(That's Mara, salience-suppressed, never surfaced — introduction by absence. Locked.)*

- **Serra enters.** Dead Hand's missing star rogue logs in late, and the match finally starts with the threat 404 actually came to test themselves against. Her lateness is a separate tactical/professional beat, not connected to Seb's grief.

- **The duel.** Marcus and Serra find each other. They're equals — the duel reveals that, it doesn't earn it. He is never her superior, never her underdog who levels up. He reads her not by face but by how she decides — target selection, attacking through interference, turning a failed entry into a second attack, refusing to reset when disruption can become leverage — and rates her going in; she's a benchmark, he's a body on a #25 guild she doesn't rate back. Underneath, they reproduce the celebration's escalating attention without recognizing each other at first — a resonance, not yet a recognition.

- **Recognition in the duel.** The duel forces personal recognition mid-fight. Serra severely wounds Brent; Marcus leaves Brent to extract himself and switches aggressively into Predator to exploit Serra's overcommitment. He narrowly misses the decisive hit, but tears away her hood / veil in the exchange and sees her face — red hair, eyes, Serra. Marcus recognizes her first and hesitates for a fraction. Serra instantly punishes the opening and lands a hard retaliatory strike that cracks the face / neck seam of Marcus's Aspect shell. For a heartbeat, the damaged combat shell exposes enough of the real biometric avatar beneath — shaved head, eyes, jawline — for certainty. Serra recognizes Marcus second, within the same combat beat, only a fraction after he recognizes her.

- **One exchange too long.** After recognition, neither breaks contact. If anything, the duel sharpens. Both stay a beat past necessity; a gap opens; both teams pay. Seb calls him off — "Marcus, break contact." "...return to formation." "Marcus." — and Marcus hears him and stays one exchange longer. The plant for the whole relationship: their attention can become exclusionary, and other people absorb the cost.

- **Scene break: the intrusion.** The duel scene ends when the screens snow over. Marcus has not won. Serra has not withdrawn. Both have one more move forming, and Astria steals the answer. The interruption should land as a violation of the duel's unresolved charge before the plot turns into logistics. A suited Astria figure appears, but Roth remains unnamed on the page here.

- **The coercion scene.** The suited Astria figure takes the match in place of the Dead Hand leader and runs **consent-capture as cold procedure, not a pitch** — *"participation remains voluntary."* An Astria-manufactured tornado "boss" voids the scrim's logic; logout is dead. The legal language should echo the ML anomaly's sanitized labels: the system keeps insisting no one is being forced while every available path has already been arranged.

- **Seb's grief, not rescue.** Seb's brother is already dead before Astria appears, so the medical-cost rescue lever is gone before formal consent. That makes the offer worse, not cleaner. Astria does not tempt Seb with saving his brother; it reaches him after the thing he wanted most is already lost. His consent is **grief, not rescue** — not *maybe this saves him*, but *something has to happen next, and I no longer know how to stand still.*

- **Brent names the gun.** Brent cuts through the procedure once the coercion becomes concrete: *"It's a gun."* The line should feel like the plain-English translation of every sanitized label in the chapter.

- **Consent under duress.** Marcus, Seb, Brent, and Mathias verbally consent. Do not let this read like heroic acceptance of an offer. It is a procedural trap, emotionally different for each of them: Marcus sees the system violation; Seb is inside fresh grief and looking for a next action; Brent sees the gun; Mathias sees the doors closing. End on the brink — the full weight of what just happened not yet landed.

## Discord Bridge
*Marcus / Serra · short, between Ch 1 and Ch 2*

**What happens:** The locked attention from the scrim spills off the game into a brief Discord exchange — two people who clearly have history, each misreading it. Compact and oblique; the reader feels the charge without being shown its origin yet.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **A short thread.** **She believes she reopened a door; he believes she contained the kiss.** Neither says the real thing. The exchange has to work on first read as charged post-duel banter and on second read as two people talking around the altana.
  - Serra: *"I checked. You really did qualify twenty-fifth."*
  - Marcus: *"We prefer 'the final team deemed worthy.'"*
  - Serra: *"You qualified by four points."*
  - Marcus: *"Four points is a dominant margin if you stop comparing it to larger numbers."*
  - Serra: *"You were more convincing last night."*
  - Marcus: *"Different venue."*
  - Serra: *"Better venue?"*
  - Marcus: *"The view was better."*
  - Serra: *"It was."*
- **Marcus chooses not to dig.** He notices he never asked her guild and decides not to investigate through Discord or public rosters. After kissing her, it would feel invasive. *(The reader still doesn't know about the kiss; that lands in Ch 6A.)*

## Ch 2 — The Facility
*Marcus · Earth Day 1*

**What happens:** Astria collects them and flies them to a California R&D facility. The team reunites in person; Serra arrives and introduces herself — and here, *after* the scrim, the identities finally land, in two staggered steps. This is the emotional climax of the whole Earth sequence, and the larger half is Serra's.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **Pickup and arrival.** Handlers (Eldrin, Selia); the flight; the facility; meeting Withers and Vail; the patio reunion with Seb, Brent, and Mathias. Keep the tone wrong in a controlled way: too polite, too prepared, too normal around a choice that was not normal.
- **The team aftershock.** Before Serra arrives, show the old 404 dynamic under strain. Seb is functioning, but grief has changed the air around him. Brent is more blunt than usual. Mathias is watching everyone watch each other. Marcus is still carrying the ML anomaly and the scrim violation as the same shape wearing different clothes.
- **Serra arrives.** She introduces herself from Dead Hand; a wink — the first in-person Marcus/Serra beat, read as friendly, no visible romance. **Mara arrives late.** *(On a second read this surface is the cover over the reveal below.)*
- **Recognition, step one — Marcus.** The altana woman, the studied pro, and the anonymous opponent collapse into one person: **Serra was the assassin.** A new dimension on someone already compelling — strong, but he stays functional. He has learned something real; he does not yet understand what it means.
- **Recognition, step two — Serra (the larger detonation).** **Marcus was the anonymous Aspect.** It injures a professional read she trusts: *not incompetent — she'd mistaken the distance between competent and dangerous* — and it means she **chose him twice without knowing.** Stay with her here; this is the scene that lingers. Her reaction stays in character — an answer half a beat late, sustained attention, a question she wouldn't normally ask, seeking him out privately — not melodrama.
- **Serra's private aftershock.** Give her one concrete excuse to seek him out: a question about the exact moment he knew she was Dead Hand's assassin, a pointed comment about his Aspect transition, or a quiet *"How long did you know?"* that means more than its literal content. She should not confess the altana outright or ask for emotional definition. She is trying to recalibrate a person she selected socially, competitively, and personally without realizing those selections were all the same person.
- **The terms.** Conference room: LeBlanc, Roth, Withers lay out the deal and the risks; unanimous formal consent; medical exams. Keep the procedural language clean enough to be chilling. The same institutional sin keeps repeating: the system offers labels, forms, and choices after the meaningful steering has already happened.

## Ch 3 — Reins
*Marcus, then LeBlanc · Earth Day 1, night*

**What happens:** A last quiet beat between Marcus and Serra before surgery that resolves nothing and means everything; the implant surgery; and the first on-page proof that something beyond Astria has been steering all of this.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **The pre-surgery beat.** One narrow personal moment — it acknowledges the growing history, **resolves nothing about the kiss, declares no relationship**, and ends on Serra's **"See you after, Marcus."** Weight the reader can't fully cash yet. This should feel almost harmless on first read and devastating after Marcus dies immediately afterward.
- **The last Earth image.** Before the void, give Earth one final sensory anchor: fluorescent surgical light, the cold bite of the implant chair, Serra's voice lingering longer than the sedative should allow, Marcus thinking there will be an after. The transition needs a wound, not just a scene change.
- **Surgery.** All six receive the implant.
- **LeBlanc & Roth, in private (LeBlanc POV).** The hidden broker's voice through Roth: *"We will take the reins in the Dominion Realm."* The first sign that an agent of the **Soulkeepers' Exchange** shaped Astria from outside. This pays off the cold open: Astria was not the top of the chain, and the model's impossible steering was the first visible seam.
- **The void.** The six wake in a limbo; the voice explains the Realm and gives the **Eriadne directive**; the mists pull them apart. The team is scattered before Book 1 even begins.

## Act 1 Guardrails

- Do not surface the full celebration / altana / kiss context until Serra's later flashback. Act 1 may imply pressure and missing context; it should not explain it.
- Do not let the duel become personal identity recognition. In the scrim, Marcus recognizes Serra competitively through decision architecture; the personal recognition happens later at the facility.
- Do not write Marcus as superior or as an underdog leveling up to Serra. They are equals; awareness is asymmetric.
- Do not let Roth's offer feel like a pitch. It is consent-capture: legal procedure wrapped around a coercive environment.
- Do not reveal Mara on-page in Ch 1. Her introduction is absence: the second assassin no one can find.
- Do not let the ML anomaly become a lore dump. It should work as a human-stakes systems problem first and a cosmic steering clue only in retrospect.
- Do not let "accurate model" mean "true understanding." The cold open and Book 1 ending must rhyme around the same correction: clean output is not moral authority.
