# Act 3 — The False Rescue

*Days ~3–21. Marcus + Serra, with the first interludes for Seb, Mara, and Mathias. This is the road act: the reunion, the caravan, the false-authority crew, the warmth trap, the namebinding, the second kiss, and the split that ends the first half of the book.*

**The region** (one shared place, not separate zones): rolling pale **limestone uplands** with a **northern forest ridge** — light forest north, open dry hills and washes south, an old road through the middle running toward the caravan country, then the magistrate/namebinding jurisdiction, then eventually Eriadne. Dangerous enough to die if you're stupid; not a monster apocalypse. The opening should feel like vast wilderness plus desperate coincidence: *they could have missed each other forever.*

**The power state under this whole act:** Marcus's eyes are perception only — Stage 1 climbing to Stage 2 — never a weapon, and he's recently dead with no buffer; his wins come from *reading*, never force. Serra is lethal-competent but un-powered; her interface stays dormant. Every "how does this resolve" answer has to obey that.

## Ch 7 — Alone
*Marcus · Days 3–7*

**What happens:** Marcus, freshly resurrected and weak, solves the basic problems of staying alive, and the eyes wake on their own when he needs them — at a cost. One chapter alone, no more, then he finds the road.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **Waking diminished.** Pale, weak, recently dead in a way his body hasn't processed. He moves toward the forest line — shelter, water, shade, cover.
- **First water, first cost.** A survival problem solved by *observation*; the **Neurochromatic Eyes wake involuntarily** and drain him (reserve, stability). The thesis of his whole arc in one scene: he survives by reading, and reading costs.
- **Ayla, with limits.** She gives questions, not answers; she misdirects around her own damage. Not a UI.
- **Finds the road.** Ends oriented, not safe.
- *(Still to decide: his landing terrain/landmark — distinct from Serra's.)*

## Ch 8 — The Road
*Marcus · Day ~7 · the reunion*

**What happens:** Marcus nearly walks straight into Serra. Two people at zero in an alien world, the Earth Thing instantly present and completely unspoken. They don't process it — they fall into rhythm.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **The near-miss.** Serra is at the forest edge, on elevation, **watching a caravan below** (deciding if it's safe, useful, or dangerous) — so her attention is on the wagons. **Marcus sees her first**, coming up from the open south. The caravan is *in view but not yet in trouble*, which gives the reunion room to breathe — no combat, no crowd.
- **Both at zero.** She has the spear; he has nothing; nothing from the old game carried over. The recognition is bodily, wary, loaded.
- **The Earth Thing, unaddressed.** It's right there and neither names it. They default to tactical, survival, practical: *how long have you been here? what happened to your eyes? your weapon? do you trust them?* No confession — relief hidden in banter. (locked — they fall into rhythm before defining anything)
- They fall into step toward the caravan.

## Ch 9 — The Caravan
*Marcus · Day ~8*

**What happens:** They meet the caravan and its people, and a road crew dressed as legal authority stops them — a threat that can't be beaten by force, only read. Marcus's first live use of the eyes is a social puncture, not a power; the crew leaves having quietly gotten exactly what it came for.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **The caravan and its people.** A small practical convoy — three wagons, a dozen people, a couple of nervous guards. **Dara** runs it: practical, system-oriented, not warm, resents people who "help" by breaking her system; Marcus has to earn her. **Cael** (her partner or brother — *to decide*) is the quiet map-reader who **clocks Marcus's eyes early and says nothing.** **Vaeli**, an elven child, ruin-field injured and slow to heal, with a latent sensitivity — she *feels* buried structures and ley currents (a background fact, never announced). Mundane cargo on the surface; ledgers, debt papers, and sealed correspondence underneath — the caravan as the bloodstream of ordinary civilization.
- **The road-authority crew.** Not bandits — **bandits wearing bureaucracy.** Charter inspectors / levy wardens / sanctioned collectors at a narrow limestone road-cut: a chain across the road, hidden-archer elevation, an official-looking checkpoint table, a copied seal, mismatched sashes. Their real job isn't robbery — it's to **mark and classify Vaeli, confirm vulnerability, soften the group, and manufacture the pretext** that makes a later "rescue" look legitimate. (locked — first visible piece of the False Rescue machine)
- **First live use of the eyes.** Force can't win (numbers, chokepoint, archers, threatened animals, fake authority). The eyes don't show *truth* — they show **performance fracture:** the leader's authority is rehearsed, the men check him before acting, the seal is procedurally wrong, the checkpoint is built to *document*, not collect. **Marcus punctures the performance** (*"you rehearsed the authority, not the job"*); **Serra repositions** off his read — the read/move rhythm that pays off at the finale, in embryo.
- **"Got what it needed."** The crew retreats — and Marcus thinks the group won. They didn't: the crew **marked Vaeli** (a red road-seal stamped on her bandage/travel-paper under an "injury declaration"). They prevented the obvious harm and missed the hidden purpose.

## Ch 10 — The Iron Vultures
*Marcus · Day ~9*

**What happens:** The Iron Vultures — who were watching the same crew — surface and offer the group utility, not friendship. They're competent, gray, unsentimental survivors, and they're the connection to the next stretch of road.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **Watching the same crew.** The Vultures arrive as observers with their own angle — not rescuers, not paladins.
- **Utility, not friendship.** The offer is transactional; the rules of this world start leaking out through them rather than through exposition. *"We're useful, you're useful — let's not pretend this is kindness yet."*
- They travel on together.

## Ch 11 — Weather
*Marcus + Serra (with interludes) · Days ~9–13*

**What happens:** A travel-and-skill stretch. Serra's Warrior fundamentals start surfacing; Marcus's eyes climb to Stage 2; and the Vultures explain the False Rescue system the way you'd describe weather — which is exactly why it lands so hard. Meanwhile, three of the others begin their own roads.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **Training.** Serra's combat fundamentals surface — her body ahead of her training, the first whisper of *pressure/commitment* (the shape her interface will someday take). Marcus can't match her physically; his growth is the eyes.
- **Eyes → Stage 2.** Perception deepens; the cost moves with it.
- **"It's just weather."** A Vulture explains the False Rescue system **matter-of-factly, not as confession** — manufactured emergencies, monetized rescue. **Serra goes still** — the shape lands for her before it does for Marcus — and the space between the two of them quietly forms.
- **⟢ Serra (her POV):** what she notices about Marcus's post-resurrection instability — his analysis is the thing holding him together — and why his presence is both grounding *and* dangerous.
- **⟢ Seb (interlude):** lands desolate, then enslaved in a **salt-flat forced-labor camp** — white glare, brine pits, water rationed like currency, control by contract and collar. His brother is already dead, so he's in the aftermath of the one thing he couldn't lead or pay his way out of. *(No power yet.)* He survives by **organizing** people — ration flow, guard patterns, rest, hope.
- **⟢ Mara (interlude):** lands in **Veshara**, a status-by-display city-state where *regard* is the currency, and is taken in by a decent patron for her beauty. The trap is forming: a powerful man will pursue her, and her refusal will be the ignition.

## Ch 12 — Maera Vell
*Marcus · Days ~14–15 · the warmth trap*

**What happens:** The first real human warmth since Marcus died — a roadhouse, soup, rest — and the warmth is completely genuine, which is exactly why the betrayal works. Maera reports him off-screen, because the guild pays and silence has consequences.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **The Copper Kettle.** Stew always on the hearth, drying racks, patchwork quilts, a lost-things shelf, children's height-marks in the doorframe. Maera gives Marcus seconds before he asks; gives Serra privacy instead of fake mothering; waters the animals before arguing price. The warmth is **real** — that's the whole point. (locked — warmth ≠ loyalty)
- **Marcus over-shares.** Not stupidity — **starvation.** Exhausted, recently dead, desperate for warmth, he gives too much. The eyes read Maera correctly; his *model* of what the read means is wrong.
- **She reports him (off-screen).** Through her road-book — guest ledger, safety registry, informant log — she passes word to the magistrate-guild: pale strange-eyed man, red-haired woman, near the Vultures, caravan connection, the injured child, direction of travel. The reader doesn't see it happen. (locked — do not telegraph)
- *(Still to decide: is Serra in the room during the over-share, or apart? The "Maera gives Serra privacy" detail leans present-but-apart.)*

## Ch 13 — The Ledger
*Marcus + Serra · Days ~15–16 · namebinding on-page*

**What happens:** The machinery the road crew was feeding finally shows its face — bureaucratic, quiet, and worse for it. Vaeli is namebound and taken under the language of "debt resolution." Serra reads the whole system before Marcus does; his ache to build something un-seizable surfaces from the wreckage of the warmth he just trusted.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **The magistrate-guild.** Bureaucratic horror — clerks, documents, muscle. Namebinding presented as paperwork, not villainy.
- **Vaeli is taken.** A quiet ritual dressed as "debt resolution," never "slavery." She registers as a **Strong** soul — a notable catch, which underlines that this is monetized, not routine. (locked — Strong soul)
- **Serra reads the system.** She connects manufactured emergency → monetized rescue (the Ch 9 pattern completes for her) and **goes still** — she grasps the system-level meaning, imposed identity and ownership, *before Marcus does.* This is the seed of her registry mission. Marcus realizes Maera's report is why they were found (Ch 12 pays off).
- **The home-building ache.** From the wreckage of trusting that warmth, Marcus wants to build something that can't be reported or seized — personal before political.
- **⟢ Mathias (interlude):** solo near the **Eriadne ruin-fringe** — geographically *ahead* of Marcus and Serra. He **detects portal pressure / a trace from the other side** — residue, an old biological signal, something *not-here* the ruins are connected to. **No live drone, no fight — just contact**, and the realization the place is connected to something not here yet. He starts loading **Scout** through research, and falls in with a local mapper-scavenger band (the **Vergehands**). (locked — no embodied Xyloryn before the finale)

## Ch 14 — The Pursuit & The Pitch
*Marcus + Serra · Days ~16–19 · the second kiss*

**What happens:** Everyone moves the same direction. Marcus wants to *save the child*; Serra wants to *stop the wrong* — same target, different engine. The Vultures won't fight a sanctioned op for morality, so the case made to them is practical. And at the end, with the operation set for dawn, the second kiss — this time Serra initiates.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **Same direction, different drive.** Marcus: save Vaeli. Serra: stop the machine. They need the Vultures, who won't risk people for a moral cause — so the pitch is **practical** (the magistrate's seizable haul; the office isn't backed by any larger power). The Vultures join **on conditions** (the op funds itself from seized assets; capped risk with a standing right to pull out; one job, not an oath; their name stays off it).
- **The catch.** After the child is taken, Serra moves first; Marcus chases and **reaches** her. Pursuit *succeeds* this time (the deliberate rhyme: here he *can* and *does* follow; at the end of the book he *won't*).
- **The second kiss — the height ritual.** Plan settled, op set for dawn, a quiet interval (not adrenaline — no enemies near; they have time to leave and don't), on an elevated open-air vantage over the compound. **Serra initiates** — she does the thing she's constitutionally unable to do, *claim an uncertain thing*, and answers the question the Earth kiss left her holding (*did he think it was a mistake?*). It's **present-tense** (*I choose this now*, not *I promise the after*): mutual desire confirmed, **no relationship named**, and the obligation question that fuels the ending gets *louder*, not resolved. **Marcus's restraint:** he withholds *what does this mean* (forcing a definition would make it a negotiation). **Serra's rule, enacted:** she takes the now and does **not** ask for the after — *"I don't ask people to stay,"* performed, never said. **Aftermath:** touch eases, she seeks him more openly, the companions notice a shift and get no confirmation — and **both privately assume an "after"** that the next chapter destroys. (locked — Axis E reconcile; no sex scene, no label, no promise)

## Ch 15 — The Operation & The Split
*Marcus + Serra · Days ~19–21 · the end of the first half*

**What happens:** The rescue half-works — Marcus saves Vaeli but can't save everyone, and it marks him. Serra kills the magistrate, discovers he was only a node in a much larger system, and leaves — not because Marcus failed her, but because she's found something she can't ignore. The Earth Thing is at its heaviest, and nobody speaks it.

**Scenes / beats:**
- **The operation.** Chaotic intervention. **Marcus extracts Vaeli; others don't make it.** He doesn't get to save everyone, and it marks him.
- **⟢ Serra kills the magistrate** (her POV; off-page from Marcus's). A deliberate killing. She finds a **registry-fragment** and learns the truth: he's a **node, not the source** — names are sub-leased from a master registry up the chain. She pockets a cold lead. **Her interface does *not* unlock here** — it stays dormant until the finale. (locked)
- **The separation.** No speech. The Earth Thing at its heaviest yet. She leaves on her own road — **her Book-1 solo arc is the dungeon** — following the cold lead toward the Court of Seals (a limb of the Aurelian Sovereignty, a name she doesn't have yet). **That hunt is Book 2.** Marcus can't follow. The departure goes unnamed — the thing the ending will finally break.
- **Aftermath staging note:** the split sets up the long separation; the next time they're in the same room is the Ch 25 reunion, and both are changed.
- **⟢ Brent (interlude):** alone for a long stretch — **dropped in the open ocean**, breathe-or-die, no mythology, just practical judgment (control breathing, read the current, lash debris, let the water carry him). He reaches a **coastal salvage community** — not pirates, not noble rescuers, people who recover what the sea gives back: *"you're alive — can you work?"* He earns a place by fixing what will break, and his Warden/Reckoner path begins through use. *(His real power surfaces later; here he's useful before he's powerful.)*
